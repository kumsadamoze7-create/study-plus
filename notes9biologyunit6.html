🌍 Unit 6: Ecology

Ecology is an important branch of Biology that studies the relationships between organisms and their environment.

Every organism lives in an environment and interacts with both living and non-living components of that environment.

Ecology:

Ecology is the branch of Biology concerned with the study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their environment.

🌱 6.1.1 Definitions of Common Ecological Terms

Ecology

Ecology studies how organisms interact with one another and how they interact with their physical environment.

Ecologist

An ecologist is a scientist who studies ecological relationships and interactions between organisms and their environment.

Environment

The environment includes all the living and non-living factors surrounding an organism and affecting its life.

Habitat

A habitat is the place where an organism normally lives.

Examples:
  • A pond can be the habitat of fish.
  • A forest can be the habitat of birds.
  • Soil can be the habitat of earthworms.

Niche

A niche describes the role or job of an organism in its environment and how it uses available resources.

Producer

Producers are organisms that make their own food, usually through photosynthesis.

Green plants and algae are important producers.

Consumer

Consumers cannot make their own food and obtain food by eating producers or other consumers.

Decomposer

Decomposers break down dead organisms and organic remains and release substances that can be reused in the ecosystem.

Bacteria and fungi are important decomposers.

☀️ 6.1.2 Biotic and Abiotic Components

Abiotic Components

Abiotic factors are the non-living components of an ecosystem that affect organisms.

Examples of Abiotic Factors

  • Light
  • Temperature
  • Water
  • Air
  • Soil
  • Mineral nutrients
  • Salinity
  • pH
  • Humidity
  • Wind

Light

Light is an important source of energy for most ecosystems. Green plants use light energy during photosynthesis.

Temperature

Temperature affects the metabolic activities, growth, reproduction and distribution of organisms.

Water

All living organisms require water. The availability of water can affect where organisms are able to survive.

Biotic Components

Biotic components are the living parts of an ecosystem.

Examples

  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Fungi
  • Bacteria
  • Other microorganisms

🌎 6.1.3 Ecological Levels

Ecologists organize the living world into different levels to make ecological interactions easier to study.

1. Organism

An organism is an individual living thing.

Example: one lion, one tree or one fish.

2. Population

A population is a group of organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time and capable of interbreeding.

Example: a population of Ethiopian wolves living in the same area.

3. Community

A community consists of populations of different species living and interacting in the same area.

Example: plants, birds, insects and mammals living together in a forest.

4. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of a biological community together with the non-living physical environment with which it interacts.

5. Biosphere

The biosphere is the global zone of life on Earth. It includes all ecosystems.

Level Meaning
Organism One individual living thing
Population Members of the same species in an area
Community Different populations living together
Ecosystem Community plus its physical environment
Biosphere All regions of Earth where life exists

🌿 6.1.4 Ecosystems

An ecosystem contains living organisms and their physical environment interacting together.

Components of an Ecosystem

  • Biotic components
  • Abiotic components

Producers

Producers capture energy and manufacture organic food. Plants and algae are common producers.

Consumers

Consumers obtain energy by eating other organisms.

Primary Consumers

Primary consumers feed directly on producers.

Example: grasshopper feeding on grass.

Secondary Consumers

Secondary consumers feed on primary consumers.

Tertiary Consumers

Tertiary consumers may feed on secondary consumers and can occupy higher trophic levels.

Decomposers

Decomposers break down dead organic material and help recycle nutrients within ecosystems.

🌍 6.1.5 Biomes

A biome is a large ecological region characterized by particular climate conditions and characteristic vegetation and animals.

Major Terrestrial Biomes

🌴 Tropical Rainforest

  • High temperature
  • High rainfall
  • Dense vegetation
  • Very high biodiversity

🌾 Grassland

  • Dominated mainly by grasses
  • Rainfall is generally lower than tropical rainforest
  • Supports many grazing animals

🏜️ Desert

  • Very low rainfall
  • Large temperature differences may occur
  • Plants and animals have special adaptations to limited water

🌲 Temperate Forest

  • Moderate climate
  • Seasonal changes are common
  • Trees and other plants are adapted to seasonal conditions

❄️ Tundra

  • Very cold conditions
  • Short growing season
  • Vegetation is generally low-growing

Aquatic Biomes

🌊 Marine Biome

Marine ecosystems include salt-water environments such as oceans and seas.

💧 Freshwater Biome

Freshwater ecosystems include rivers, lakes, ponds and streams.

Biome Main Characteristics

🌱 6.1.6 Ecological Succession

Ecological succession is the gradual change in the species composition and structure of a community over time.

Primary Succession

Primary succession begins in an area where there was previously no established soil or biological community, such as newly exposed rock.

Secondary Succession

Secondary succession occurs in an area where a community has been disturbed but soil remains.

Examples of disturbance:
  • Fire
  • Flood
  • Deforestation
  • Agricultural disturbance

Pioneer Species

Pioneer species are among the first organisms to colonize an area during ecological succession.

Climax Community

A climax community refers to a relatively stable mature community that develops after succession under particular environmental conditions.

Primary Succession Secondary Succession
Starts where soil is absent or newly forming Starts where soil remains
Usually slower Usually faster
Example: newly exposed rock Example: area recovering after fire

🤝 6.2 Ecological Relationships

Organisms in an ecosystem interact with one another in many ways. These relationships affect survival, population size and distribution.

Competition

Competition occurs when organisms require the same limited resource.

Resources may include food, water, space, light or mates.

Intraspecific Competition

Competition between members of the same species.

Interspecific Competition

Competition between members of different species.

Predation

Predation occurs when one organism, the predator, captures and eats another organism, the prey.

Example: a lion hunting a zebra.

Mutualism

Mutualism is a relationship in which both organisms benefit.

Commensalism

Commensalism is a relationship in which one organism benefits while the other is neither significantly helped nor harmed.

Parasitism

Parasitism is a relationship in which one organism, the parasite, benefits while the host is harmed.

Examples include:
  • Ticks feeding on animals
  • Some parasitic worms living in hosts
Relationship Effect
Mutualism Both benefit (+/+)
Commensalism One benefits, other is not significantly affected (+/0)
Parasitism Parasite benefits, host is harmed (+/-)
Predation Predator benefits, prey is harmed (+/-)
Competition Both competitors may be negatively affected (-/-)

🇪🇹 Ecology in Ethiopia

Ethiopia contains diverse environments and ecosystems because of differences in altitude, climate, rainfall, temperature and geography.

Different ecosystems support different plants and animals. Examples include highland environments, lowland areas, forests, grasslands, wetlands, lakes and river systems.

Why should we protect ecosystems?

  • To conserve biodiversity.
  • To protect habitats.
  • To maintain ecological balance.
  • To protect soil and water resources.
  • To support human livelihoods.
  • To protect plants and wildlife for future generations.
