7.1 Revision on Congruency and Similarity

Congruent Figures

Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and the same size.

Corresponding sides are equal.
Corresponding angles are equal.
Triangle ABC and triangle DEF are congruent if their corresponding sides and angles are equal.

Similar Figures

Two figures are similar if they have the same shape, but their sizes may be different.

Corresponding angles are equal.
Corresponding sides are proportional.

7.2 Definition of Similar Figures

Similar figures have the same shape but not necessarily the same size.

Corresponding angles are equal

Corresponding sides have the same ratio
Triangle ABC has sides 3, 4, 5.
Triangle DEF has sides 6, 8, 10.

6/3 = 8/4 = 10/5 = 2

Therefore the triangles are similar.
⚡ Similar = Same shape + proportional sides.

7.3 Theorems on Similar Plane Figures

Similarity Theorem

Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.

ΔABC ∼ ΔDEF

SSS Similarity Theorem

If the three corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, the triangles are similar.

AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF
Triangle 1:
3, 4, 5

Triangle 2:
6, 8, 10

3/6 = 4/8 = 5/10

Therefore the triangles are similar by SSS.

SAS Similarity Theorem

If two corresponding sides are proportional and the included angle is equal, the triangles are similar.

AB/DE = AC/DF

∠A = ∠D
⚡ SSS → three sides proportional.

SAS → two sides proportional + included angle equal.

7.4 Ratio of Perimeters of Similar Plane Figures

For similar figures, the ratio of their perimeters is equal to the ratio of their corresponding sides.

Perimeter ratio = Side ratio
A triangle has sides 3, 4, 5.
A similar triangle has sides 6, 8, 10.

Side ratio = 6/3 = 2

Therefore:
P₂/P₁ = 2
⭐ If the scale factor is k:

P₂ = kP₁

7.5 Ratio of Areas of Similar Plane Figures

The ratio of the areas of two similar figures is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

Area ratio = (Side ratio)²
If the side ratio is 2:3:

Area ratio = 2² : 3²
= 4 : 9
⚡ Length → k
Perimeter → k
Area → k²

7.6 Construction of Similar Figures

Similar figures can be constructed by enlarging or reducing a given figure using a scale factor.

Scale Factor

Scale Factor = New Length / Original Length
Original side = 4 cm
Scale factor = 3

New side = 4 × 3
= 12 cm

Types of Scale

If k > 1 → enlargement

If 0 < k < 1 → reduction

⚡ k = 1 → same size.

7.7 Applications of Similarity

Similarity is useful when measuring objects that are difficult to measure directly.

Example: Height of a Tree

A person is 1.5 m tall and casts a 2 m shadow. A tree casts a 10 m shadow. Find the height of the tree.

1.5 / 2 = h / 10

2h = 15
h = 7.5 m

Therefore the tree is 7.5 m tall.

Applications

  • Measuring heights of buildings
  • Measuring heights of trees
  • Maps and scale drawings
  • Engineering drawings
  • Architecture
  • Models and prototypes
