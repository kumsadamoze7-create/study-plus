Unit 3 — Natural Resource Base of Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Overview

Natural resources are the materials and environmental features that people obtain from nature and use to support life and economic activities.

Ethiopia has a wide variety of natural resources because of its large geographical area, varied altitude, different climatic conditions, diverse geology and ecosystems.

Important idea:

Natural resources support agriculture, industry, energy production, settlement, tourism and the livelihoods of millions of people. However, poor management can cause resource depletion and environmental degradation.

3.1 Meaning and Classification of Natural Resources

What is a Natural Resource?

Natural resources are useful materials, organisms, energy sources and environmental features that occur naturally and can be used by human beings.

Examples

  • Land
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Forests
  • Wildlife
  • Minerals
  • Fossil fuels
  • Solar energy
  • Wind energy
  • Geothermal energy

Classification by Renewability

Type Meaning Examples
Renewable resources Resources that can naturally regenerate if properly managed. Forests, water, wildlife, soil
Non-renewable resources Resources that exist in limited quantities and take very long periods to form. Minerals, coal, petroleum, natural gas

Renewable Resources

Renewable resources can be replenished naturally. However, renewable does not mean unlimited. A resource can become depleted if it is used faster than its natural regeneration rate.

Non-renewable Resources

Non-renewable resources are generally formed through geological processes over very long periods. Once they are heavily depleted, they cannot be replaced quickly on a human timescale.

🧠 Memory Trick

Renewable → Can regenerate
Non-renewable → Limited / takes very long to form

3.2 Land Resources of Ethiopia

Land is one of Ethiopia's most important natural resources. It provides space for agriculture, settlement, forests, infrastructure and other economic activities.

Major Uses of Land

  • Crop production
  • Livestock grazing
  • Settlement
  • Forestry
  • Industry
  • Transportation
  • Tourism
  • Wildlife conservation

Land Use

Land use refers to the way people utilize a particular area of land.

Different physical conditions determine which type of land use is most appropriate.

Example:

Fertile and relatively level land may be suitable for crop production, while steep mountainous land may be more suitable for forestry, grazing or conservation.

3.3 Soil Resources

What is Soil?

Soil is the upper layer of the Earth's surface that contains mineral particles, organic matter, water, air and living organisms and supports plant growth.

Importance of Soil

  • Supports crop production.
  • Provides nutrients for plants.
  • Supports natural vegetation.
  • Provides habitat for soil organisms.
  • Stores and filters water.
  • Supports ecosystems.

Factors Affecting Soil Formation

  • Parent material
  • Climate
  • Relief/topography
  • Living organisms
  • Time

Soil Erosion

Soil erosion is the removal and transport of the fertile upper layer of soil by agents such as running water and wind.

Causes of Soil Erosion

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Cultivation on steep slopes
  • Heavy rainfall and runoff
  • Improper farming practices
  • Removal of vegetation

Effects of Soil Erosion

  • Loss of fertile topsoil
  • Declining agricultural productivity
  • Siltation of rivers and reservoirs
  • Land degradation
  • Loss of soil nutrients
  • Reduced water quality

Soil Conservation

Soil conservation means protecting soil from erosion and maintaining its fertility and productive capacity.

Methods of Soil Conservation

  • Terracing
  • Contour ploughing
  • Strip cropping
  • Agroforestry
  • Afforestation
  • Controlled grazing
  • Crop rotation
  • Maintaining vegetation cover
🧠 Remember:

Remove vegetation → More runoff → More erosion

3.4 Water Resources of Ethiopia

Water is essential for human life, agriculture, industry, energy production and ecosystems.

Major Sources of Water

  • Rivers
  • Lakes
  • Groundwater
  • Springs
  • Rainfall
  • Wetlands

Rivers

Ethiopia has many rivers originating mainly from the highlands. They provide water for irrigation, hydroelectric power, domestic use and ecosystems.

Major River Systems

River / Basin Importance
Abay (Blue Nile) Hydropower, irrigation and regional water resources
Awash Irrigation, agriculture and domestic/industrial uses
Omo-Gibe Hydropower, agriculture and ecosystems
Wabi Shebelle Water supply, irrigation and pastoral areas
Genale-Dawa Water resources and agriculture

Lakes

Ethiopia has numerous lakes, particularly in and around the Rift Valley.

Lakes provide water, fisheries, tourism, transport in selected areas and habitats for wildlife.

Groundwater

Groundwater is water stored beneath the Earth's surface in spaces within soil and rocks.

It is an important source of water in areas where surface water is limited.

3.5 Water Resource Problems and Conservation

Major Problems

  • Water pollution
  • Deforestation in watersheds
  • Soil erosion and sedimentation
  • Seasonal water shortages
  • Flooding
  • Drought
  • Overuse of water resources

Water Conservation

  • Protecting watersheds
  • Planting trees
  • Efficient irrigation
  • Rainwater harvesting
  • Preventing pollution
  • Protecting wetlands
  • Using water efficiently
Watershed:

A watershed is an area of land from which water drains to a common river, lake or other outlet.

3.6 Forest Resources

Forests are areas dominated by trees and other vegetation. They are important natural resources with environmental, economic and social values.

Importance of Forests

  • Protect soil from erosion.
  • Improve watershed protection.
  • Provide habitat for wildlife.
  • Store carbon.
  • Provide fuelwood and construction materials.
  • Provide non-timber forest products.
  • Support tourism.
  • Help regulate local environmental conditions.

Deforestation

Deforestation is the removal or clearing of forests and conversion of forest land to other uses.

Causes of Deforestation

  • Agricultural expansion
  • Fuelwood collection
  • Charcoal production
  • Settlement expansion
  • Construction
  • Overgrazing
  • Illegal logging

Effects of Deforestation

  • Soil erosion
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Watershed degradation
  • Reduced habitat
  • Land degradation
  • Contribution to greenhouse gas emissions

Forest Conservation

  • Afforestation
  • Reforestation
  • Agroforestry
  • Community forest management
  • Controlling illegal cutting
  • Alternative energy sources

3.7 Wildlife Resources

Wildlife includes wild animals, birds and other organisms that live in natural environments.

Importance of Wildlife

  • Biodiversity conservation
  • Tourism
  • Scientific research
  • Ecological balance
  • Education
  • Potential economic benefits

Threats to Wildlife

  • Habitat destruction
  • Deforestation
  • Agricultural expansion
  • Illegal hunting
  • Climate change
  • Pollution
  • Human-wildlife conflict

Wildlife Conservation

Wildlife conservation means protecting wild species and their habitats so that they can survive and continue to perform their ecological functions.

Conservation Methods

  • National parks
  • Wildlife sanctuaries
  • Protected areas
  • Habitat restoration
  • Anti-poaching activities
  • Community participation

3.8 Mineral Resources

Minerals are naturally occurring inorganic substances with specific physical and chemical properties.

Importance of Minerals

  • Construction
  • Manufacturing
  • Energy production
  • Export earnings
  • Employment
  • Infrastructure development

Examples of Mineral Resources

  • Gold
  • Platinum
  • Potash
  • Salt
  • Iron-related minerals
  • Gemstones
  • Limestone

Mining

Mining is the process of extracting useful minerals from the Earth's crust.

Environmental Effects of Mining

  • Land degradation
  • Water pollution
  • Soil disturbance
  • Loss of vegetation
  • Habitat destruction
  • Dust and noise pollution
Responsible mining:

Mining should be managed carefully, with environmental protection, rehabilitation of disturbed land and appropriate waste management.

3.9 Energy Resources

Energy resources are sources from which useful energy can be obtained for households, transportation, industry and other activities.

Renewable Energy Resources

  • Hydropower
  • Solar energy
  • Wind energy
  • Geothermal energy
  • Biomass

Hydropower

Hydropower uses flowing or falling water to generate electricity.

Ethiopia has significant hydropower potential because of its highlands and river systems.

Solar Energy

Solar energy comes from radiation from the Sun. It can be used to produce electricity and heat.

Wind Energy

Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of moving air into electrical energy.

Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy uses heat from inside the Earth. It has particular potential in areas associated with tectonic and volcanic activity.

Biomass

Biomass energy comes from biological materials such as wood, agricultural residues and animal waste.

3.10 Non-renewable Energy Resources

Non-renewable energy resources are energy sources that exist in limited quantities and take very long periods to form.

Examples

  • Coal
  • Petroleum
  • Natural gas

Problems Associated with Fossil Fuels

  • Air pollution
  • Greenhouse gas emissions
  • Environmental degradation
  • Health impacts
  • Limited availability

3.11 Natural Resources and Tourism

Natural landscapes and ecosystems are important foundations for tourism.

Natural Attractions

  • Mountains
  • Lakes
  • Rivers
  • Waterfalls
  • Wildlife
  • Forests
  • Unique geological formations

Benefits of Tourism

  • Employment
  • Foreign exchange earnings
  • Local business opportunities
  • Infrastructure development
  • Conservation incentives
Important:

Tourism can also create pressure on natural resources if it is not managed sustainably.

3.12 Natural Resource Degradation

Natural resource degradation occurs when the quality, quantity or productive capacity of natural resources declines.

Major Causes

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Soil erosion
  • Population pressure
  • Unsustainable farming
  • Mining
  • Pollution
  • Climate variability and change

Effects

  • Loss of agricultural productivity
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Water shortages
  • Desertification and land degradation
  • Food insecurity
  • Loss of livelihoods

3.13 Natural Resource Conservation

Natural resource conservation means the responsible protection, management and sustainable use of natural resources.

Major Conservation Approaches

  • Afforestation and reforestation
  • Soil and water conservation
  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Protected areas
  • Community-based resource management
  • Efficient water use
  • Renewable energy development
  • Environmental education
  • Waste reduction and recycling

Sustainable Use

Sustainable resource use means using resources in ways that meet present needs while maintaining the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

🧠 Remember:

Use → Protect → Restore → Sustain

3.14 People and Natural Resources

People depend on natural resources for food, water, energy, shelter, employment and economic development.

At the same time, human activities can change the natural environment.

Positive Human Actions

  • Tree planting
  • Protected area management
  • Soil conservation
  • Water conservation
  • Renewable energy development
  • Environmental education

Negative Human Actions

  • Deforestation
  • Pollution
  • Overgrazing
  • Overfishing
  • Illegal hunting
  • Unsustainable mining
  • Unplanned settlement expansion
