Unit 4 — Solving Inequalities

📚 Study Plus Detailed Notes

📘 Unit 4 Overview

An inequality compares two quantities that are not necessarily equal. Instead of using only the equality sign, inequalities use symbols such as <, >, ≤ and ≥.

Important:
An inequality usually has a range of possible solutions, rather than only one answer.
⚡ Golden Rule:

When multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

> becomes <
< becomes >
≥ becomes ≤
≤ becomes ≥

4.1 Inequality Symbols

Symbol Meaning Example
< Less than 3 < 5
> Greater than 7 > 4
≤ Less than or equal to x ≤ 6
≥ Greater than or equal to x ≥ 2
Examples:

2 < 8 ✔️
9 > 3 ✔️
5 ≤ 5 ✔️
7 ≥ 7 ✔️
⚡ Memory Trick:
The wide/open side of the symbol points toward the larger number.

2 < 7
The wide side faces 7.

4.2 Equation vs Inequality

Equation Inequality
x + 3 = 8 x + 3 < 8
2x = 10 2x ≥ 10

An equation normally asks for values that make both sides exactly equal.

An inequality asks for all values that satisfy a comparison.

4.3 Representing Inequalities on a Number Line

A number line can be used to show the solution set.

Open Circle

Use an open circle when the endpoint is not included.

x > 3

3 is not included because the inequality is strictly greater.
←──────○──────→
     3

Closed Circle

Use a closed circle when the endpoint is included.

x ≥ 3

3 is included.
←──────●══════→
     3
⚡ Remember:

< or > → Open circle ○
≤ or ≥ → Closed circle ●

4.4 Solving One-Step Inequalities

Addition

Solve:
x + 5 < 12
Subtract 5 from both sides:
x < 7

Subtraction

x − 4 ≥ 9
Add 4:
x ≥ 13

Multiplication

3x < 15
Divide by 3:
x < 5
⚡ Rule:
Adding or subtracting the same number does not reverse the inequality.

4.5 Solving Inequalities with Negative Numbers

This is one of the most important rules in the unit.

⚠️ VERY IMPORTANT:

When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, you MUST reverse the inequality sign.
Solve:
−2x > 8
Divide by −2. Because we divide by a negative number, reverse the sign:
x < −4

Why does the sign reverse?

Multiplication by a negative number reverses the order of numbers on the number line.

Since:

2 > 1

Multiply both by −1:

−2 < −1
⚡ Exam Memory:

Negative × or ÷ → Flip the sign! 🔄

4.6 Two-Step Inequalities

Solve:
2x + 3 ≤ 11
Subtract 3:
2x ≤ 8
Divide by 2:
x ≤ 4

Example with Negative Coefficient

−3x + 6 ≥ 15
Subtract 6:
−3x ≥ 9
Divide by −3 and reverse the sign:
x ≤ −3
⚡ Order:
1. Undo addition/subtraction.
2. Undo multiplication/division.
3. Check whether you divided by a negative.

4.7 Inequalities with Brackets

Use the distributive property just as you do with equations.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
Solve:
2(x + 3) > 10
Expand:
2x + 6 > 10
Subtract 6:
2x > 4
Divide by 2:
x > 2
⚡ Bracket Trick:
Multiply the number outside by every term inside the bracket.

4.8 Variables on Both Sides

Solve:
5x + 2 > 2x + 11
Subtract 2x:
3x + 2 > 11
Subtract 2:
3x > 9
Divide by 3:
x > 3
⚡ Trick:
Put all x terms on one side and constants on the other.

4.9 Inequalities with Fractions

Use the same idea as solving equations with fractions. Multiply every term by a common denominator.

Solve:
x/3 + 2 < 6
Subtract 2:
x/3 < 4
Multiply by 3:
x < 12
If the multiplier is negative, remember to reverse the inequality sign.
⚡ Fraction Trick:
Find the LCM of the denominators and multiply the whole inequality by it.

4.10 Inequalities with Decimals

Solve:
0.5x > 4
Multiply both sides by 10:
5x > 40
Divide by 5:
x > 8
Multiply by 10, 100, 1000, etc. to remove decimal places.

4.11 Compound Inequalities

A compound inequality contains two inequality statements.

Example

2 < x < 7

This means x is greater than 2 and less than 7.

Possible integer values are: 3, 4, 5, 6

Example with ≤

2 ≤ x ≤ 7

Here both endpoints are included.

⚡ Remember:
< → endpoint excluded.
≤ → endpoint included.

4.12 Inequality Word Problems

Real-life conditions often use inequalities.

Common Words

Words Symbol
less than <
greater than >
at most ≤
at least ≥
no more than ≤
no less than ≥
Problem: A student needs at least 60 marks to pass. Let the mark be x.
x ≥ 60
⚡ Translation Trick:

"at least" → ≥
"at most" → ≤
"more than" → >
"less than" → <

4.13 Checking an Inequality Solution

Choose a test value from the proposed solution region and substitute it into the original inequality.

Suppose:
x > 3
Choose x = 5.
5 > 3 ✔️
So 5 belongs to the solution set. Choose x = 2:
2 > 3 ❌
So 2 does not belong to the solution set.

4.14 Special Cases

Always True

2x + 5 > 2x − 3
Subtract 2x:
5 > −3
This is always true. Therefore, all real numbers are solutions.

Always False

3x + 2 < 3x − 5
Subtract 3x:
2 < −5
This is false. Therefore, there is no solution.

4.15 Number-Line Rules

Solution Circle Direction
x > a Open ○ Right →
x ≥ a Closed ● Right →
x < a Open ○ Left ←
x ≤ a Closed ● Left ←
🧠 Easy Memory:

Greater → Right
Less → Left
Equal included → Closed ●
Equal not included → Open ○

🧠 Quick Revision

  • An inequality compares two expressions.
  • The main symbols are <, >, ≤ and ≥.
  • Open circle means the endpoint is not included.
  • Closed circle means the endpoint is included.
  • Greater-than solutions point right.
  • Less-than solutions point left.
  • Adding or subtracting does not reverse the sign.
  • Multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the sign.
  • Use the distributive property with brackets.
  • Use the LCM when fractions are present.
  • Translate word problems carefully.

🎯 Unit 4 Exam Tips

  • Always check whether you multiplied or divided by a negative number.
  • If you did, reverse the inequality sign.
  • Do not use a closed circle for < or >.
  • Do not use an open circle for ≤ or ≥.
  • Greater-than means shade or move to the right.
  • Less-than means shade or move to the left.
  • For word problems, pay special attention to words like "at least" and "at most".
  • Check your answer using a test value.
