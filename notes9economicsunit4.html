📖 Unit Introduction

Demand and supply are two fundamental concepts in economics. They help us understand how consumers and producers behave in markets and how prices and quantities are determined.

Demand represents the quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given period of time.

Supply represents the quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices during a given period of time.

The interaction between demand and supply determines the market equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity.

Main idea:

Demand → Consumer side
Supply → Producer side
Demand + Supply → Market Equilibrium

4.1 Concept of Demand

Meaning of Demand

The terms demand, desire and want are sometimes used to describe what a person would like to have. However, in economics demand has a more specific meaning.

Demand means the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to buy at various given prices during a given period of time.

Demand is an effective desire backed by the ability and willingness to buy.

Effective Demand

A desire becomes effective demand when it is supported by the necessary conditions for purchasing the commodity.

The important conditions include:

  • Ability to pay for the commodity.
  • Willingness to pay the price.
  • Availability of the commodity.
Example:

A student may want to buy a laptop. But if the student does not have enough money to buy it, the desire alone is not effective demand.

If the student has enough money and is willing to buy the laptop at the given price, the desire can become effective demand.

Demand and Price

Demand is always considered in relation to a particular price and a particular period of time.

For example, a consumer may be willing to buy 5 kg of oranges at Birr 20 per kg this week.

Ceteris Paribus

Ceteris paribus is a Latin expression meaning "other things remaining constant."

When economists study the relationship between price and quantity demanded, they assume other relevant factors remain unchanged.

📉 Law of Demand

The law of demand states that, ceteris paribus, the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded are inversely related.

Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓

Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑

Therefore, when the price of a commodity increases, consumers normally demand a smaller quantity. When the price decreases, consumers normally demand a larger quantity, assuming other things remain constant.

Why does the demand curve slope downward?

  • Consumers tend to buy more when prices fall.
  • Consumers tend to buy less when prices rise.
  • A lower price increases purchasing power.
  • Consumers may substitute cheaper goods for relatively expensive alternatives.
Exam rule:

Demand curve → slopes downward from left to right.

📋 Demand Schedule

A demand schedule is a tabular representation of the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

It shows the quantity of a commodity demanded by a consumer or group of consumers at different prices during a specified period.

Individual Household Demand Schedule

Price (Birr/kg) Quantity Demanded (kg/week)
5 5
4 7
3 9
2 11
1 13
At Birr 5 per kg, the consumer demands 5 kg. At Birr 1 per kg, the consumer demands 13 kg. This demonstrates the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

📉 Demand Curve

A demand curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

The vertical axis normally represents Price (P) and the horizontal axis represents Quantity Demanded (Qd).

PRICE ↑
HIGH PRICE → LOW QUANTITY DEMANDED

LOW PRICE → HIGH QUANTITY DEMANDED
QUANTITY DEMANDED →
The demand curve normally slopes downward from left to right.

🧮 Demand Function

A demand function is a mathematical representation of the relationship between price and quantity demanded, ceteris paribus.

Qd = f(P)

Where:

  • Qd = Quantity demanded
  • P = Price of the commodity
A simple demand relationship may be written as:

Qd = 20 − 2P

If P = 5:
Qd = 20 − 2(5)
Qd = 10

👥 Market Demand

Market demand refers to the total quantity of a commodity that all consumers in a market are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given period.

Market demand is obtained by adding the quantities demanded by individual consumers at each price.

Market Demand = Sum of individual demands at each price

Individual and Market Demand Schedule

Price Consumer 1 Consumer 2 Consumer 3 Market Demand
8 0 0 0 0
5 3 5 1 9
3 5 7 2 14
0 7 9 4 20
At Birr 5: Consumer 1 = 3 kg Consumer 2 = 5 kg Consumer 3 = 1 kg Therefore: Market Demand = 3 + 5 + 1 = 9 kg

Horizontal Summation

The market demand curve is obtained by horizontally adding the individual demand curves at each price.

📌 Factors Affecting Demand

Besides the price of the commodity itself, several factors can affect demand.

  • Income of consumers
  • Prices of related goods
  • Tastes and preferences
  • Number of consumers
  • Expectations about future prices and income
  • Population and demographic factors
Important: A change in the commodity's own price normally causes a change in quantity demanded, represented by movement along the demand curve. A change in other demand determinants can cause the demand curve itself to shift.

4.2 Concept of Supply

Meaning of Supply

Supply refers to the quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices during a given period.

Supply vs Stock

Stock is the total quantity of a commodity available with a producer, while supply refers to the quantity actually brought to the market and offered for sale at a particular price.

A farmer may harvest 1,000 kg of oranges but keep some in storage. The quantity actually brought to the market for sale represents the supply at that time.

📈 Law of Supply

The law of supply states that, ceteris paribus, there is a direct or positive relationship between the price of a commodity and its quantity supplied.

Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑

Price ↓ → Quantity Supplied ↓

Therefore, producers normally offer a larger quantity for sale when the price is higher and a smaller quantity when the price is lower, other things remaining constant.

Exam rule:

Supply curve → slopes upward from left to right.

📋 Supply Schedule

A supply schedule is a tabular presentation of different quantities of a commodity offered for sale at different prices during a given period.

Price (Birr/kg) Quantity Supplied (kg/week)
10 60
15 70
20 80
25 90
30 100
As the price increases from Birr 10 to Birr 30, quantity supplied increases from 60 kg to 100 kg. This illustrates the law of supply.

📈 Supply Curve

A supply curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between price and quantity supplied.

Price is normally shown on the vertical axis and quantity supplied on the horizontal axis.

PRICE ↑
HIGH PRICE → HIGH QUANTITY SUPPLIED

LOW PRICE → LOW QUANTITY SUPPLIED
QUANTITY SUPPLIED →
The supply curve normally slopes upward from left to right.

🧮 Supply Function

A supply function is a mathematical representation of the relationship between price and quantity supplied, with other factors remaining constant.

Qs = f(P)

Where:

  • Qs = Quantity supplied
  • P = Price of the commodity
A simple supply relationship may be:

Qs = 10 + 2P

If P = 5:
Qs = 10 + 2(5)
Qs = 20

🏪 Market Supply

Market supply is the total amount of a commodity that all producers in a market are willing and able to sell at different prices during a given period.

Market Supply = Sum of individual supplies at each price

The market supply curve is obtained by horizontally adding the individual supply curves of all sellers at each price.

📌 Factors Affecting Supply

  • Price of inputs
  • Technology
  • Prices of related goods in production
  • Number of sellers
  • Taxes and subsidies
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Natural conditions
A change in the commodity's own price normally changes quantity supplied and causes movement along the supply curve. A change in other supply determinants can shift the supply curve.

⚖️ Demand vs Supply

Demand Supply
Represents consumers. Represents producers.
Shows willingness and ability to buy. Shows willingness and ability to sell.
Price and quantity demanded are inversely related. Price and quantity supplied are directly related.
Demand curve slopes downward. Supply curve slopes upward.
Qd = f(P) Qs = f(P)

4.3 Market Equilibrium

Meaning of Equilibrium

Equilibrium refers to a state of balance.

In a market, equilibrium occurs when the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy equals the quantity producers are willing and able to sell at a given price.

Qd = Qs

Market Equilibrium

Market equilibrium is the point at which the market demand curve and market supply curve intersect.

At this point, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Equilibrium Price

Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Equilibrium Quantity

Equilibrium quantity is the quantity bought and sold at the equilibrium price.

Equilibrium Condition:

Qd = Qs

Example

Price Quantity Demanded Quantity Supplied Result
10 100 40 Shortage
20 80 60 Shortage
30 60 60 Equilibrium
40 40 80 Surplus
At price = Birr 30: Quantity demanded = 60 Quantity supplied = 60 Therefore: Equilibrium Price = Birr 30 Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units

📉 Shortage

A shortage occurs when quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at a particular price.

Qd > Qs → Shortage
If: Qd = 100 Qs = 60 Then: Shortage = 100 − 60 = 40 units

📈 Surplus

A surplus occurs when quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at a particular price.

Qs > Qd → Surplus
If: Qs = 100 Qd = 60 Then: Surplus = 100 − 60 = 40 units

📊 Market Equilibrium Graph

Graphically, equilibrium is found at the intersection of the demand curve and supply curve.

PRICE ↑ | S | / | / |---●--- Equilibrium | / \ | / \ |/ \ D +----------------→ QUANTITY

The equilibrium point determines both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity.

🔄 Changes in Demand and Supply

Increase in Demand

An increase in demand means consumers are willing and able to buy more at each given price.

Other things remaining constant, an increase in demand tends to raise equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity.

Decrease in Demand

A decrease in demand means consumers are willing and able to buy less at each given price.

Other things remaining constant, a decrease in demand tends to lower equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity.

Increase in Supply

An increase in supply means producers are willing and able to sell more at each given price.

Other things remaining constant, an increase in supply tends to lower equilibrium price and raise equilibrium quantity.

Decrease in Supply

A decrease in supply means producers are willing and able to sell less at each given price.

Other things remaining constant, a decrease in supply tends to raise equilibrium price and lower equilibrium quantity.
