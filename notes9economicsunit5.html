📖 Unit Introduction

Production and cost are important concepts in economics. Production explains how firms transform inputs into outputs, while cost explains the resources and expenses used in producing goods and services.

This unit introduces the basic concepts of production and production function, the short-run and long-run periods of production, stages of production, and different types of production costs.

5.1 Definition of Production, Inputs and Outputs

Meaning of Production

Production is the process of combining various inputs to produce an output for consumption. It is the process through which inputs are transformed into goods and services that satisfy human wants.

Production = Transformation of Inputs into Outputs
Example:

A farmer uses land, labour, seeds, fertilizer and tools to produce wheat.

Land + Labour + Capital + Seeds + Other inputs

↓

Wheat

Meaning of Input

Inputs are economic resources used in the production of goods and services.

There are four basic inputs commonly used in production:

  1. Land
  2. Labour
  3. Capital
  4. Entrepreneurial ability

1. Land

Land includes all natural resources used in production.

Examples include soil, water, forests, minerals and other natural resources.

2. Labour

Labour refers to the physical and mental effort of people used in the production of goods and services.

Examples:
Teacher, farmer, driver, engineer, doctor and factory worker.

3. Capital

Capital refers to produced resources used to produce other goods and services.

Examples: Machines, buildings, tools, vehicles and equipment.

4. Entrepreneurial Ability

Entrepreneurial ability is the ability to organize and combine the other factors of production and take business decisions and risks.

Outputs

Outputs are the final goods and services produced from the inputs used in production.

Inputs → Production Process → Outputs

Goods and Services as Outputs

Outputs can be tangible goods or intangible services.

Goods Services
Wheat Education
Clothes Medical treatment
Furniture Transportation
Food Banking

Fixed Inputs

A fixed input is an input whose quantity cannot easily be changed during the period under consideration.

Examples:
Buildings, machinery and some types of capital equipment.

Variable Inputs

A variable input is an input whose quantity can be changed during the period under consideration.

Examples:
Raw materials and labour.
Fixed Input Variable Input
Quantity cannot easily change in the period. Quantity can change in the period.
Building Labour

Production Function

A production function describes the technical relationship between inputs and outputs.

Production Function:

Q = f(L, K, Land, E)

Where:

  • Q = Output
  • L = Labour
  • K = Capital
  • Land = Natural resources
  • E = Entrepreneurial ability
Remember: Production function describes the maximum output that can be produced from given combinations of inputs under given technology.

5.2 Periods of Production

Meaning of Production Period

Periods of production are classified according to the degree of flexibility of economic resources.

Short Run

The short run is a period of production in which at least one input is fixed while other inputs can be varied.

Short Run → At least one input is fixed.
A bakery may have a fixed building and oven but can increase the number of workers and the amount of flour used.

Long Run

The long run is a period of production in which all inputs can be varied.

Long Run → All inputs are variable.
Short Run Long Run
At least one input is fixed. All inputs are variable.
Some inputs cannot be changed immediately. All inputs can be adjusted.
Firm has limited flexibility. Firm has greater flexibility.

Total Product (TP)

Total Product is the total amount of output produced using a given amount of inputs.

TP = Total Output

Average Product (AP)

Average Product is the amount of total product produced per unit of variable input.

AP = TP / L

Where L represents labour or the variable input.

If: TP = 100 quintals Labour = 5 workers

AP = 100 / 5
AP = 20 quintals per worker

Marginal Product (MP)

Marginal Product is the change in total product resulting from using one additional unit of variable input.

MP = ΔTP / ΔL
If total product increases from 50 to 70 when one additional worker is employed:

MP = 70 − 50
MP = 20 units

Stages of Production

The short-run production process can be divided into three stages based on the behaviour of Total Product, Average Product and Marginal Product.

Stage I — Increasing Returns

In Stage I, total product increases rapidly and average product increases.

Marginal product is generally increasing and reaches its maximum before beginning to fall.

Stage II — Diminishing Returns

In Stage II, total product continues to increase but at a decreasing rate.

Marginal product is positive but decreasing and eventually becomes zero.

Stage II ends when MP = 0 and TP reaches its maximum.

Stage III — Negative Returns

In Stage III, total product decreases as more units of the variable input are added.

Marginal product becomes negative.

MP < 0 → Total Product decreases.
🧠 Easy Memory:

Stage I → TP increasing rapidly
Stage II → TP increasing slowly
Stage III → TP decreasing

5.3 Cost of Production

Meaning of Cost of Production

Cost of production refers to the expenses or sacrifices made by a firm in order to produce goods and services.

Production requires resources, and using those resources has a cost.

Explicit Cost

Explicit costs are actual monetary payments made by a firm for inputs and services.

Examples:
  • Wages paid to workers
  • Rent paid for a building
  • Payment for raw materials
  • Electricity bills
  • Transportation expenses

Implicit Cost

Implicit costs are the opportunity costs of using resources owned by the firm or owner without making direct monetary payments.

If an entrepreneur uses his own building for a business instead of renting it to another person, the rent that could have been earned is an implicit cost.

Economic Cost

Economic cost includes both explicit and implicit costs.

Economic Cost = Explicit Cost + Implicit Cost

Accounting Cost

Accounting cost generally refers to the monetary expenses recorded in the firm's accounts, mainly explicit costs.

Cost Meaning
Explicit Cost Actual monetary payment
Implicit Cost Opportunity cost of owned resources
Economic Cost Explicit + Implicit cost
Accounting Cost Recorded monetary expenses

Fixed Cost

Fixed cost is a cost that does not change with the level of output in the short run.

Examples:
Rent, insurance and some payments associated with fixed assets.

Variable Cost

Variable cost changes as the level of output changes.

Examples:
Raw materials, fuel and some labour costs.

Total Fixed Cost (TFC)

Total Fixed Cost is the total amount of fixed cost incurred by a firm.

TFC = Fixed Cost

Total Variable Cost (TVC)

Total Variable Cost is the total amount of variable cost incurred by a firm at a particular level of output.

Total Cost (TC)

Total Cost is the sum of total fixed cost and total variable cost.

TC = TFC + TVC

This illustrates that fixed cost remains 60 Birr even when output is zero, while variable cost changes with output. Total cost is the sum of the two. 2

Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

Average Fixed Cost is fixed cost per unit of output.

AFC = TFC / Q

Average Variable Cost (AVC)

Average Variable Cost is variable cost per unit of output.

AVC = TVC / Q

Average Total Cost (ATC)

Average Total Cost is total cost per unit of output.

ATC = TC / Q

Average total cost can also be divided into average fixed cost and average variable cost.

ATC = AFC + AVC

Marginal Cost (MC)

Marginal cost is the additional cost resulting from producing one more unit of output.

MC = ΔTC / ΔQ

In the short run, because total fixed cost does not change:

MC = ΔTVC / ΔQ
Example:

Suppose total cost increases from 100 Birr to 125 Birr when output increases from 2 units to 4 units.

MC = (125 − 100) / (4 − 2)

MC = 25 / 2

MC = 12.5 Birr per additional unit

📊 Relationship Between Production and Cost

Production and cost are closely related. A firm uses inputs to produce output, and the use of inputs creates production costs.

INPUTS ↓ PRODUCTION PROCESS ↓ OUTPUT ↓ COST OF PRODUCTION

When a firm changes the quantity of variable inputs, output changes and variable costs also change.

Therefore, production decisions and cost decisions are closely connected.

🧮 Important Formulas

Concept Formula
Average Product AP = TP / L
Marginal Product MP = ΔTP / ΔL
Total Cost TC = TFC + TVC
Average Fixed Cost AFC = TFC / Q
Average Variable Cost AVC = TVC / Q
Average Total Cost ATC = TC / Q
Average Cost Relationship ATC = AFC + AVC
Marginal Cost MC = ΔTC / ΔQ

📝 Unit Summary

  • Production is the process of transforming inputs into outputs.
  • The four basic inputs are land, labour, capital and entrepreneurial ability.
  • Outputs are goods and services produced through the production process.
  • Fixed inputs cannot easily be changed during the period under consideration.
  • Variable inputs can be changed during the period under consideration.
  • The short run is a period in which at least one input is fixed.
  • The long run is a period in which all inputs are variable.
  • Total Product is the total output produced.
  • Average Product is output per unit of variable input.
  • Marginal Product is the additional output produced by an additional unit of variable input.
  • Production has three stages: Stage I, Stage II and Stage III.
  • Explicit cost involves actual monetary payments.
  • Implicit cost represents the opportunity cost of owned resources.
  • Economic cost is explicit cost plus implicit cost.
  • Fixed cost does not change with output in the short run.
  • Variable cost changes with output.
  • Total cost equals total fixed cost plus total variable cost.
  • Average total cost equals total cost divided by output.
  • Marginal cost is the additional cost of producing an additional unit of output.
