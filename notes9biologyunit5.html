Unit 5 — Human Health, Nutrition, and Disease

📖 Complete Study Notes

📚 Unit 5 Introduction

Human health depends on many factors including proper nutrition, clean water, personal hygiene, physical activity, a safe environment, disease prevention and appropriate medical care.

Nutrition is especially important because the human body needs different nutrients for energy, growth, repair and normal body functions.

Key idea:

Good health is supported by a balanced diet, healthy lifestyle, disease prevention and proper use of health services.

🍎 5.1 Food and Nutrition

What is Food?

Food is any substance that is eaten or drunk by humans and that provides nutrients needed by the body.

What is Nutrition?

Nutrition is the process by which living organisms obtain and use food substances for energy, growth, repair and maintenance of the body.

Why do we need food?

  • To provide energy.
  • To support growth and development.
  • To repair damaged tissues.
  • To maintain normal body functions.
  • To protect the body from diseases.

🥗 5.2 Nutrients

Nutrients are chemical substances found in food that are needed by the human body for growth, energy and normal functioning.

The major nutrients include carbohydrates, fats and oils, proteins, vitamins and minerals. Water and dietary fibre are also essential parts of a healthy diet.

Nutrient Main Function Examples
Carbohydrates Provide energy Rice, bread, maize, potatoes
Fats and oils Provide concentrated energy and help insulation Oil, butter, nuts, seeds
Proteins Growth and repair of body tissues Eggs, meat, beans, milk
Vitamins Help regulate chemical reactions and body functions Fruits and vegetables
Minerals Needed for body structures and chemical processes Milk, vegetables, meat
Water Transport, temperature control and chemical reactions Water, fruits, vegetables
Fibre Helps digestion and prevents constipation Vegetables, fruits, whole grains

🍞 Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are nutrients that mainly provide energy for the body.

One gram of carbohydrate provides approximately 16 kJ of energy.

Examples

  • Maize
  • Rice
  • Bread
  • Potatoes
  • Grains
  • Fruits

Functions

  • Provide energy for daily activities.
  • Provide energy for body processes.
  • Help spare proteins from being used as an energy source.

🥑 Fats and Oils

Fats and oils are important nutrients that provide a concentrated source of energy.

One gram of lipid provides approximately 37 kJ of energy.

Functions

  • Provide energy.
  • Store energy in the body.
  • Help insulate the body.
  • Protect some internal organs.
  • Help the body absorb certain vitamins.

Sources

  • Vegetable oils
  • Nuts
  • Seeds
  • Butter
  • Animal fats

🥚 Proteins

Proteins are nutrients required for growth and repair of body tissues. They are also involved in many important processes in the body.

One gram of protein can provide approximately 17 kJ of energy.

Functions

  • Growth of the body.
  • Repair of damaged tissues.
  • Formation of new cells.
  • Production of enzymes.
  • Production of some hormones.

Sources

  • Eggs
  • Milk
  • Meat
  • Fish
  • Beans
  • Lentils
  • Peas
Protein deficiency:

Severe protein deficiency, especially in children, can lead to serious growth and health problems.

🍊 Vitamins

Vitamins are organic substances required in relatively small amounts for normal body functions.

Different vitamins have different functions. A deficiency of a particular vitamin can cause a specific deficiency disease.

Vitamin Important Function Deficiency
Vitamin A Vision and healthy tissues Night blindness
Vitamin B group Energy metabolism and nervous system functions Different deficiency diseases
Vitamin C Healthy connective tissues and wound healing Scurvy
Vitamin D Helps calcium absorption and bone development Rickets

🧂 Minerals

Minerals are inorganic substances required by the body in small amounts for normal growth and body functions.

Mineral Function Deficiency Problem
Calcium Strong bones and teeth Weak bones
Iron Formation of haemoglobin Anaemia
Iodine Normal thyroid function Goitre
Phosphorus Bones, teeth and energy processes Weak bones

💧 Water

Water is essential for life and makes up a large proportion of the human body.

Functions of Water

  • Helps transport substances around the body.
  • Helps regulate body temperature.
  • Provides a medium for chemical reactions.
  • Helps remove waste products.
  • Helps maintain normal cell functions.

🌾 Dietary Fibre

Dietary fibre is the indigestible part of plant material.

Importance

  • Helps food move through the digestive tract.
  • Helps prevent constipation.
  • Helps maintain a healthy digestive system.

Sources

  • Vegetables
  • Fruits
  • Whole grains
  • Beans

🍽️ 5.3 Balanced Diet

A balanced diet is a diet that contains the different nutrients and water required by the body in appropriate amounts.

A balanced diet should provide:

  • Carbohydrates
  • Proteins
  • Fats and oils
  • Vitamins
  • Minerals
  • Water
  • Fibre

The exact amount of food required varies depending on factors such as age, sex, physical activity and physiological condition.

⚠️ 5.4 Malnutrition

Malnutrition occurs when the body receives too little, too much, or an inappropriate balance of nutrients.

Types

  • Undernutrition
  • Overnutrition
  • Nutrient deficiency
  • Nutrient imbalance

Malnutrition can affect growth, development, immunity and overall health.

🚫 5.6 Substance Abuse

Substance abuse is the harmful or inappropriate use of substances that can affect physical or mental health.

Examples

  • Alcohol
  • Tobacco
  • Some drugs
  • Other harmful substances

Possible Effects

  • Damage to body organs.
  • Reduced ability to make good decisions.
  • Dependence and addiction.
  • Problems in school and social life.
  • Increased risk of accidents.
  • Long-term health problems.

🦠 5.7 Disease

A disease is a condition that interferes with the normal functioning of the body or mind.

Diseases may be caused by infectious agents, genetic factors, nutritional problems, environmental factors, lifestyle and other causes.

🦠 5.8 Infectious Diseases

Infectious diseases are diseases caused by disease-causing microorganisms or other infectious agents and can be transmitted from one person or host to another.

Examples

  • Malaria
  • Tuberculosis
  • Cholera
  • Measles
  • HIV/AIDS
  • COVID-19

Possible Disease-Causing Agents

  • Bacteria
  • Viruses
  • Protozoa
  • Fungi
  • Parasitic organisms

🔄 5.9 Transmission of Infectious Diseases

Infectious diseases can spread through different routes depending on the disease and the infectious agent.

Common routes include:

  • Airborne droplets and particles.
  • Contaminated food and water.
  • Direct contact.
  • Contaminated objects.
  • Blood and body fluids.
  • Vectors such as mosquitoes.

🛡️ 5.10 Prevention and Control of Disease

Important methods include:

  • Regular hand washing.
  • Safe drinking water.
  • Proper food hygiene.
  • Proper disposal of waste.
  • Good personal hygiene.
  • Vaccination where appropriate.
  • Vector control.
  • Early diagnosis and treatment.
  • Using health services appropriately.
  • Maintaining a healthy lifestyle.

🦠 5.11 COVID-19

COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the coronavirus SARS-CoV-2.

Transmission

The virus can spread through respiratory particles released when an infected person breathes, talks, coughs or sneezes. Risk can be higher in crowded or poorly ventilated places.

Common symptoms

  • Fever
  • Cough
  • Tiredness
  • Sore throat
  • Headache
  • Muscle aches
  • Shortness of breath in some cases

Prevention

  • Follow appropriate public-health guidance.
  • Improve ventilation.
  • Avoid close contact when sick.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes.
  • Wash hands regularly.

❤️ 5.12 Non-Infectious Diseases

Non-infectious diseases are diseases that are not caused by infectious organisms and are generally not transmitted from person to person.

Examples

  • Diabetes
  • Some forms of cancer
  • Hypertension
  • Some cardiovascular diseases
  • Some genetic disorders

🏃 5.13 Healthy Lifestyle

A healthy lifestyle helps reduce the risk of many health problems.

  • Eat a balanced diet.
  • Exercise regularly.
  • Get adequate sleep.
  • Maintain personal hygiene.
  • Avoid harmful substance use.
  • Drink safe water.
  • Seek medical care when necessary.

📌 Unit 5 Summary

  • Food provides nutrients required by the body.
  • Carbohydrates are an important source of energy.
  • Proteins are important for growth and repair.
  • Fats and oils provide concentrated energy and other functions.
  • Vitamins and minerals are needed in relatively small amounts.
  • Water and fibre are important for healthy body functions.
  • A balanced diet provides nutrients in appropriate amounts.
  • Malnutrition results from inadequate, excessive or imbalanced nutrition.
  • Deficiency of nutrients can cause diseases.
  • Infectious diseases are caused by infectious agents.
  • Non-infectious diseases are not transmitted from person to person.
  • Good hygiene, safe food and water, vaccination and appropriate health care can help prevent disease.
  • A healthy lifestyle supports human health.
