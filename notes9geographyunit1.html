🌍 Grade 9 Geography

Unit 1 — Geological History and Topography of Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Overview

This unit introduces the meaning, scope and branches of Geography. It also explains the location, size and shape of Ethiopia, the geological history of Ethiopia and the major processes responsible for the formation of the country's present topography and landforms.

Important:

Geography does not only describe places. It studies where things are located, why they are located there, how places are different, and how people and the natural environment interact.

1.1 Geography: Meaning, Scope and Branches

Meaning of Geography

Geography is the scientific study of the Earth's surface, places, people, physical features, human activities and the relationships between people and their environment.

The word Geography comes from Greek words:

  • Geo — Earth
  • Graphia/Graphos — writing, description or study

Therefore, Geography can broadly be understood as the study or description of the Earth and its phenomena.

What Does Geography Study?

Geography studies both natural and human phenomena. Important areas include:

  • Landforms
  • Climate and weather
  • Soils
  • Vegetation
  • Rivers and lakes
  • Population
  • Settlement
  • Agriculture
  • Industry
  • Transport
  • Trade
  • Culture
  • Human-environment relationships

Scope of Geography

The scope of Geography is very broad because it deals with both physical and human phenomena and their spatial relationships.

Major Spheres Studied in Geography

Sphere Meaning
Lithosphere The solid rocky part of the Earth.
Hydrosphere All forms of water on, under and around the Earth.
Atmosphere The layer of gases surrounding the Earth.
Biosphere The zone where living organisms exist.

Branches of Geography

1. Physical Geography

Physical Geography studies the natural features and processes of the Earth.

  • Geomorphology — study of landforms
  • Climatology — study of climate
  • Meteorology — study of weather and atmospheric conditions
  • Hydrology — study of water
  • Biogeography — study of distribution of living organisms
  • Soil Geography — study of soils and their distribution

2. Human Geography

Human Geography studies people, their activities, distribution and relationships with places.

  • Population Geography
  • Settlement Geography
  • Economic Geography
  • Cultural Geography
  • Political Geography
🧠 Short Trick

Physical Geography = Nature 🌍
Human Geography = People 👥

Example:

Studying the formation of mountains is Physical Geography. Studying population distribution is Human Geography. Studying how rainfall affects agriculture connects physical and human geography.

1.2 Location, Size and Shape of Ethiopia

Geographical Location

Location refers to the position of a place on the Earth's surface.

The location of Ethiopia can be described using absolute and relative location.

Absolute Location

Absolute location identifies the exact position of a place using latitude and longitude.

Ethiopia:

Approximately between 3°N and 15°N latitude and 33°E and 48°E longitude.

Relative Location

Relative location describes the position of a place in relation to other places.

Ethiopia is located in the Horn of Africa.

Countries Bordering Ethiopia

Direction Country

Landlocked Position

Ethiopia is a landlocked country. This means that it does not have a direct coastline or seaport.

However, its position in the Horn of Africa gives it important geographical, economic and strategic connections with neighbouring countries and the Red Sea region.

Size of Ethiopia

Ethiopia has a large territory of approximately 1.1 million square kilometres.

The large size of the country contributes to its diversity in:

  • Climate
  • Altitude
  • Landforms
  • Vegetation
  • Soils
  • Agricultural activities
  • Natural resources

Shape of Ethiopia

The shape and size of a country influence transportation, communication, administration and the distribution of economic activities.

Ethiopia has a broad and irregular shape with a large variety of physical environments.

1.3 Geological History of Ethiopia

The geological history of Ethiopia explains how the rocks, mountains, plateaus, valleys, volcanic features and other landforms of the country developed through very long periods of geological time.

What is Geology?

Geology is the scientific study of the Earth, including its rocks, minerals, structure, history and the processes that have shaped it.

Major Geological Processes

The Earth's surface is continuously affected by internal and external processes.

1. Endogenic / Internal Processes

Endogenic processes originate from inside the Earth. They are mainly driven by internal heat and energy.

  • Tectonic movement
  • Folding
  • Faulting
  • Earthquakes
  • Volcanic activity
  • Uplift and subsidence

2. Exogenic / External Processes

Exogenic processes operate mainly at or near the Earth's surface.

  • Weathering
  • Erosion
  • Transportation
  • Deposition
  • River action
  • Wind action
  • Glacial action
🧠 Easy Memory Trick

Endogenic = ENDO = Inside
Exogenic = EXO = Outside

Weathering

Weathering is the breakdown or decomposition of rocks at or near the Earth's surface without the materials necessarily being transported away.

Types of Weathering

  • Physical weathering — rocks break into smaller pieces without major chemical change.
  • Chemical weathering — minerals in rocks are chemically altered.
  • Biological weathering — plants, animals and other organisms contribute to rock breakdown.

Erosion

Erosion is the wearing away and removal of soil or rock materials by agents such as running water, wind, ice and waves.

Deposition

Deposition occurs when transported materials are dropped or deposited in a new location.

Example:

River water can erode soil from one location, transport the material downstream and deposit it where the river flow becomes slower.

🌋 Tectonic Processes

Tectonic processes are movements and deformation of the Earth's crust caused mainly by movements of tectonic plates.

Plate Movement

The Earth's outer rigid layer is divided into large tectonic plates. Their movement can cause major changes to the Earth's surface.

Folding

Folding occurs when layers of rocks are compressed and bend rather than breaking.

Folding can contribute to the formation of mountain ranges and elevated landforms.

Faulting

Faulting occurs when rocks break and move along fractures called faults.

Faulting is especially important in the development of rift valleys.

Earthquakes

An earthquake is the sudden release of energy within the Earth that produces seismic waves and ground shaking.

Volcanism

Volcanism refers to the movement of magma toward or onto the Earth's surface.

Volcanic activity has played an important role in shaping parts of Ethiopia's highlands and Rift Valley.

🏔️ The Great Rift Valley in Ethiopia

The Ethiopian Rift Valley forms part of the larger East African Rift System.

It is associated with tectonic activity, faulting, volcanism and the formation of numerous lakes and depressions.

Important Features

  • Faults and fractures
  • Volcanic cones
  • Hot springs
  • Lakes
  • Escarpments
  • Low-lying areas

Importance of the Rift Valley

  • Tourism
  • Lake resources
  • Geothermal energy potential
  • Agriculture in suitable areas
  • Unique ecosystems
  • Mineral resources in some areas

⛰️ 1.4 Topography and Major Landforms of Ethiopia

Topography refers to the arrangement and variation of the physical features and elevations of an area.

Major Physiographic Regions

Ethiopia is characterized by considerable variation in altitude. Major physical regions include highlands, lowlands, plateaus, mountains, valleys and escarpments.

Ethiopian Highlands

The Ethiopian Highlands occupy a large part of the country and contain extensive plateaus, mountains, deep valleys and escarpments.

The highlands have historically supported dense settlement and agriculture in many areas because of relatively favourable climatic conditions and fertile soils in suitable locations.

Lowlands

The lowlands are generally lower in elevation and often have warmer and, in many places, drier climates.

Pastoralism and agro-pastoralism are important livelihood systems in many lowland areas.

Mountains

Ethiopia contains many high mountains and volcanic mountains.

Mountains influence climate, drainage, vegetation and human settlement.

Plateaus

A plateau is an elevated area of land with a relatively broad or gently undulating surface compared with surrounding steep slopes.

Valleys

A valley is a relatively low area between higher landforms. Rivers commonly flow through valleys.

Escarpments

An escarpment is a steep slope or long cliff-like break in the general level of the land.

📈 Altitude and Its Importance

Altitude is the height of a place above mean sea level.

Altitude has a strong influence on temperature, rainfall patterns, vegetation, agriculture and settlement.

General Rule:

As altitude increases, temperature generally decreases. Therefore, highland areas tend to be cooler than lowland areas at similar latitudes.

Effects of Altitude

  • Temperature differences
  • Variation in vegetation
  • Different agricultural systems
  • Influence on settlement patterns
  • Influence on livestock production
  • Influence on rainfall and local climate

⭐ Importance of Ethiopia's Diverse Topography

The variation in Ethiopia's topography produces important opportunities and challenges.

Advantages

  • Different agricultural environments
  • Rich biodiversity
  • Hydroelectric power potential
  • Tourism opportunities
  • Different mineral resources
  • Varied climate and vegetation
  • Water resources

Challenges

  • Steep slopes can make transportation difficult.
  • Soil erosion can be severe on cultivated slopes.
  • Some lowlands have harsh climatic conditions.
  • Mountainous terrain can make infrastructure expensive.
  • Different environments require different development strategies.
