Unit 2 — Physical Quantities and Measurement

Complete Study Note

📘 Unit 2 Introduction

Physics is a science that deals with physical phenomena. To study physics properly, we need to describe physical objects and events using quantities that can be measured.

This unit introduces physical quantities, units, measurement, measuring instruments, SI units, prefixes, scalar and vector quantities, unit conversion and measurement safety.

1. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a measurable property of an object, substance or phenomenon.

Examples include:

  • Length
  • Mass
  • Time
  • Temperature
  • Speed
  • Force
  • Energy
  • Volume
  • Density
Remember:

A physical quantity must be measurable and can be expressed using a numerical value and a unit.

2. Measurement

Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown physical quantity with an accepted standard quantity.

For example, when we say that a table is 2 metres long, we are comparing its length with the standard unit metre.

Example:

Length = 5 m

Here 5 is the numerical value and m is the unit.

3. Standard Units

A standard unit is an agreed unit used to measure a physical quantity consistently.

Standard units are important because they allow people in different places to make measurements that can be compared.

Why do we need standard units?
  • To make measurements uniform.
  • To compare measurements.
  • To communicate scientific results clearly.
  • To avoid confusion between different units.

4. International System of Units (SI)

The International System of Units (SI) is the internationally accepted system of measurement used in science.

The SI system contains seven fundamental physical quantities.

Physical Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Electric current ampere A
Temperature kelvin K
Amount of substance mole mol
Luminous intensity candela cd

5. Fundamental Physical Quantities

A fundamental quantity is a physical quantity that is independent and is not defined in terms of other physical quantities.

There are seven SI fundamental quantities.

  1. Length
  2. Mass
  3. Time
  4. Electric current
  5. Thermodynamic temperature
  6. Amount of substance
  7. Luminous intensity

6. Derived Physical Quantities

A derived quantity is obtained from one or more fundamental quantities.

Examples include:

  • Area
  • Volume
  • Speed
  • Acceleration
  • Force
  • Density
  • Pressure
  • Energy
  • Power
Speed:
Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Density:
Density = Mass ÷ Volume

7. Measuring Instruments

Different physical quantities require different measuring instruments.

Quantity Instrument
Length Ruler / metre rule / measuring tape
Mass Balance
Time Clock / stopwatch
Temperature Thermometer

8. Measuring Length

Length is the distance between two points.

The SI unit of length is the metre (m).

Common instruments used to measure length include:

  • Ruler
  • Metre rule
  • Measuring tape
  • Vernier caliper
  • Micrometer screw gauge
Important:

The instrument should be placed correctly and the eye should be positioned properly when reading a scale.

9. Measuring Mass

Mass describes the amount of matter contained in an object.

The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg).

A balance is commonly used to measure mass.

10. Measuring Time

Time tells us the duration or interval between events.

The SI unit of time is the second (s).

A stopwatch can be used to measure short time intervals.

11. Measuring Temperature

Temperature describes how hot or cold a body is.

A thermometer is used to measure temperature.

The SI unit of thermodynamic temperature is the kelvin (K).

12. SI Prefixes

Prefixes are added to units to represent multiples or fractions of a unit.

Prefix Symbol Meaning
Kilo k 1000
Centi c 1/100
Milli m 1/1000
Micro μ 1/1,000,000
Mega M 1,000,000

13. Unit Conversion

Unit conversion means changing a measurement from one unit to another without changing the actual quantity.

1 km = 1000 m

1 m = 100 cm

1 m = 1000 mm

1 kg = 1000 g

1 min = 60 s

1 h = 3600 s
Example 1:

Convert 3 km to metres.

3 × 1000 = 3000 m

Example 2:

Convert 500 cm to metres.

500 ÷ 100 = 5 m

Example 3:

Convert 2 kg to grams.

2 × 1000 = 2000 g

14. Scalar Quantities

A scalar quantity has magnitude only.

Examples:

  • Mass
  • Time
  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Temperature
  • Energy
Remember:

Scalar = magnitude only.

15. Vector Quantities

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Examples:

  • Force
  • Velocity
  • Displacement
  • Acceleration
Remember:

Vector = magnitude + direction.

16. Distance and Displacement

Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object.

Distance is a scalar quantity.

Displacement

Displacement is the straight-line change in position from the starting point to the final point, together with direction.

Displacement is a vector quantity.

17. Speed

Speed tells us how quickly an object covers a distance.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

SI unit of speed = m/s.

Example:

An object travels 100 m in 20 s. Find its speed.

Speed = 100 ÷ 20

Speed = 5 m/s

18. Density

Density is the mass contained in a unit volume of a substance.

Density = Mass ÷ Volume

ρ = m / V

The SI unit of density is kg/m³.

Example:

An object has a mass of 10 kg and a volume of 2 m³.

Density = 10 ÷ 2

Density = 5 kg/m³

19. Accuracy and Precision

Accuracy

Accuracy describes how close a measured value is to the true or accepted value.

Precision

Precision describes how close repeated measurements are to one another.

Simple way to remember:

Accuracy → closeness to the true value.

Precision → closeness of repeated measurements to each other.

20. Measurement and Laboratory Safety

Safety is important whenever measurements or experiments are performed.

  • Follow instructions carefully.
  • Use instruments correctly.
  • Do not taste chemicals.
  • Do not run in the laboratory.
  • Keep the working area clean.
  • Report accidents immediately.
  • Handle instruments carefully.
  • Use appropriate protective equipment.
