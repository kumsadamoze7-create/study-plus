📖 Unit Introduction

Entrepreneurship is an important part of economic activity. It involves identifying opportunities, organizing resources, developing ideas and accepting the possibility of failure in order to create value.

The idea of entrepreneurship has developed over time. The term entrepreneur comes from the French word associated with undertaking or conducting business.

Entrepreneurship is influenced by many areas including economics, history, politics, education, culture, experience and networking.

Entrepreneurship can help transform people from job seekers into job creators.

8.1 Definition of Enterprise, Entrepreneur and Entrepreneurship

8.1.1 Enterprise

An enterprise is a legally recognized business entity that can operate on its own.

An enterprise may have the ability to:

  • Enter contracts
  • Own property
  • Have liabilities
  • Operate bank accounts
  • Produce goods or provide services

8.1.2 Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur is a person who develops a business idea, recognizes an opportunity and accepts the risk of establishing an enterprise to provide goods or services that satisfy customers' needs.

Entrepreneur = Person
A person who identifies opportunities, organizes resources and takes business risks.

8.1.3 Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying opportunities, organizing the resources needed to pursue them and investing resources to create long-term value.

It involves bringing resources together in new ways to establish and operate an enterprise.

Concept Meaning
Enterprise The business organization or legal business entity.
Entrepreneur The person who creates and develops the business idea and takes risk.
Entrepreneurship The process of identifying opportunities and organizing resources to pursue them.

8.1.4 Entrepreneurship and Economic Development

Entrepreneurship can contribute to economic development by:

  • Creating employment
  • Increasing production
  • Encouraging innovation
  • Creating income
  • Using resources productively
  • Creating new goods and services
  • Opening new markets

8.1.5 Ethiopian Examples

Bethlehem Tilahun Alemu

She founded SoleRebels, a shoe business known for using recycled materials including old tires.

Haile Gebrselassie

The Ethiopian athlete also became an entrepreneur and investor, with activities in areas such as automobiles, hospitality, real estate, agriculture and technology.

8.1.6 Five Critical Elements of Entrepreneurship

  1. Ability to identify a potentially profitable opportunity.
  2. Ability to turn the opportunity into a commercial activity through innovation.
  3. Ability to pursue the opportunity over the long term.
  4. Ability to follow a systematic approach.
  5. Acceptance that failure is possible.

8.1.7 History of Entrepreneurship

The meaning of entrepreneur has changed through history.

  • In ancient times, entrepreneurs were associated with people managing large commercial projects using resources provided to them.
  • During the 17th century, entrepreneurs could be people who entered contracts with governments to provide goods or services.
  • In the 18th century, Richard Cantillon emphasized the role of the entrepreneur as a risk-taker.
  • Later economic thinking emphasized organizing and operating an enterprise for personal gain.
  • In the 20th century, the entrepreneur became increasingly associated with innovation and invention.

8.1.8 Social Entrepreneurship

Social entrepreneurship focuses on identifying and developing solutions to social problems while using entrepreneurial ideas and activities.

Social entrepreneurship = Entrepreneurship directed toward creating positive social change.

Social entrepreneurs may create products or services that improve people's lives and address community problems.

🧠 Remember

Enterprise → Business entity
Entrepreneur → Person
Entrepreneurship → Process
Social entrepreneurship → Business ideas used to create positive social impact

8.2 Creativity and Innovation in Solving Local Problems

8.2.1 Creativity

Creativity is the ability to generate useful new ideas, alternatives and possibilities for solving problems and recognizing opportunities.

Creativity involves looking at problems from different perspectives and combining existing ideas in new ways.

Three Important Elements of Creativity

  1. Creativity is an ongoing process.
  2. Creativity produces ideas that may have value.
  3. Creativity can involve recombining existing ideas in unexpected ways.
Example: If a community has a problem with plastic waste, a creative person may think of new ways to reuse the waste instead of throwing it away.

8.2.2 Innovation

Innovation is the implementation of a new idea at an individual, group or organizational level.

Innovation is central to entrepreneurship because it helps turn opportunities and ideas into useful products, services or processes.

Four Types of Innovation

Type Meaning
Invention Creation of a new product, service or process.
Expansion Growth or extension of an existing product, service or process.
Duplication Replication of an existing product, service or process.
Synthesis Combining existing concepts or factors to create a new formulation.

8.2.3 From Creativity to Entrepreneurship

Creativity ↓ New Ideas ↓ Innovation ↓ Solutions to Problems ↓ Business Opportunity ↓ Entrepreneurship

Creativity generates ideas, while innovation applies useful ideas to real problems and opportunities.

New ideas can help businesses become more efficient and gain a competitive advantage.

8.2.4 Solving Local Problems

Entrepreneurship can begin by identifying a problem in the local community and developing a useful solution.

Examples of local problems that can create opportunities:
  • Waste management
  • Transportation problems
  • Access to clean water
  • Food preservation
  • Educational services
  • Agricultural production problems
  • Energy problems
Creativity → Generate ideas

Innovation → Apply ideas

Entrepreneurship → Turn opportunities into economic activity

8.3 Entrepreneurial Attitudes, Behaviour and Mind-set

8.3.1 Entrepreneurial Mindset

An entrepreneurial mindset is a person's way of thinking and attitude toward achieving goals through entrepreneurial activities.

Entrepreneurs are often willing to make decisions, search for customers, take calculated risks and learn from failure.

8.3.2 Key Entrepreneurial Attitudes

1. Passion

Entrepreneurs should have strong interest and enthusiasm for their ideas, goals and businesses.

2. Bravery

Entrepreneurs need courage to make decisions and continue even when failure is possible.

3. Flexibility

Entrepreneurs should be able to change their approach when circumstances change.

4. Strong Work Ethics

Entrepreneurship requires hard work, commitment, discipline and continuous effort.

5. Integrity

Integrity means being honest, trustworthy and responsible in business relationships.

Attitude Meaning
Passion Strong enthusiasm for the business idea and goals.
Bravery Courage to act despite uncertainty and possible failure.
Flexibility Ability to adapt to changing situations.
Strong work ethics Commitment to hard and responsible work.
Integrity Honesty and trustworthiness.

8.3.3 Skills and Attributes

Entrepreneurial skills can be grouped into two major categories:

A. General Management Skills

  • Strategy skills
  • Planning skills
  • Marketing skills
  • Financial skills
  • Project management skills
  • Time management skills

Strategy Skills

The ability to understand the business as a whole, its market, customers and competitors.

Planning Skills

The ability to think about the future and prepare actions needed to achieve business objectives.

Marketing Skills

The ability to understand customer needs and explain how a product or service satisfies those needs.

Financial Skills

The ability to manage money, expenses, cash flow and investment decisions.

Project Management Skills

The ability to organize activities, objectives, time and resources to complete a project.

Time Management Skills

The ability to use time productively and complete important tasks on schedule.

B. People Management Skills

  • Communication skills
  • Leadership skills
  • Motivational skills
  • Delegation skills
  • Negotiation skills

Communication Skills

The ability to express ideas and information clearly through spoken and written communication.

Leadership Skills

The ability to guide and inspire people to work toward business objectives.

Motivational Skills

The ability to encourage people to perform their tasks and understand what motivates them.

Delegation Skills

The ability to assign appropriate tasks to different people according to their skills.

Negotiation Skills

The ability to understand the interests of different parties and work toward acceptable outcomes.

🧠 Entrepreneurial Skills

General Management: Strategy → Planning → Marketing → Finance → Project → Time

People Management: Communication → Leadership → Motivation → Delegation → Negotiation

8.4 Windows of Entrepreneurial Opportunities

8.4.1 Meaning of Entrepreneurial Opportunity

An entrepreneurial opportunity exists when identifiable customer demand can be matched with the possibility of providing the needed product or service.

Not every business idea is automatically a good opportunity. The entrepreneur must study customer needs and investigate whether the idea can become a successful project.

8.4.2 How Opportunities Arise

  • Through deliberate searching
  • Through new technology
  • By chance
  • By recognizing problems
  • By studying customer needs
  • By identifying changes in markets
Problem / Need ↓ Research ↓ Business Idea ↓ Opportunity ↓ Business Activity

8.4.3 Joseph Schumpeter

Joseph Schumpeter emphasized the importance of innovation in entrepreneurship and economic growth.

He described the process through which innovation replaces older ways of doing business as creative destruction.

8.4.4 Methods of Finding New Business Opportunities

  1. Develop a new market for an existing product.
  2. Find a new source of resources that can reduce production cost.
  3. Use existing technology to produce an old product in a new way.
  4. Use existing technology to produce a new product.
  5. Use new technology to produce a new product.

8.4.5 Demand and Supply Opportunities

Entrepreneurial opportunities may arise from changes in demand, changes in supply or technological innovation.

Opportunity Example
Demand opportunity Customers need a product that is not sufficiently available.
Supply opportunity A new resource or production method makes production easier or cheaper.
Technological opportunity New or existing technology is used to create new products or methods.

8.5 Entrepreneurial Success, Teamwork and Diversity

8.5.1 Importance of Teamwork

Even successful leaders usually need a strong team to achieve business objectives.

1. Teamwork Fosters Cooperation

Team members can share ideas, experience and responsibilities. Cooperation helps people work toward a common goal.

2. Collaboration Broadens Perspectives

Different team members may have different experiences and ideas. This can help the business see problems from different perspectives.

3. Teamwork Increases Productivity

Work can be divided according to the knowledge and skills of team members. This can allow tasks to be completed more effectively.

4. Teamwork Provides Learning Opportunities

Team members can learn from one another by sharing knowledge, experience and skills.

5. Teamwork Shapes Organizational Culture

Good teamwork can create trust, cooperation, loyalty and a positive working environment.

8.5.2 Diversity

Diversity means having people with different backgrounds, experiences, ages, abilities, perspectives and other characteristics working together.

A diverse team can provide different ideas and perspectives, which may help with creativity and problem solving.

Example: A business team containing people with different skills may combine their knowledge to develop a better solution to a customer problem.

Benefits of Teamwork and Diversity

  • Better cooperation
  • More ideas
  • Broader perspectives
  • Improved problem solving
  • Higher productivity
  • Learning from others
  • Better working relationships
Teamwork = Cooperation + Shared Ideas + Productivity + Learning

Diversity = Different People + Different Perspectives + Better Ideas

8.6 Finance and Promotion of Entrepreneurship

8.6.1 Why Entrepreneurs Need Finance

Entrepreneurial ventures require money to start, operate, develop products, purchase equipment and expand.

Some financial needs are internal, while others may require external financing.

8.6.2 Budgetary Requirements

1. Permanent Capital

Permanent capital is used to finance the basic establishment, major development and expansion of an enterprise.

It can come from investment by owners, partners or other forms of long-term capital.

2. Working Capital

Working capital is short-term finance used to support normal business operations.

Working capital can be used for:
  • Raw materials
  • Operating expenses
  • Day-to-day business activities

3. Asset Finance

Asset finance is medium- or long-term financing used to purchase physical assets needed by a business.

Examples include:

  • Machinery
  • Equipment
  • Vehicles
  • Buildings
Finance Type Main Purpose
Permanent Capital Start-up, major development and expansion.
Working Capital Short-term operating needs.
Asset Finance Purchase of physical assets.

8.6.3 Sources of Finance

1. Personal Savings

Entrepreneurs can use their own savings as start-up capital.

2. Friends and Relatives

After using personal resources, entrepreneurs may seek support from friends and relatives who are willing to invest.

3. Partners

An entrepreneur can bring in partners who contribute capital and participate in the business.

4. Angel Investors

Angel investors are private individuals who invest their own money in start-up businesses while accepting the associated risk.

5. Venture Capital Firms

Venture capital firms invest in new businesses that have significant growth potential, expecting a return on their investment.

Source Meaning
Personal savings Entrepreneur's own money.
Friends and relatives Investment or financial support from people close to the entrepreneur.
Partners Capital contributed by business partners.
Angel investors Private investors who invest in promising start-ups.
Venture capital Investment by specialized firms in high-growth businesses.
Business Idea ↓ Finance Needed ↓ Personal Savings ↓ Friends / Relatives / Partners ↓ Angel Investors / Venture Capital ↓ Business Development

8.6.4 Financial Management

Obtaining finance is not enough. Entrepreneurs must also manage financial resources carefully.

  • Maintain sufficient cash flow.
  • Control unnecessary expenses.
  • Use capital efficiently.
  • Plan financial needs.
  • Evaluate risks and expected returns.
