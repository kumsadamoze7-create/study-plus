THE AGE OF REVOLUTIONS

1750s – 1815

📖 Unit 9 Introduction

Unit 9 examines the major political, economic and social revolutions that transformed Europe, America and the wider world from the 1750s to 1815.

The period witnessed the development of industrial capitalism, the American War of Independence, the French Revolution, the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte and the Congress of Vienna.

  2. French Revolution
  3. Napoleonic Era

9.1 Industrial Capitalism in Europe

🏭 What is Industrial Capitalism?

Industrial Capitalism:

An economic system based on private ownership, investment of capital, factories, wage labour and large-scale industrial production.

Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the second half of the 18th century and later spread to other parts of Europe and the world.

Why Britain?

  • Availability of coal and iron
  • Capital for investment
  • Growing population
  • Large markets
  • Improved transportation
  • Agricultural changes
  • Political and economic stability

Important Inventions

Invention Importance
Spinning Jenny Increased textile production.
Water Frame Used water power for spinning.
Steam Engine Provided mechanical power for industry and transportation.
Power Loom Mechanized weaving.

James Watt

James Watt improved the steam engine and made it more efficient for industrial use.

Important:

The improved steam engine became one of the most important technologies of the Industrial Revolution.

Economic Effects

  • Growth of factories
  • Mass production
  • Increase in production
  • Expansion of markets
  • Growth of capitalism
  • Development of transportation

Social Effects

Industrialization changed European society dramatically. Large numbers of people moved from rural areas to cities looking for employment.

Two major social classes became increasingly important:

  • Bourgeoisie – factory and capital owners.
  • Proletariat – workers who sold their labour for wages.

Negative Effects

  • Poor working conditions
  • Low wages
  • Child labour
  • Long working hours
  • Unemployment
  • Pollution
  • Poor sanitation
  • Spread of diseases

9.2 French Revolution

🇫🇷 Meaning

The French Revolution was a major political and social revolution that began in France in 1789.

Causes of the French Revolution

1. Social Inequality

French society was divided into three estates.

Estate Main Groups
First Estate Clergy
Second Estate Nobility
Third Estate Common people, bourgeoisie, workers and peasants

The Third Estate represented the majority of the population but carried much of the tax burden.

2. Financial Crisis

France faced serious financial problems because of wars, government spending and debt.

3. Enlightenment Ideas

Thinkers such as Rousseau, Voltaire and Montesquieu influenced people with ideas about liberty, equality, rights and government.

4. Food Crisis

Poor harvests contributed to rising bread prices and hardship among ordinary people.

📅 1789

1789:

The French Revolution began.

Storming of the Bastille

On 14 July 1789, revolutionaries in Paris attacked the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison.

14 July 1789:

The Storming of the Bastille became a powerful symbol of the French Revolution.

Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen

The declaration expressed principles such as liberty, equality and political rights.

Reign of Terror

During the radical phase of the Revolution, the Committee of Public Safety exercised strong authority.

Many people considered enemies of the Revolution were executed.

Maximilien Robespierre

Robespierre became one of the leading figures of the radical phase of the Revolution.

Consequences

  • End of many feudal privileges
  • Growth of ideas of citizenship
  • Spread of liberty and equality ideas
  • Political transformation in France
  • Rise of Napoleon Bonaparte
  • Influence on other revolutionary movements

9.3 The Napoleonic Era

👑 Napoleon Bonaparte

Napoleon Bonaparte was a French military leader who rose to political power after the French Revolution.

Rise to Power

Napoleon became First Consul in 1799 and later became Emperor of the French in 1804.

1799:

Napoleon became First Consul.

1804:

Napoleon became Emperor of France.

Napoleonic Code

Napoleon introduced the Civil Code, commonly known as the Napoleonic Code.

It emphasized equality before the law, property rights and legal reforms.

Napoleon's Expansion

Napoleon conquered or controlled large parts of Europe. His armies spread French political and legal ideas.

Continental System

Napoleon attempted to weaken Britain by restricting European trade with Britain.

Decline of Napoleon

Napoleon faced resistance from European powers and serious military difficulties.

Russian Campaign

Napoleon's invasion of Russia in 1812 ended disastrously. The harsh conditions and Russian resistance severely weakened his army.

Battle of Waterloo

1815:

Napoleon was defeated at the Battle of Waterloo.

After Waterloo, Napoleon was sent into exile on Saint Helena, where he later died.

9.4 American War of Independence

🇺🇸 Background

The thirteen British colonies in North America became increasingly dissatisfied with British policies.

Causes

  • Taxation by Britain
  • Political grievances
  • Demand for representation
  • Economic restrictions
  • Influence of Enlightenment ideas

Boston Tea Party

In 1773, colonists protested British taxation and commercial policies by throwing tea into Boston Harbor.

Declaration of Independence

4 July 1776:

The thirteen colonies declared independence from Britain.

George Washington

George Washington was the commander of the Continental Army and later became the first President of the United States.

End of the War

The American colonies eventually defeated Britain with important foreign assistance, especially from France.

Consequences

  • Creation of the United States of America
  • End of British rule over the thirteen colonies
  • Spread of republican ideas
  • Influence on the French Revolution

9.5 Congress of Vienna

🌍 What was it?

The Congress of Vienna was an international meeting held after the defeat of Napoleon to reorganize Europe.

1814–1815:

Congress of Vienna.

Important Leader

Klemens von Metternich of Austria played a major role in the Congress.

Main Objectives

  • Restore political stability
  • Restore legitimate monarchies
  • Prevent another Napoleon
  • Create a balance of power
  • Redraw European boundaries

Balance of Power

Balance of Power:

A system intended to prevent one country from becoming so powerful that it could dominate Europe.

Consequences

  • European boundaries were reorganized.
  • Conservative governments became stronger.
  • European powers attempted to maintain peace.
  • Nationalist and liberal movements continued to grow.
