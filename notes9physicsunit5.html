📘 Unit 5 Introduction

A machine is a device that helps us perform work more easily. Simple machines are basic mechanical devices that can change the size or direction of a force.

People use simple machines in homes, agriculture, construction, transportation and many other workplaces.

1. What is a Simple Machine?

A simple machine is a basic mechanical device that helps us do work more easily by changing the magnitude or direction of an applied force.

Examples include:
  • Lever
  • Pulley
  • Inclined plane
  • Wheel and axle
  • Screw
  • Wedge

2. Purpose of Simple Machines

Simple machines are used to:

  • Reduce the effort needed to do a task.
  • Change the direction of an applied force.
  • Increase the distance through which a force acts.
  • Make work more convenient.
  • Help lift, cut, move or hold objects.
Important: A machine does not create energy. It makes a task easier by changing the way force is applied.

3. Types of Simple Machines

Simple Machine Example

4. Lever

A lever is a rigid bar that can rotate around a fixed point called the fulcrum.

Important Parts of a Lever

  • Fulcrum: Fixed point around which the lever turns.
  • Load: The resistance or object being moved.
  • Effort: The force applied to move the load.

5. Classes of Levers

First-Class Lever

The fulcrum is located between the effort and the load.

Examples: seesaw, scissors, crowbar.

Second-Class Lever

The load is located between the fulcrum and the effort.

Examples: wheelbarrow, bottle opener, nutcracker.

Third-Class Lever

The effort is located between the fulcrum and the load.

Examples: tweezers, fishing rod, human forearm.

6. Pulley

A pulley is a simple machine consisting of a wheel with a groove around which a rope or cable passes.

Pulleys are commonly used to lift loads.

Fixed Pulley

A fixed pulley is attached to a fixed support. It mainly changes the direction of the effort.

Movable Pulley

A movable pulley moves with the load and can reduce the effort needed to lift the load.

7. Inclined Plane

An inclined plane is a sloping surface used to move an object to a higher or lower level.

Examples:
  • Ramp
  • Sloping road
  • Loading ramp

An inclined plane allows a load to be moved using a smaller force over a longer distance.

8. Wheel and Axle

A wheel and axle consists of a large wheel attached to a smaller cylindrical axle.

Examples include:
  • Door knob
  • Steering wheel
  • Water wheel

9. Wedge

A wedge is a simple machine made of two inclined planes joined together.

It is commonly used for cutting, splitting, separating or holding objects.

Examples:
  • Axe
  • Knife
  • Nail

10. Screw

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.

Screws are used to hold objects together or to lift and move objects.

Examples:
  • Wood screw
  • Bolt
  • Bottle cap

11. Mechanical Advantage

Mechanical Advantage (MA) tells us how much a machine multiplies the applied effort.

MA = Load / Effort

A machine with a mechanical advantage greater than 1 reduces the effort required to move a load.

If the load is 200 N and the effort is 50 N:

MA = 200 / 50
MA = 4

12. Velocity Ratio

Velocity Ratio (VR) is the ratio of the distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load.

VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load
If the effort moves 4 m while the load moves 1 m:

VR = 4 / 1
VR = 4

13. Efficiency of a Machine

Efficiency tells us how effectively a machine converts input work into useful output work.

Efficiency = (Useful output work / Input work) × 100%

For an ideal machine, efficiency is 100%. In real machines, efficiency is less than 100% because of friction and other energy losses.

14. Relationship Between MA, VR and Efficiency

Efficiency = (MA / VR) × 100%
If:
MA = 4
VR = 5

Efficiency = (4 / 5) × 100%
Efficiency = 80%

15. Input and Output

The input is the work or energy supplied to the machine.

The output is the useful work or energy produced by the machine.

Because of friction and other losses:

Output work is normally less than input work.

16. Ideal and Real Machines

Ideal Machine Real Machine
No energy loss Some energy is lost
Efficiency = 100% Efficiency < 100%
No friction Friction exists

17. Example Calculation

A machine lifts a load of 600 N using an effort of 150 N. Find its mechanical advantage.

MA = Load / Effort

MA = 600 / 150

MA = 4

18. Efficiency Example

A machine has a mechanical advantage of 3 and a velocity ratio of 4. Find its efficiency.

Efficiency = (MA / VR) × 100%

= (3 / 4) × 100%

= 75%

19. Simple Machines in Everyday Life

  • A knife is a wedge.
  • A ramp is an inclined plane.
  • A crowbar is a lever.
  • A wheelbarrow is a second-class lever.
  • A door knob is a wheel and axle.
  • A pulley can be used to draw water from a well.
  • A screw is used to fasten materials.

20. Important Formulas

MA = Load / Effort

VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load

Efficiency = (Output work / Input work) × 100%

Efficiency = (MA / VR) × 100%

  • A lever rotates around a fulcrum.
  • Levers are classified into three classes.
  • Pulleys are useful for lifting loads.
  • An inclined plane reduces the effort needed by increasing the distance over which the effort acts.
  • Mechanical Advantage is MA = Load / Effort.
  • Velocity Ratio compares the distance moved by effort and load.
  • Efficiency measures how effectively a machine performs.
  • Real machines have efficiency less than 100%.
