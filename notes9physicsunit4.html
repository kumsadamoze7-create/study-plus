📘 Unit 4 Introduction

In Unit 3, we studied how to describe the motion of objects. In this unit, we study the causes of motion and the effects of forces.

The unit covers force, types of forces, Newton's laws of motion, friction, work, energy and power.

1. Force

A force is a push or pull that can change the motion, direction or shape of an object.

Force is a vector quantity because it has magnitude and direction.

SI unit of force = Newton (N)
Examples of force:
  • Pushing a door
  • Pulling a box
  • Kicking a football
  • Gravity pulling objects downward

2. Effects of Force

A force can:

  • Start the motion of an object.
  • Stop a moving object.
  • Increase speed.
  • Decrease speed.
  • Change the direction of motion.
  • Change the shape of an object.

3. Types of Forces

Contact Forces

Contact forces act when two objects are physically touching.

  • Frictional force
  • Normal force
  • Tension force
  • Applied force

Non-Contact Forces

Non-contact forces act without physical contact.

  • Gravitational force
  • Magnetic force
  • Electric force

4. Mass and Weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object.

The SI unit of mass is kilogram (kg).

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

Weight = mass × gravitational acceleration

W = mg

Weight is measured in Newton (N).

A 5 kg object is on Earth. Taking g = 10 m/s²:

W = mg
W = 5 × 10
W = 50 N

5. Newton's First Law

Newton's First Law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.

This law is also called the law of inertia.

A passenger moves forward when a bus suddenly stops. The passenger's body tends to continue moving forward.

6. Inertia

Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of motion.

The greater the mass of an object, the greater its inertia.

7. Newton's Second Law

Newton's Second Law states that the acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass.

F = ma

Where:

  • F = force in Newtons
  • m = mass in kilograms
  • a = acceleration in m/s²
A 10 kg object accelerates at 2 m/s².

F = ma
F = 10 × 2
F = 20 N

8. Newton's Third Law

Newton's Third Law states that for every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force.

When you push the ground backward while walking, the ground pushes you forward.

9. Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

Balanced forces are forces whose resultant is zero. They do not change the motion of an object.

Unbalanced forces have a non-zero resultant force and can change the motion of an object.

Balanced Unbalanced
Net force = 0 Net force ≠ 0
No acceleration Acceleration occurs

10. Friction

Friction is a force that opposes relative motion between two surfaces in contact.

Advantages of Friction

  • Allows us to walk.
  • Allows vehicles to stop.
  • Helps us hold objects.
  • Allows writing with a pencil.

Disadvantages of Friction

  • Produces heat.
  • Causes wear and tear.
  • Reduces mechanical efficiency.

11. Work

In physics, work is done when a force causes displacement in the direction of the force.

Work = Force × Displacement

W = Fd

The SI unit of work is the Joule (J).

A force of 20 N moves a box through 5 m.

W = Fd
W = 20 × 5
W = 100 J

12. When is Work Zero?

Work is zero when there is no displacement in the direction of the force.

If you push a wall but the wall does not move, the displacement is zero. Therefore:

Work = 0 J

13. Energy

Energy is the capacity or ability to do work.

The SI unit of energy is the Joule (J).

Energy exists in different forms, including kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical and electrical energy.

14. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its motion.

KE = ½mv²

Where:

  • m = mass
  • v = velocity
A 2 kg object moves at 4 m/s.

KE = ½ × 2 × 4²
KE = 16 J
KE = 16 J

15. Potential Energy

Potential energy is stored energy due to an object's position or condition.

Gravitational potential energy depends on height.

PE = mgh

Where:

  • m = mass
  • g = gravitational acceleration
  • h = height
A 2 kg object is raised to a height of 5 m. Take g = 10 m/s².

PE = mgh
PE = 2 × 10 × 5
PE = 100 J

16. Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.

Mechanical Energy = KE + PE

17. Conservation of Energy

The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred or transformed from one form to another.

When a ball falls from a height:

Potential Energy → Kinetic Energy

18. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

Power = Work ÷ Time

P = W/t

The SI unit of power is the Watt (W).

If 100 J of work is done in 5 seconds:

P = 100 ÷ 5
P = 20 W
