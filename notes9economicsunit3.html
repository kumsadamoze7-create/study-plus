📖 Unit Introduction

Economic resources are the inputs used to produce goods and services. Since resources are limited, they must be used carefully and efficiently.

This unit explains the types of economic resources, factor payments, renewable and non-renewable resources, different types of markets, circular flow of economic activities and the importance of land as an economic resource in Ethiopia.

Main idea:
Resources → Production → Markets → Income → Economic Activities

3.1 Types of Resources and Factor Payments

Meaning of Resources

Resources are inputs used in the production process to produce goods and services that satisfy human wants.

Resources = Inputs used to produce goods and services.

Types of Resources

Resources can generally be classified into two broad categories:

  1. Free resources
  2. Economic resources

Free Resources

A free resource is a resource whose available quantity is greater than what people desire to have at zero price.

Free resources are sometimes described as gifts of nature that are available without direct payment in normal circumstances.

Examples may include:
  • Air
  • Sunshine
  • Solar energy
  • Some naturally available water resources

Economic Resources

An economic resource is a scarce resource whose available quantity is less than what people would like to have at zero price.

Because economic resources are scarce, they have alternative uses and require choices to be made.

Examples include:
  • Land
  • Labour
  • Capital
  • Entrepreneurship

Factors of Production

The four major factors of production are:

  1. Land
  2. Labour
  3. Capital
  4. Entrepreneurship

1. Land

In economics, land refers to all natural resources used in the production of goods and services.

It includes not only the physical land surface but also natural resources found in or on the land.

  • Soil
  • Water
  • Forests
  • Minerals
  • Wildlife
  • Natural vegetation
  • Other natural resources
Factor payment for land = Rent

2. Labour

Labour refers to the physical and mental effort of human beings used in the production of goods and services.

Labour includes both skilled and unskilled human work.

Examples:
  • Teacher
  • Doctor
  • Farmer
  • Engineer
  • Driver
  • Construction worker
Factor payment for labour = Wage

3. Capital

Capital refers to produced resources that are used to produce other goods and services.

Capital is not simply money. In economics, capital mainly refers to productive assets such as machinery, tools and buildings.

Examples of Physical Capital

  • Machines
  • Tools
  • Factories
  • Buildings used for production
  • Vehicles used in production
  • Equipment

Human Capital

Human capital refers to the knowledge, skills, education, training and experience embodied in people that increase their productive ability.

A trained doctor can provide medical services more effectively because education and training have increased the doctor's human capital.
Factor payment for capital = Interest

4. Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the ability to organize and combine the other factors of production, make decisions, introduce ideas and take business risks.

An entrepreneur identifies opportunities and organizes resources to produce goods or services.

Functions of an Entrepreneur

  • Organizes factors of production.
  • Makes business decisions.
  • Identifies business opportunities.
  • Introduces innovation.
  • Takes risks.
  • Coordinates production.
Factor payment for entrepreneurship = Profit

Factors and Factor Payments

Factor of Production Meaning Factor Payment
Land Natural resources used in production. Rent
Labour Human physical and mental effort. Wages
Capital Produced resources used for production. Interest
Entrepreneurship Organization, innovation and risk-taking. Profit
Exam shortcut:

Land → Rent
Labour → Wage
Capital → Interest
Entrepreneurship → Profit

3.2 Renewable and Non-renewable Resources

Renewable Resources

Renewable resources are resources that can naturally be replenished or renewed over time, provided they are properly managed.

Examples

  • Solar energy
  • Wind energy
  • Forests
  • Water
  • Soil
Solar energy is renewable because sunlight is continuously available from the sun.

Non-renewable Resources

Non-renewable resources are resources that exist in limited quantities and cannot be replenished within a short human time period once they are used.

Examples

  • Coal
  • Petroleum
  • Natural gas
  • Many minerals

Fossil fuels such as coal, petroleum and natural gas take very long periods to form.

Renewable vs Non-renewable

Renewable Non-renewable
Can be replenished naturally. Cannot be replenished quickly.
Can be available continuously if properly managed. Supply is limited.
Examples: solar, wind and forests. Examples: coal, petroleum and natural gas.

Resource Conservation

Resource conservation means protecting and using resources carefully so that they are not unnecessarily wasted or destroyed.

Why conserve resources?

  • To reduce waste.
  • To protect the environment.
  • To maintain resources for future generations.
  • To support sustainable development.
  • To prevent unnecessary depletion.

Reduce, Reuse and Recycle

R Meaning
Reduce Use fewer resources and avoid unnecessary consumption.
Reuse Use an item again instead of throwing it away.
Recycle Process used materials so they can be used again.

Pollution and Resource Use

Pollution occurs when harmful substances or activities contaminate the environment and negatively affect living things and natural resources.

Examples of Pollution

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution
Overuse and careless use of natural resources can cause environmental degradation and reduce the availability and quality of resources.

3.3 Types of Markets

Meaning of Market

A market is an institution or arrangement where buyers and sellers interact to facilitate the exchange of goods, services or resources.

A market does not necessarily need to be a physical place. It can also operate digitally or electronically.

Examples:
  • Local shops
  • Open markets
  • Online markets
  • Labour markets
  • Financial markets

Major Types of Markets in this Unit

  1. Goods and services market
  2. Labour market
  3. Financial market

1. Goods and Services Market

The goods and services market is where goods and services are bought and sold.

Households purchase goods and services from firms, while firms receive revenue from selling them.

Examples:
  • Food market
  • Clothing market
  • Transportation services
  • Education services
  • Mobile phone market

2. Labour Market

The labour market is where workers supply labour and employers demand labour.

Households generally supply labour, while firms and other organizations demand labour.

Price of labour = Wage

3. Financial Market

A financial market is a market where financial assets and funds are exchanged.

It helps channel savings toward investment and provides financial resources to businesses and other borrowers.

Examples

  • Stock market
  • Bond market
  • Share market

Comparison of Markets

Market What is exchanged? Main participants
Goods and Services Goods and services Households and firms
Labour Human labour Workers and employers
Financial Financial assets and funds Savers, investors, firms and financial institutions

3.4 Circular Flow of Economic Activities

Meaning of Circular Flow

The circular flow of economic activities is a simplified model showing how economic resources, goods, services and money move among different sectors of an economy.

The main sectors studied are households, business firms and government.

Two-Sector Model

The two-sector model contains two economic agents:

  1. Households
  2. Business firms

There is no government sector in the simple two-sector model.

Households in the Two-Sector Model

  • Own factors of production.
  • Supply resources to firms.
  • Receive income.
  • Buy goods and services.
  • Consume goods and services.

Firms in the Two-Sector Model

  • Demand factors of production.
  • Produce goods and services.
  • Pay income to households.
  • Sell goods and services.

Real Flow

Real flow refers to the movement of factors of production from households to firms and goods and services from firms to households.

HOUSEHOLDS
↓ Factors of Production
BUSINESS FIRMS
↓ Goods and Services
HOUSEHOLDS

Money Flow

Money flow refers to the movement of money payments in the opposite direction to real flows.

HOUSEHOLDS
↑ Income: Wages, Rent, Interest, Profit
BUSINESS FIRMS
↑ Consumer Spending
HOUSEHOLDS

Real Flow vs Money Flow

Real Flow Money Flow
Factors move from households to firms. Income moves from firms to households.
Goods and services move from firms to households. Consumer expenditure moves from households to firms.
Involves resources, goods and services. Involves monetary payments.

Three-Sector Model

The three-sector model adds the government to households and business firms.

The three sectors are:

  1. Households
  2. Business firms
  3. Government

Government Revenue

Government obtains revenue mainly through taxes and other sources.

Government Spending

Government spends money on public goods and services and may provide subsidies and transfer payments.

  • Government purchases goods and services from firms.
  • Government pays wages to public employees.
  • Government provides subsidies to some firms.
  • Government provides transfer payments to households.
  • Government provides public services.
HOUSEHOLDS ⇄ BUSINESS FIRMS

HOUSEHOLDS → TAXES → GOVERNMENT
FIRMS → TAXES → GOVERNMENT

GOVERNMENT → PUBLIC SERVICES / TRANSFERS / SUBSIDIES → HOUSEHOLDS & FIRMS

Saving and Investment

Households may save part of their income. Savings can enter the financial system and help provide funds for investment by firms.

Simple relationship:

Household Saving → Financial Market → Investment Funds → Firms

Subsidies

A subsidy is financial assistance provided by the government to support certain producers, consumers or activities.

Transfer Payments

Transfer payments are payments made by government to individuals or households without a direct exchange of currently produced goods or services.

Importance of Circular Flow

  • Shows interaction among economic agents.
  • Shows movement of resources.
  • Shows movement of goods and services.
  • Shows movement of money.
  • Helps explain national economic activity.
  • Shows the relationship between income and expenditure.

3.5 Land as an Economic Resource in Ethiopia

Importance of Land

Land is one of the most important natural resources in Ethiopia. It is particularly important because agriculture plays a major role in the Ethiopian economy and depends heavily on land.

Uses of Land

  • Agricultural production
  • Construction
  • Settlement
  • Forestry
  • Mining
  • Transport infrastructure
  • Animal grazing

Land and Agriculture

The productivity of agricultural land depends on factors such as soil quality, climate, rainfall, altitude, water availability and farming practices.

Soil

Soil provides the physical medium in which many crops grow. Different soils have different characteristics and levels of fertility.

Climate

Climate affects agricultural production through temperature, rainfall and length of growing seasons.

Altitude and Agro-Ecological Conditions

Differences in altitude create different agro-ecological conditions in Ethiopia. These differences influence which crops and agricultural activities are suitable in different areas.

Land Ownership in Ethiopia

The nature of land ownership and land-use rights in Ethiopia has changed over different historical periods.

Before 1974

Before the 1974 revolution, Ethiopia had different land tenure arrangements, including systems associated with landlords, peasants and traditional landholding arrangements.

1975–1991

After the 1975 land reform, rural land was nationalized and major changes were made to landholding and land-use arrangements.

Since 1991

Since 1991, land remains under public ownership under the constitutional framework, while farmers and other users may have use rights according to the law.

Exam point:
When discussing Ethiopian land, distinguish between land ownership and land-use rights.

Problems Affecting Land Resources

  • Soil erosion
  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Land degradation
  • Loss of soil fertility
  • Pollution
  • Unsustainable farming practices

Land Conservation

Land conservation involves using land in ways that protect soil, water, vegetation and productive capacity for present and future generations.

  • Terracing
  • Tree planting
  • Crop rotation
  • Proper grazing management
  • Soil conservation
  • Water conservation
  • Improved farming practices
