6.1 Definition of Money

Meaning of Money

Money is anything that is generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for the repayment of debts.

Money = Anything generally accepted in payment for goods, services and debts.

Why Do We Need Money?

Money makes transactions easier. Without money, people would have to exchange goods directly with one another.

For example:

A farmer has wheat and wants shoes.

Instead of searching for a shoemaker who wants wheat, the farmer can sell the wheat for money and use the money to buy shoes.

Characteristics of Good Money

For something to function effectively as money, it should have several important characteristics.

1. Standardization

Money should be standardized so that units of the same denomination have the same value and appearance.

2. Acceptability

Money should be widely accepted by people when buying goods and services.

3. Divisibility

Money should be divisible into smaller units so that different amounts can be paid.

4. Portability

Money should be easy to carry from one place to another.

5. Durability

Money should be able to remain usable for a reasonable period without easily losing its usefulness.

Characteristic Meaning
Standardization Uniform and standardized units
Acceptability People accept it as payment
Divisibility Can be divided into smaller units
Portability Easy to carry
Durability Can remain usable for a long time
🧠 Remember:

Good Money =

Standardization + Acceptability + Divisibility + Portability + Durability

6.2 Evolution of Money

The Barter System

Barter is the direct exchange of one good or service for another without using money.

GOOD A ↓ GOOD B

No money involved.

Example of Barter

A farmer gives wheat to a person in exchange for shoes.

Wheat ↔ Shoes

Limitations of Barter

Although barter can work in simple situations, it has several important limitations.

  • Double coincidence of wants.
  • Difficulty in measuring value.
  • Difficulty in storing wealth.
  • Difficulty in making deferred payments.
  • Difficulty in transporting some goods.
  • Difficulty in dividing some goods.

Double Coincidence of Wants

Double coincidence of wants means that both parties in an exchange must want what the other person has.

A farmer who wants shoes must find a shoemaker who wants wheat. This makes barter difficult.

Commodity Money

Commodity money refers to goods that were used as money because they had value and were generally accepted in exchange.

Examples include:

  • Salt
  • Cattle
  • Grain
  • Precious metals
  • Beads

Money and Precious Metals

Gold, silver and other precious metals were historically used as money because they were valuable, durable and relatively easy to store.

Paper Money

Paper money developed as a more convenient form of money than carrying heavy commodities or metals.

Fiat Money

Fiat money is money that has value mainly because it is declared legal tender and is generally accepted by people, rather than because the material itself has a high commodity value.

Evolution of Money in Ethiopia

Different forms of money have been used in Ethiopia throughout history.

Among the commodities used as money were salt bars known as Amole, cloth, beads and other goods.

Amole: Salt bars were historically an important medium of exchange in Ethiopia.

Maria Theresa Thaler

The Maria Theresa Thaler became an important form of money used in Ethiopia and other parts of the region.

The Ethiopian Birr

The Birr developed as the major national currency of Ethiopia and continues to serve as the country's monetary unit.

🧠 Evolution Memory:

Barter ↓ Commodity Money ↓ Metal Money ↓ Paper Money ↓ Modern Money ↓ Electronic Money

6.3 Functions of Money

Money performs several important functions in an economy.

1. Medium of Exchange

Money serves as a medium of exchange because it is accepted as a means of payment for goods and services.

When you buy a book for 200 Birr, you give money to the seller and receive the book.

2. Unit of Account

Money provides a common unit for measuring and comparing the values of goods and services.

If:
Book = 200 Birr
Pen = 20 Birr
Bag = 1,000 Birr

Their values can easily be compared because they are expressed in the same unit.

3. Store of Value

Money allows people to store purchasing power for future use.

A person can save money today and use it to purchase goods and services in the future.

4. Standard of Deferred Payment

Money provides a standard for payments that are made in the future.

If a person borrows 10,000 Birr and agrees to repay it later, the debt can be stated in monetary terms.
Function Meaning
Medium of Exchange Used to buy and sell goods and services
Unit of Account Measures and compares value
Store of Value Stores purchasing power for future use
Standard of Deferred Payment Used for future debt payments
🧠 Four Functions:

M - U - S - S

M = Medium of Exchange
U = Unit of Account
S = Store of Value
S = Standard of Deferred Payment

6.4 Demand and Supply of Money

Demand for Money

Demand for money means the amount of money that people want to hold rather than spend immediately.

John Maynard Keynes explained three major motives for holding money.

1. Transaction Motive

People hold money to make everyday transactions and payments.

Buying:
  • Food
  • Clothes
  • Transportation
  • School materials

2. Precautionary Motive

People hold money as a precaution against unexpected future needs.

Examples:
Medical emergencies, accidents or unexpected expenses.

3. Speculative Motive

People may hold money in liquid form to take advantage of expected future changes in financial markets.

Don't confuse:

Precautionary → Emergency / unexpected needs
Speculative → Future financial opportunities

Money Supply

Money supply refers to the total amount of money available to and held by the public at a particular time.

Money Supply = Currency with the Public + Deposit Money with the Public

Currency with the Public

This includes notes and coins held by people outside banks.

Deposit Money

Deposit money refers to money held in bank accounts that can be used for payments.

Factors Affecting Demand for Money

  • Income level
  • Price level
  • Interest rate
  • Volume of transactions
  • Expectations about the future
  • Uncertainty

Factors Affecting Money Supply

  • Central bank monetary policy
  • Banking system activities
  • Currency issued
  • Credit creation
  • Economic conditions

Role of the National Bank of Ethiopia

The National Bank of Ethiopia has important responsibilities related to the country's monetary and financial system, including the issuance and management of currency and monetary policy.

6.5 Money and Electronic Money

Meaning of Electronic Money

Electronic money, commonly called e-money, is monetary value stored and transferred electronically and used to make payments without necessarily using physical cash.

Examples

  • Electronic bank transfers
  • Debit cards
  • Credit cards
  • ATM transactions
  • Mobile-based electronic payments
  • Online banking

Automatic Teller Machine (ATM)

An ATM is an electronic banking machine that allows customers to perform certain banking transactions without directly visiting a bank employee.

ATM can be used for:
  • Withdrawing money
  • Checking account balance
  • Some account services

Electronic Fund Transfer

Electronic fund transfer means transferring money electronically from one account or person to another.

Sender ↓ Electronic Payment System ↓ Receiver

Advantages of Electronic Money

  • Fast transactions
  • Convenient payments
  • Less need to carry physical cash
  • Can support remote transactions
  • Can make record keeping easier
  • Can save time

Risks and Problems of Electronic Money

  • Fraud
  • Cybercrime
  • Loss of passwords or PINs
  • Technical failures
  • Network problems
  • Privacy concerns
  • Unauthorized transactions
Safety:

Never share your PIN, password or verification code with another person.
