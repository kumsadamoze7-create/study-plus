Unit 3 — Motion

Complete Study Note

📘 Unit 3 Introduction

Motion is one of the most important topics in physics. We observe objects moving around us every day. Cars move along roads, people walk, balls are thrown, and planets move around the Sun.

In this unit, we study how to describe motion using distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, graphs and equations of motion.

1. Rest and Motion

Rest

An object is said to be at rest if its position does not change with respect to a reference point over time.

Motion

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to a reference point over time.

Example:

A car moving along a road is in motion relative to a person standing beside the road.

2. Reference Point

A reference point is a fixed point used to determine whether an object has changed its position.

Motion and rest are relative concepts because an object can be at rest relative to one reference point and moving relative to another.

A passenger sitting inside a moving bus is at rest relative to another passenger, but is moving relative to a person standing beside the road.

3. Position

The position of an object tells us where the object is located relative to a reference point.

If the position of an object changes with time, the object is moving.

4. Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object.

Distance is a scalar quantity.

The SI unit of distance is the metre (m).

Example:

A student walks 300 m from home to school and then walks 100 m back toward home.

Total distance = 300 + 100 = 400 m

5. Displacement

Displacement is the change in position of an object from its initial position to its final position, including direction.

Displacement is a vector quantity.

Displacement = Final position − Initial position

If a person moves 10 m east from a starting point, the displacement is:

10 m east

Important:

Distance is always positive, while displacement can be positive, negative, or zero depending on the chosen direction.

6. Distance vs Displacement

Distance Displacement
Scalar quantity Vector quantity
Total path travelled Change from initial to final position
Has magnitude only Has magnitude and direction
Always positive Can be zero

7. Speed

Speed tells us how fast an object covers distance.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

v = d / t

The SI unit of speed is metre per second (m/s).

Example:

A car travels 200 m in 10 s. Find its speed.

Speed = 200 ÷ 10

Speed = 20 m/s

8. Average Speed

Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.

Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

An object travels 100 m in 5 s and then another 100 m in 10 s.

Total distance = 200 m

Total time = 15 s

Average speed = 200 ÷ 15

Average speed ≈ 13.33 m/s

9. Velocity

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with time.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

v = Δx / t

Velocity is a vector quantity, so it has both magnitude and direction.

A car travels 100 m east in 20 s.

Velocity = 100 ÷ 20

Velocity = 5 m/s east

10. Speed vs Velocity

Speed Velocity
Scalar Vector
Uses distance Uses displacement
Direction is not required Direction is required
Example: 20 m/s Example: 20 m/s east

11. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes with time.

Acceleration = Change in Velocity ÷ Time

a = (v - u) / t

Where:

  • a = acceleration
  • v = final velocity
  • u = initial velocity
  • t = time

The SI unit of acceleration is m/s².

12. Calculating Acceleration

Example:

A car increases its velocity from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 seconds. Find its acceleration.

a = (v - u) ÷ t

a = (30 - 10) ÷ 5

a = 20 ÷ 5

a = 4 m/s²

13. Deceleration

Deceleration means a decrease in velocity with time. It is sometimes called negative acceleration.

If a car slows from 20 m/s to 5 m/s, its velocity is decreasing.

14. Uniform Motion

An object is in uniform motion when it travels equal distances in equal intervals of time.

A car travelling at a constant speed of 10 m/s covers 10 m every second.

15. Non-Uniform Motion

An object is in non-uniform motion when it travels unequal distances in equal intervals of time or its direction changes.

Examples include:

  • A car speeding up.
  • A car slowing down.
  • A vehicle moving around a curved road.

16. Motion Graphs

Graphs are useful tools for representing motion. The most common graphs are:

  • Distance-time graph
  • Displacement-time graph
  • Velocity-time graph

17. Distance-Time Graph

A distance-time graph shows how the distance travelled changes with time.

Time is usually placed on the horizontal axis and distance on the vertical axis.

Key idea:

The slope of a distance-time graph represents speed.

Speed = Slope of distance-time graph

Speed = Change in distance ÷ Change in time

18. Slope of a Graph

The slope of a straight line can be calculated using:

Slope = Rise ÷ Run

Slope = Change in vertical quantity ÷ Change in horizontal quantity

19. Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time.

Time is placed on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis.

Important:

The slope of a velocity-time graph represents acceleration.

Acceleration = Change in velocity ÷ Change in time

20. Area Under Velocity-Time Graph

The area under a velocity-time graph represents the displacement of an object.

Displacement = Area under velocity-time graph

21. Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

For motion with constant acceleration, the following equations are useful.

v = u + at

s = ut + ½at²

v² = u² + 2as

s = ½(u + v)t

Where:

  • u = initial velocity
  • v = final velocity
  • a = acceleration
  • t = time
  • s = displacement

22. Equation of Motion Example

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity.

Given:

  • u = 0 m/s
  • a = 2 m/s²
  • t = 5 s

Use:

v = u + at

v = 0 + (2 × 5)

v = 10 m/s

23. Free Fall

An object is in free fall when it moves under the effect of gravity alone, ignoring air resistance.

Near the Earth's surface, the acceleration due to gravity is approximately:

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

In many school calculations, g = 10 m/s² may be used as an approximation.

📚 Unit 3 Summary

  • Motion is a change in position with time.
  • Rest and motion depend on the reference point.
  • Distance is a scalar quantity.
  • Displacement is a vector quantity.
  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time.
  • Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time.
  • Acceleration = Change in velocity ÷ Time.
  • Uniform motion means equal distances in equal times.
  • Non-uniform motion involves changing speed or direction.
  • The slope of a distance-time graph gives speed.
  • The slope of a velocity-time graph gives acceleration.
  • The area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.
  • For constant acceleration, equations of motion can be used.
  • Acceleration due to gravity is approximately 9.8 m/s².
