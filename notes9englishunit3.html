🇬🇧 Grade 9 English

Unit 3 — Comparison

Learn • Understand • Practice • Prepare

📚 1. What is Comparison?

Comparison is used to compare people, animals, objects, places, actions, qualities, quantities, or situations.

We can compare two things or more than two things. Comparison can show that something is equal, greater, smaller, better, worse, faster, slower, more, or less.

Examples:

Abebe is tall.

Abebe is taller than Bekele.

Abebe is the tallest student in the class.

📊 2. Degrees of Comparison

There are three main degrees of comparison:

Degree Use Example
Positive No comparison or basic quality Abebe is tall.
Comparative Compare two Abebe is taller than Bekele.
Superlative Compare three or more Abebe is the tallest student.
🔥 Exam Trick:
  • Two things → Comparative
  • Three or more → Superlative
  • Basic description → Positive

📝 3. Comparison with Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns. Many comparisons are made by changing the adjective.

Short Adjectives

Positive → adjective Comparative → adjective + er Superlative → the + adjective + est
Positive Comparative Superlative
tall taller the tallest
short shorter the shortest
fast faster the fastest
small smaller the smallest

John is taller than Peter.

John is the tallest boy in the class.

🔤 4. Adjectives Ending in -e

When a short adjective ends in e, add only r for comparative and st for superlative.

Positive Comparative Superlative
large larger largest
nice nicer nicest
brave braver bravest
❌ largeer
✅ larger

🔄 5. Adjectives Ending in Consonant + Y

If an adjective ends with consonant + y, change y → i before adding er or est.

Positive Comparative Superlative
happy happier happiest
easy easier easiest
busy busier busiest
lazy lazier laziest
❌ happyer
✅ happier

🔁 6. CVC Rule

Some short adjectives have a consonant-vowel-consonant pattern. The final consonant is doubled before adding er or est.

CVC = Consonant + Vowel + Consonant

Positive Comparative Superlative
big bigger biggest
hot hotter hottest
thin thinner thinnest
sad sadder saddest

📖 7. Long Adjectives

Long adjectives usually use more and most.

Comparative: more + adjective + than

Superlative: the most + adjective
Positive Comparative Superlative
beautiful more beautiful the most beautiful
expensive more expensive the most expensive
intelligent more intelligent the most intelligent
difficult more difficult the most difficult

⚠️ 8. Irregular Adjectives

Some adjectives do not follow the normal er/est or more/most rules. They must be learned separately.

Positive Comparative Superlative
good better the best
bad worse the worst
far farther/further farthest/furthest
little less least
many more most
much more most

⚖️ 9. Equal Comparison

When two people or things have the same degree of a quality, we use:

as + adjective + as

Hana is as tall as Sara.

This book is as interesting as that book.

Negative Equal Comparison

not as + adjective + as

Hana is not as tall as Sara.

🔎 10. Comparative + Than

Comparative forms are commonly followed by than when two things are directly compared.

John is taller than Peter.

This car is more expensive than that car.

❌ John is taller as Peter.
❌ John is more tall than Peter.
✅ John is taller than Peter.

🏃 11. Comparison with Adverbs

Adverbs describe how, when, where, or to what degree an action takes place.

Examples: quickly, slowly, carefully, beautifully, well, badly

Short Adverbs

Positive Comparative Superlative
fast faster fastest
hard harder hardest
early earlier earliest

Dawit runs faster than Sami.

Abebe runs the fastest.

📝 12. Adverbs Ending in -ly

Many adverbs ending in -ly use more and most.

Positive Comparative Superlative
carefully more carefully most carefully
slowly more slowly most slowly
beautifully more beautifully most beautifully

She sings more beautifully than her sister.

⚠️ 13. Irregular Adverbs

Adverb Comparative Superlative
well better best
badly worse worst
little less least
much more most
far farther/further farthest/furthest

She speaks English better than me.

⏰ 14. Comparison with Tenses

Comparison is not itself a tense. However, comparison can be used inside different tenses.

Present Simple

John is taller than Peter.

Past Simple

John was taller than Peter when they were young.

Present Perfect

John has become taller than Peter.

Future

John will probably be taller than Peter next year.

🔥 Important:

The tense changes the verb, while the comparison changes the adjective or adverb.

🧠 15. Comparison with Modal Verbs

Common modal verbs include:

can, could, may, might, must, should, would, shall, will

Modal + Base Verb (V1)

John can run faster than Peter.

She should study more carefully.

You must work harder.

❌ He can runs faster.
✅ He can run faster.

❌ She should studied harder.
✅ She should study harder.

💪 16. Much / Far / A Lot + Comparative

These words can strengthen a comparative adjective or adverb.

He is much taller than me.

This book is far more interesting than that one.

The new car is a lot more expensive.

❌ much tallest
❌ much more taller
✅ much taller
✅ much more interesting

➕ 17. Even + Comparative

Even can emphasize a comparative form.

This problem is even harder than the previous one.

The second exam was even more difficult.

📈 18. Comparative + and + Comparative

This structure describes gradual change.

comparative + and + comparative

The weather is getting colder and colder.

He is becoming more and more confident.

English is becoming more and more important.

🔗 19. The + Comparative, The + Comparative

This structure shows that two changes are related.

The + comparative..., the + comparative...

The more you study, the more you learn.

The harder you work, the better you become.

The faster you run, the more tired you become.

🔥 This structure is very important for examinations.

📉 20. Less / Least

Less and least express a lower degree.

This book is less expensive than that one.

This is the least expensive book.

🔢 21. Fewer vs Less

This is an important examination point.

Fewer → Countable Nouns

fewer students, fewer books, fewer cars

Less → Uncountable Nouns

less water, less money, less time

🔥 Exam Trick:

Can you count it? → FEWER

Cannot count it directly? → LESS

🔢 22. Number vs Amount

Number → Countable

a large number of students

a small number of books

Amount → Uncountable

a large amount of water

a small amount of money

🏆 23. One of the + Superlative

When someone or something belongs to a group containing the highest-ranking members, we use:

one of the + superlative + plural noun

He is one of the best students in the school.

❌ one of the best student
✅ one of the best students

🏅 24. By Far + Superlative

By far is used to strongly emphasize a superlative.

He is by far the best player.

This is by far the most difficult question.

⚖️ 25. Too / Enough

Too + Adjective

Too means more than necessary or desirable.

The water is too hot to drink.

Adjective + Enough

Enough means sufficient.

The water is cool enough to drink.

Enough + Noun

We have enough money.

❌ enough beautiful
✅ beautiful enough

🌟 26. So / Such

So + Adjective / Adverb

She is so beautiful.

He runs so quickly.

Such + Noun Phrase

She is such a beautiful girl.

It was such a difficult exam.

🔎 27. Comparative Without Than

A comparative does not always need the word "than". Sometimes the context already makes the comparison clear.

Which is better?

I need a bigger one.

Try to work harder.

👤 28. Pronouns After Than

Formal English can use a complete clause after than. Informal English commonly uses an object pronoun.

She is taller than I am.

She is taller than me.

In examinations, look carefully at the complete sentence and the intended grammatical structure.

🧠 29. Modal + Comparative Adverb

Modal + V1 + Comparative Adverb

You should drive more carefully than before.

He can run faster than his brother.

We must work harder.

🔥 30. Major Examination Tricks

  • than → usually look for comparative.
  • the + -est / the most → superlative.
  • as ... as → equal comparison.
  • not as ... as → unequal comparison.
  • one of the → superlative + plural noun.
  • the more..., the more... → correlative comparative.
  • -ly adverb → usually more/most.
  • Modal → modal + V1.
  • much/far/a lot/even → usually before comparative.
  • fewer → countable nouns.
  • less → uncountable nouns.

📌 31. Complete Comparison Formula Sheet

Positive: Subject + be + adjective

Comparative: Subject + be + comparative + than + object

Superlative: Subject + be + the + superlative + noun

Equal: as + adjective + as

Negative equal: not as + adjective + as

Short adjective: adjective + er

Short adjective superlative: the + adjective + est

Long adjective: more + adjective

Long adjective superlative: the most + adjective

Lower degree: less + adjective

Lowest degree: the least + adjective

Progressive: comparative + and + comparative

Correlative: the + comparative..., the + comparative...

Modal: modal + V1

🎯 Final Summary

Comparison is much more than simply adding -er or using more. You must understand the type of adjective or adverb, the number of things being compared, the tense of the sentence, modal verb rules, countable and uncountable nouns, equal comparison, irregular forms, and special comparison structures.

For examinations, always look for clue words such as than, the, as...as, one of the, much, far, less, fewer, more and more, the more...the more.

🔥 Remember:

2 things → Comparative

3 or more → Superlative

Equal → as ... as

Countable → fewer

Uncountable → less

Modal → V1
