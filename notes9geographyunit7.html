🌍 Grade 9 Geography

Unit 7

Contemporary Geographic Issues and Public Concerns in Ethiopia

📚 Unit Introduction

Ethiopia faces different geographic and environmental issues that affect people, natural resources and economic development.

Understanding these problems helps communities, government and individuals take appropriate actions to protect natural resources and improve the quality of life.

Unit 7 focuses mainly on:
  • Contemporary geographic issues
  • Natural resource degradation
  • Land degradation
  • Deforestation
  • Soil erosion
  • Desertification
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Climate-related problems
  • Environmental protection
  • Ethiopian Green Legacy Movement

7.1 Contemporary Geographic Issues

Contemporary geographic issues are current problems and concerns related to people, places, resources and the natural environment.

Geographic issues can be environmental, economic, social or related to the interaction between people and their environment.

Examples

  • Land degradation
  • Deforestation
  • Soil erosion
  • Water scarcity
  • Climate change
  • Desertification
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Urban environmental problems
  • Pollution
  • Population pressure

7.2 Natural Resources

Natural resources are materials and features obtained from nature that are useful to humans.

Examples

  • Land
  • Water
  • Forests
  • Wildlife
  • Minerals
  • Soil
  • Fisheries
  • Energy resources

Importance of Natural Resources

  • Provide food.
  • Provide water.
  • Provide raw materials.
  • Support agriculture.
  • Support industries.
  • Create employment.
  • Support tourism.
  • Maintain ecosystems.

7.3 Natural Resource Degradation

Natural resource degradation is the decline in the quality, quantity or productivity of natural resources as a result of natural processes and, especially, human activities.

Major Causes

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Overcultivation
  • Soil erosion
  • Mining
  • Pollution
  • Rapid population growth
  • Improper land use
  • Climate variability and change
Important:

When natural resources are degraded, agricultural productivity, biodiversity, livelihoods and economic development can be affected.

7.4 Land Degradation

Land degradation is the decline in the quality and productive capacity of land.

Causes

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Overcultivation
  • Soil erosion
  • Unsustainable farming
  • Mining
  • Improper irrigation
  • Urban expansion

Effects

  • Reduced agricultural productivity
  • Loss of soil fertility
  • Food insecurity
  • Loss of vegetation
  • Increased erosion
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Reduced water quality

Control Measures

  • Terracing
  • Contour farming
  • Afforestation
  • Agroforestry
  • Crop rotation
  • Controlled grazing
  • Soil conservation
  • Proper land-use planning

7.5 Soil Erosion

Soil erosion is the removal and transport of soil particles by agents such as water and wind.

Major Agents

  • Running water
  • Wind
  • Human activities that expose soil

Factors Increasing Soil Erosion

  • Deforestation
  • Cultivation on steep slopes
  • Overgrazing
  • Heavy rainfall
  • Strong winds
  • Lack of vegetation cover

Effects

  • Loss of fertile topsoil
  • Reduced crop production
  • Siltation of rivers and reservoirs
  • Land degradation
  • Water pollution

Control

  • Terracing
  • Contour ploughing
  • Planting trees
  • Grass strips
  • Mulching
  • Maintaining vegetation cover
🧠 Remember:

Vegetation cover protects soil from direct impact of rainfall and reduces runoff.

7.6 Deforestation

Deforestation is the permanent or long-term removal of forest vegetation and conversion of forest land to other uses.

Causes

  • Expansion of agriculture
  • Fuelwood collection
  • Charcoal production
  • Settlement expansion
  • Construction
  • Commercial logging
  • Infrastructure development

Effects

  • Soil erosion
  • Loss of wildlife habitat
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Reduced carbon storage
  • Changes in local water cycles
  • Land degradation
  • Reduced availability of forest products

Solutions

  • Afforestation
  • Reforestation
  • Agroforestry
  • Forest protection
  • Alternative energy sources
  • Community participation
  • Proper land-use planning

7.7 Desertification

Desertification is land degradation in dryland areas resulting from climatic variations and human activities.

Causes

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Overcultivation
  • Unsustainable land use
  • Drought
  • Climate variability
  • Population pressure

Effects

  • Loss of vegetation
  • Reduced agricultural productivity
  • Food insecurity
  • Water scarcity
  • Migration
  • Loss of biodiversity

Control Measures

  • Tree planting
  • Water conservation
  • Controlled grazing
  • Soil conservation
  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Restoration of degraded land

7.8 Loss of Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms, including diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.

Causes of Biodiversity Loss

  • Habitat destruction
  • Deforestation
  • Pollution
  • Overexploitation
  • Illegal hunting
  • Climate change
  • Invasive species

Importance of Biodiversity

  • Maintains ecosystem functions.
  • Provides food.
  • Provides medicinal resources.
  • Supports tourism.
  • Supports agriculture.
  • Provides genetic resources.
  • Supports ecological balance.

Conservation Methods

  • National parks
  • Wildlife sanctuaries
  • Community conservation
  • Habitat protection
  • Reforestation
  • Anti-poaching measures

7.9 Water Resource Problems

Water is essential for human life, agriculture, industry, energy production and ecosystems.

Major Problems

  • Water scarcity
  • Water pollution
  • Flooding
  • Drought
  • Overuse of water resources
  • Watershed degradation

Causes of Water Pollution

  • Industrial waste
  • Domestic wastewater
  • Agricultural chemicals
  • Solid waste
  • Mining activities
  • Soil erosion and sedimentation

Solutions

  • Protect watersheds.
  • Treat wastewater.
  • Reduce pollution.
  • Use water efficiently.
  • Protect rivers and lakes.
  • Improve sanitation.

7.10 Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperature, precipitation and other characteristics of the climate system.

Major Human Causes

  • Burning fossil fuels
  • Deforestation
  • Industrial activities
  • Transport emissions
  • Some agricultural activities

Possible Effects in Ethiopia

  • Changes in rainfall patterns
  • More frequent or severe droughts in some areas
  • Flood risks in some areas
  • Pressure on water resources
  • Effects on agricultural production
  • Impacts on ecosystems

Adaptation Measures

  • Climate-smart agriculture
  • Water harvesting
  • Drought-resistant crops
  • Early warning systems
  • Disaster preparedness
  • Watershed management

Mitigation Measures

  • Afforestation
  • Renewable energy
  • Energy efficiency
  • Reducing deforestation
  • Sustainable transport

7.11 Environmental Pollution

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment in amounts that cause harmful effects.

Types

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution

Sources

  • Industries
  • Vehicles
  • Waste disposal
  • Agricultural chemicals
  • Construction
  • Mining

Control

  • Proper waste management
  • Pollution regulations
  • Cleaner technologies
  • Public awareness
  • Recycling
  • Wastewater treatment

7.12 🇪🇹 Ethiopian Green Legacy Movement

Green Legacy is an Ethiopian environmental initiative emphasizing large-scale tree planting, environmental restoration and climate-resilient development.

Main Objectives

  • Increase tree and forest cover.
  • Restore degraded landscapes.
  • Reduce environmental degradation.
  • Improve watershed protection.
  • Support climate action.
  • Promote environmental awareness.
  • Encourage community participation.

Importance of Tree Planting

  • Reduces soil erosion.
  • Improves soil protection.
  • Supports biodiversity.
  • Improves watershed protection.
  • Stores carbon.
  • Provides useful forest products.
  • Improves landscapes.
🌱 Key idea:

Planting trees is not only about increasing the number of trees. Successful restoration also requires proper species selection, care, survival and long-term management.

7.13 Environmental Conservation

Environmental conservation means protecting, managing and using natural resources responsibly so that they remain available and productive.

Major Conservation Measures

  • Afforestation
  • Reforestation
  • Soil conservation
  • Water conservation
  • Wildlife protection
  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Protected areas
  • Waste management
  • Environmental education

7.14 Role of Communities

Environmental protection cannot be achieved by government alone. Local communities are important because they directly use and manage many natural resources.

Community Actions

  • Planting trees
  • Protecting forests
  • Conserving soil
  • Protecting water sources
  • Reducing waste
  • Participating in environmental campaigns
  • Protecting wildlife
  • Using resources sustainably

7.15 Role of Government

  • Develop environmental policies.
  • Enforce environmental laws.
  • Establish protected areas.
  • Support environmental restoration.
  • Provide environmental education.
  • Support sustainable agriculture.
  • Develop early warning systems.
  • Promote renewable energy.
  • Support research and monitoring.

7.16 Sustainable Resource Management

Natural resources should be used in ways that meet present needs without destroying their ability to support future generations.

Principles

  • Use resources efficiently.
  • Reduce waste.
  • Protect ecosystems.
  • Restore degraded land.
  • Use renewable resources responsibly.
  • Involve local communities.
  • Consider future generations.
🧠 Remember:

Protect → Use wisely → Restore → Sustain
