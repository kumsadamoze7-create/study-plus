📕 Grade 9 Mathematics

Unit 3 — Solving Equations

📚 Study Plus Detailed Notes

📘 Unit 3 Overview

An equation is a mathematical statement showing that two expressions have equal values.

In this unit we learn how to find the unknown value, usually represented by a letter such as x.

Important idea:
Whatever operation you perform on one side of an equation, you must perform the same operation on the other side.
⚡ Golden Rule:

Keep the equation balanced.

Left side = Right side

3.1 What is an Equation?

An equation is a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal.
Example:
x + 5 = 12

The unknown is x.

We want to find the value of x that makes the equation true.

Equation vs Expression

Expression Equation
3x + 5 3x + 5 = 20
2a − 7 2a − 7 = 9
⚡ Trick:
If you see an = sign, you are dealing with an equation.

3.2 Solving One-Step Equations

The goal is to isolate the variable.

Addition Equation

x + 7 = 15
Subtract 7 from both sides:
x = 15 − 7
x = 8

Subtraction Equation

x − 4 = 10
Add 4 to both sides:
x = 14

Multiplication Equation

3x = 18
Divide both sides by 3:
x = 6

Division Equation

x / 5 = 4
Multiply both sides by 5:
x = 20
⚡ Reverse Operation Trick:

+ → −
− → +
× → ÷
÷ → ×

3.3 Two-Step Equations

Two-step equations require more than one operation.

Solve:
2x + 5 = 17
Step 1: Subtract 5:
2x = 12
Step 2: Divide by 2:
x = 6
⚡ Order Trick:
Undo addition/subtraction first.
Then undo multiplication/division.

3.4 Equations with Brackets

When an equation contains brackets, use the distributive property first.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
Solve:
3(x + 2) = 18
Expand:
3x + 6 = 18
Subtract 6:
3x = 12
Divide by 3:
x = 4
⚡ Bracket Trick:
The number outside the bracket multiplies every term inside.

3.5 Equations with Variables on Both Sides

Sometimes the unknown appears on both sides.

Solve:
5x + 2 = 2x + 14
Subtract 2x:
3x + 2 = 14
Subtract 2:
3x = 12
Divide by 3:
x = 4
⚡ Trick:
Move variable terms to one side and constants to the other side.

3.6 Equations with Fractions

Fractions can make equations look difficult, but we can remove them by multiplying the entire equation by the common denominator.

Solve:
x/3 + 2 = 6
Subtract 2:
x/3 = 4
Multiply by 3:
x = 12

Example with Two Fractions

x/2 + x/3 = 10
The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6. Multiply the entire equation by 6:
3x + 2x = 60
5x = 60
x = 12
⚡ Fraction Trick:
Find the LCM of the denominators, then multiply every term by the LCM.

3.7 Equations with Decimals

Decimal equations can be solved by first removing the decimal places.

Solve:
0.5x = 6
Multiply both sides by 10:
5x = 60
Therefore:
x = 12
⚡ Decimal Trick:
Multiply by 10, 100, 1000, etc. depending on the number of decimal places.

3.8 Equations from Word Problems

Many real-life problems can be translated into equations.

Step-by-Step Method

  1. Identify the unknown.
  2. Let the unknown be x.
  3. Translate the words into a mathematical equation.
  4. Solve the equation.
  5. Check the answer in the original problem.
Problem:

A number increased by 7 is 20. Find the number.

Let the number be x.
x + 7 = 20
Therefore:
x = 13
⚡ Translation Trick:

"sum" → +
"difference" → −
"product" → ×
"quotient" → ÷
"is" → =
"more than" → +
"less than" → −

3.9 Checking a Solution

After solving an equation, substitute the answer back into the original equation.

Equation:
2x + 3 = 11
We found:
x = 4
Check:
2(4) + 3 = 11
8 + 3 = 11 ✔️
Therefore x = 4 is correct.
🎯 Exam Tip:
If you have time, always substitute your answer back. It catches sign and arithmetic mistakes.

3.10 Special Cases

No Solution

Consider:
2x + 3 = 2x + 8
Subtract 2x:
3 = 8
This is impossible. Therefore: No solution.

Infinitely Many Solutions

2(x + 3) = 2x + 6
Expand:
2x + 6 = 2x + 6
This is always true. Therefore: Infinitely many solutions.
⚡ Remember:

False statement → No solution
True identity → Infinitely many solutions

🧠 Equation Rules

Operation Inverse Operation
Add Subtract
Subtract Add
Multiply Divide
Divide Multiply
Golden Rule:

Whatever you do to one side, do the same thing to the other side.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Equation contains an equality sign.
  • The goal is usually to isolate the unknown.
  • Use inverse operations to undo operations.
  • Expand brackets using the distributive property.
  • Move variable terms to one side.
  • Move constants to the other side.
  • Use the LCM to remove fractions.
  • Multiply by powers of 10 to remove decimals.
  • Translate word problems carefully.
  • Check the answer by substitution.

🎯 Unit 3 Exam Tips

  • Never change only one side of an equation.
  • Watch negative signs carefully.
  • When removing brackets, multiply every term.
  • When moving a term across the equals sign, its sign changes because you are applying the inverse operation.
  • For fractions, use the LCM.
  • For word problems, define the unknown before writing the equation.
  • Always check your final answer when possible.
  • Do not round answers unless the question asks you to.
