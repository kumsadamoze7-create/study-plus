Unit 6 — Human–Natural Environment Interactions in Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Introduction

Human beings and the natural environment are closely connected. People depend on the environment for food, water, energy, shelter, raw materials and many other needs.

At the same time, human activities change the natural environment through agriculture, settlement, industry, transport, resource extraction and other activities.

Unit 6 focuses on:
  • Human-environment relationship
  • Dependence on the environment
  • Modification of the environment
  • Adaptation to the environment
  • Optimum population and resource use
  • Overpopulation and resource use
  • Underpopulation and resource use
  • Impacts of rapid population growth

6.1 Human-Environment Relationship

Human-environment interaction refers to the relationship between human beings and the natural environment in which they live.

The environment includes the physical surroundings and natural systems such as land, water, air, climate, plants and animals.

Humans have interacted with the environment throughout history. For example, people clear land for farming, build roads and houses, use water, and obtain food and other resources from nature.

Key idea:

The relationship is two-way:

Environment → affects humans
Humans → affect the environment

6.1.1 Dependence on the Environment

Dependence means relying on the natural environment to obtain resources and conditions necessary for survival and development.

Human Needs Obtained from Nature

  • Air for breathing
  • Water for drinking and other uses
  • Food
  • Land for agriculture and settlement
  • Forest resources
  • Minerals
  • Energy resources
  • Medicinal resources

Examples from Ethiopia

  • Farmers depend on soil and rainfall for crop production.
  • Pastoralists depend on pasture and water.
  • People depend on rivers and groundwater for water supply.
  • Industries depend on raw materials and energy.
  • Communities depend on forests for various products.
🧠 Remember:

Humans depend on nature for → Food + Water + Land + Energy + Materials

6.1.2 Modification of the Environment

Modification means changing or altering the natural environment to meet human needs.

Examples of Human Modification

  • Clearing forests for agriculture
  • Building roads
  • Constructing houses and cities
  • Building dams
  • Irrigation
  • Mining
  • Industrial development
  • Creating reservoirs

Positive Modification

Some modifications can improve human welfare when they are properly planned and managed.

  • Irrigation systems
  • Water supply infrastructure
  • Soil conservation structures
  • Tree planting
  • Land restoration

Negative Modification

  • Deforestation
  • Pollution
  • Land degradation
  • Habitat destruction
  • Soil erosion
  • Uncontrolled urban expansion
Example:

Building an irrigation system can modify the natural water flow but may increase agricultural production when it is sustainably managed.

6.1.3 Adaptation to the Environment

Adaptation is the adjustment of people or human systems to environmental conditions or changes in order to reduce harm and improve survival.

Examples of Human Adaptation

  • Changing clothing according to temperature
  • Building houses suitable for local climate
  • Changing agricultural practices
  • Using irrigation during dry periods
  • Moving livestock according to availability of pasture and water
  • Using drought-resistant crops
  • Preparing for floods

Adaptation to Climate Change

Communities may adapt to changing climate conditions through improved farming practices, water management, early warning systems and disaster preparedness.

🧠 Remember:

Dependence → We use the environment
Modification → We change the environment
Adaptation → We adjust ourselves to the environment

6.1.4 Environmental Determinism

Environmental determinism is the idea that the physical environment strongly determines human activities, behaviour and development.

According to this view, climate, relief and other physical conditions have a major influence on human life.

Example:

Climate can influence the types of crops that can be grown in a particular area.

6.1.5 Environmental Possibilism

Environmental possibilism emphasizes that humans are not completely controlled by nature. People can use knowledge, technology and innovation to adapt to and modify their environment.
Example:

In areas with limited rainfall, people can use irrigation and water conservation technologies to support agriculture.

Determinism vs Possibilism
Determinism Possibilism
Environment strongly controls human activities. Humans have choices within environmental limits.
Emphasizes environmental influence. Emphasizes human adaptation and technology.

6.2 Optimum Population and Resource Use

Optimum population refers to a population size that allows the available resources, technology and economic conditions of an area to be used effectively and supports a desirable standard of living.

There is no single optimum population size for every country or place. It varies according to resources, technology, economic structure, infrastructure, environment and time.

Conditions Associated with Optimum Population

  • Effective use of resources
  • High or improving living standards
  • Employment opportunities
  • Balanced population structure
  • Availability of essential services
  • Efficient use of productive land
Important:

Optimum population is not simply the largest possible population. It is related to the balance between population and available resources and technology.

6.2.1 Population and Resource Use

The relationship between population and resources is important for development.

When population increases, demand for food, water, land, energy, housing and other resources generally also increases.

Examples

  • More people → greater demand for food.
  • More households → greater demand for water.
  • More population → greater demand for housing land.
  • More economic activity → greater demand for energy.
Simple relationship:

Population ↑ → Resource demand ↑

6.3 Overpopulation and Resource Use

Overpopulation occurs when the number of people in an area is greater than the capacity of available resources and technology to support the population at a desirable standard of living.

Important Point

Overpopulation is not determined only by the number of people. It depends on the relationship between population size, resources, technology, consumption patterns and environmental conditions.

Signs of Population Pressure

  • Land shortage
  • High pressure on farmland
  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Water shortages
  • Unemployment
  • Housing shortages
  • Environmental degradation

Effects on Natural Resources

  • Overuse of farmland
  • Deforestation
  • Soil erosion
  • Water depletion
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Resource conflicts

Possible Solutions

  • Improved agricultural technology
  • Resource conservation
  • Education
  • Family planning services
  • Employment creation
  • Efficient water management
  • Environmental protection
  • Balanced regional development

6.4 Underpopulation and Resource Use

Underpopulation occurs when the population is too small to make effective use of the available resources and economic opportunities of an area.

Possible Advantages

  • Less pressure on natural resources
  • More resources available per person
  • Lower population density
  • Less congestion
  • Potentially better environmental conditions

Possible Disadvantages

  • Shortage of labour
  • Underuse of resources
  • Small local markets
  • Difficulty providing infrastructure efficiently
  • Limited economic specialization
Example:

An area may have fertile land and mineral resources but very few people and limited infrastructure, so some resources may remain underutilized.

6.4.1 Overpopulation vs Underpopulation

Overpopulation Underpopulation
Population pressure is high. Population may be too small relative to available resources.
Resources may be overused. Resources may be underused.
May cause environmental degradation. May cause labour shortages.
Demand for services can exceed capacity. Providing services over large areas can be difficult.
🧠 Easy memory:

Over → Too many people for available capacity
Under → Too few people to fully use available potential

6.5 Impacts of Rapid Population Growth

Rapid population growth is a situation in which the population increases quickly over a relatively short period.

Rapid population growth can create opportunities, but when economic development and infrastructure do not keep pace, it can create significant pressures.

Environmental Impacts

  • Deforestation
  • Soil erosion
  • Land degradation
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Water pollution
  • Water scarcity
  • Expansion of cultivation into fragile areas
  • Waste accumulation

Economic Impacts

  • Pressure on employment
  • Increased demand for food
  • Increased demand for infrastructure
  • Pressure on public budgets
  • Housing shortages
  • Pressure on agricultural land

Social Impacts

  • Pressure on schools
  • Pressure on hospitals
  • Housing problems
  • Traffic congestion
  • Expansion of informal settlements
  • Competition for employment

6.5.1 Population Growth and Agriculture

As population grows, demand for food increases. Farmers may respond by increasing production through intensification, expansion of farmland or improved technology.

Possible Problems

  • Continuous cultivation may reduce soil fertility.
  • Expansion into forests can cause deforestation.
  • Cultivation of steep slopes can increase erosion.
  • Overgrazing can degrade pasture.

Possible Solutions

  • Improved farming techniques
  • Crop rotation
  • Agroforestry
  • Terracing
  • Contour farming
  • Improved irrigation
  • Use of appropriate agricultural technologies

6.5.2 Population Growth and Water Resources

Growing populations require increasing amounts of water for drinking, sanitation, agriculture, industry and other activities.

Possible Effects

  • Increased water demand
  • Groundwater pressure
  • Pollution of rivers and lakes
  • Competition among users
  • Higher costs of water supply

Solutions

  • Water conservation
  • Efficient irrigation
  • Rainwater harvesting
  • Watershed protection
  • Wastewater treatment
  • Pollution control

6.5.3 Population Growth and Forest Resources

Population growth may increase demand for farmland, fuelwood, construction materials and settlement land.

Possible Effects

  • Forest clearing
  • Fuelwood pressure
  • Habitat loss
  • Soil erosion
  • Reduced biodiversity

Solutions

  • Afforestation
  • Reforestation
  • Agroforestry
  • Efficient energy use
  • Community forest management
  • Forest protection

6.5.4 Population Growth and Urbanization

Rapid population growth can contribute to the expansion of towns and cities.

Urban Challenges

  • Housing shortages
  • Traffic congestion
  • Waste management problems
  • Air pollution
  • Pressure on water supply
  • Pressure on schools and hospitals
  • Unemployment

Better Urban Management

  • Proper land-use planning
  • Improved public transport
  • Waste management
  • Green spaces
  • Efficient water systems
  • Affordable housing
  • Employment opportunities

6.5.5 Population Growth and Socioeconomic Development

Population growth has both positive and negative effects depending on the availability of resources, technology, jobs and institutions.

Possible Positive Effects

  • Larger labour force
  • Larger consumer market
  • More human resources
  • Greater potential for innovation
  • Potential economic growth

Possible Negative Effects

  • Unemployment
  • Pressure on public services
  • Food insecurity
  • Environmental degradation
  • Housing shortages
  • Increased competition for resources
Important:

Population growth itself is not automatically harmful. Its effects depend on how effectively a society manages resources, technology, infrastructure, education, health and employment.

6.5.6 Population and Sustainable Development

Sustainable development means meeting present needs while maintaining the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Ways to Balance Population and Resources

  • Improve education.
  • Improve health services.
  • Increase agricultural productivity sustainably.
  • Protect natural resources.
  • Create employment.
  • Improve infrastructure.
  • Promote efficient resource use.
  • Support family planning and reproductive health.
  • Develop renewable energy.
  • Strengthen environmental management.
🧠 Key Formula:

People + Resources + Technology + Good Management = Sustainable Development
