2.1 Revision on Natural Numbers and Integers

Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are counting numbers.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

Whole Numbers

Whole numbers include zero and all natural numbers.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...}

Integers

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

Z = {...,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,...}
Example:
Is -7 an integer?

Yes. Therefore -7 ∈ Z.
🔥 Entrance Trick:
Remember:
Natural ⊂ Whole ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational ⊂ Real

2.1.1 Euclid Division Lemma

For positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that:

a = bq + r
0 ≤ r < b

a = dividend
b = divisor
q = quotient
r = remainder

Example:
Divide 47 by 5.

47 = 5 × 9 + 2

Therefore:
q = 9
r = 2
🔥 Entrance Trick:
Always check the remainder:
0 ≤ r < divisor
⚠️ The remainder can never be equal to or greater than the divisor.

2.1.2 Prime and Composite Numbers

Prime Number

A prime number has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19...

Composite Number

A composite number has more than two positive factors.

4 = 1 × 4 = 2 × 2
Therefore 4 is composite.
🔥 Important:
1 is neither prime nor composite.
2 is the only even prime number.

2.1.3 Divisibility Tests

  • 2: Last digit is even.
  • 3: Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
  • 4: Last two digits divisible by 4.
  • 5: Last digit is 0 or 5.
  • 6: Divisible by both 2 and 3.
  • 8: Last three digits divisible by 8.
  • 9: Sum of digits divisible by 9.
  • 10: Last digit is 0.
  • 11: Difference between alternating digit sums is divisible by 11.
Example:
Is 7,236 divisible by 3?

7 + 2 + 3 + 6 = 18
18 is divisible by 3.

Therefore 7,236 is divisible by 3. ✅
🔥 Entrance Trick:
For 3 and 9, do not divide the whole number. Just add its digits.

2.1.4 GCF and LCM

GCF

GCF is the greatest common factor shared by two or more numbers.

24 = 2³ × 3
36 = 2² × 3²

GCF = 2² × 3 = 12

LCM

LCM is the smallest positive common multiple.

24 = 2³ × 3
36 = 2² × 3²

LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72
🔥 Remember:
GCF → lowest powers of common primes.
LCM → highest powers of all primes.
GCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers

2.2 Rational Numbers

A rational number can be written as:

p / q, where p,q ∈ Z and q ≠ 0
Examples:
1/2, -3/4, 5, 0
🔥 Every integer is rational because:

5 = 5/1

2.2.1 Decimal Representation

Terminating Decimal

1/2 = 0.5
3/4 = 0.75
5/8 = 0.625

Converting Decimal to Fraction

0.375 = 375/1000

Simplify:
375/1000 = 3/8
🔥 Number of decimal places tells the denominator:
1 place → 10
2 places → 100
3 places → 1000

Number Line

For a/b, divide the interval into b equal parts and count a parts from zero.

3/4 lies between 0 and 1, at the third of four equal divisions.

2.2.2 Repeating Decimals to Fractions

Let x = 0.3333...

10x = 3.3333...
10x - x = 3
9x = 3
x = 1/3
🔥 1 repeating digit → multiply by 10.
2 repeating digits → multiply by 100.
3 repeating digits → multiply by 1000.
Challenge:
0.272727...

x = 0.272727...
100x = 27.272727...

99x = 27
x = 27/99 = 3/11

2.3 Irrational Numbers

An irrational number cannot be written as p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

√2, √3, √5, π

Neither Terminating nor Repeating

Irrational decimals continue forever without a repeating pattern.

√2 = 1.41421356...
🔥 Remember:
Rational decimal → terminating OR repeating.
Irrational decimal → neither terminating nor repeating.

2.3.1 Locating Irrational Numbers

To locate √2:

1² = 1
2² = 4

Therefore: 1 < √2 < 2
🔥 To estimate √n, find the two consecutive perfect squares around n.

2.3.2 Operations on Irrational Numbers

√2 + √2 = 2√2

√3 × √3 = 3

√2 × √8 = √16 = 4
⚠️ √a + √b is generally NOT equal to √(a+b).
🔥 Simplify radicals before adding or subtracting.

2.4 Real Numbers

Real numbers include both rational and irrational numbers.

Real Numbers = Rational Numbers ∪ Irrational Numbers

Comparing Real Numbers

√5 ≈ 2.236
√4 = 2

Therefore √5 > √4.

Intervals

Intervals describe a set of real numbers between two endpoints.

Open: (2,5) → 2 < x < 5
Closed: [2,5] → 2 ≤ x ≤ 5
Mixed: [2,5) → 2 ≤ x < 5
🔥 Square bracket [ ] → endpoint included.
Round bracket ( ) → endpoint excluded.

2.4.2 Absolute Value

Absolute value is the distance of a number from zero.

|x| ≥ 0
|-7| = 7
|5| = 5
|0| = 0
🔥 Absolute value never gives a negative answer.

2.4.3 Exponents and Radicals

Laws of Exponents

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

a⁰ = 1

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
2³ × 2² = 2⁵ = 32
🔥 Same base multiplication → ADD powers.
Same base division → SUBTRACT powers.

Radicals

√50 = √(25×2)
= 5√2
√12 + √27
= 2√3 + 3√3
= 5√3
🔥 Look for perfect-square factors inside the radical.

Law of Accuracy, Rounding and Significant Figures

Rounding

Look at the digit immediately after the place you want.

Round 4.376 to 2 decimal places.

4.376 → third decimal = 6
So increase 7 to 8.

Answer = 4.38

Significant Figures

Significant figures are the meaningful digits in a measurement.

45.67 → 4 significant figures
0.00456 → 3 significant figures
500. → 3 significant figures
🔥 Leading zeros are normally NOT significant.
Zeros between non-zero digits ARE significant.

Standard / Scientific Notation

a × 10ⁿ
1 ≤ a < 10
450000 = 4.5 × 10⁵

0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴
🔥 Large number → positive exponent.
Small decimal → negative exponent.

Rationalization

Rationalization removes a radical from the denominator.

1/√2 Multiply by √2/√2: = √2/2 Therefore: 1/√2 = √2/2

Conjugate Method

1/(√3 + 1) Multiply by: (√3 - 1)/(√3 - 1) Denominator: (√3 + 1)(√3 - 1) = 3 - 1 = 2
🔥 For √a + b, use the conjugate √a - b.
