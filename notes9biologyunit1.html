📘 Unit 1: Introduction to Biology

Biology is the branch of natural science that studies living organisms, their structures, functions, relationships, reproduction, growth, development, evolution and interactions with the environment.

Remember:

Biology comes from two Greek words:

  • Bio = Life
  • Logos = Study

Therefore, Biology literally means the study of life.

1️⃣ Definition of Biology

Biology is the scientific study of life and living organisms.

It investigates how organisms are structured, how their body systems function, how they reproduce, how they obtain and use energy, how they interact with one another and how they interact with their environment.

Examples of things studied in Biology

  • Cells
  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Microorganisms
  • Human body
  • Genetics
  • Ecology
  • Evolution
  • Diseases
  • Nutrition
  • Reproduction

2️⃣ Why Do We Study Biology?

Biology is important because living organisms are directly connected to human life and the environment.

Major importance of Biology

🏥 1. Medicine and Health

Biology helps us understand diseases, microorganisms, the human body, medicines, vaccines and methods of preventing diseases.

🌾 2. Agriculture

Biological knowledge helps farmers improve crop production, control pests, improve livestock and understand plant and animal diseases.

🌳 3. Environmental Protection

Biology helps us understand ecosystems, biodiversity, pollution, conservation and the effects of human activities on nature.

🍎 4. Food and Nutrition

Biology helps us understand nutrients, digestion, balanced diets, food preservation and food-related diseases.

🧬 5. Genetics

Biology helps us understand heredity, genes, chromosomes and how characteristics are passed from parents to offspring.

🔬 6. Biotechnology

Biological knowledge is used in producing medicines, improving crops, food production and many industrial processes.

🐄 7. Animal Production

Biology helps in understanding animal growth, reproduction, nutrition and disease control.

4️⃣ The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic way scientists use to investigate questions, solve problems and obtain reliable knowledge.

Steps of the Scientific Method

  1. Observation
    Collect information using the senses or scientific instruments.
  2. Question
    Ask a question based on the observation.
  3. Hypothesis
    A testable possible explanation or prediction.
  4. Experiment
    Perform a controlled investigation to test the hypothesis.
  5. Data Collection
    Record observations and measurements obtained during the experiment.
  6. Data Analysis
    Organize and interpret the collected data.
  7. Conclusion
    Decide whether the evidence supports or does not support the hypothesis.
  8. Communication
    Share the results with others through reports, presentations or scientific publications.
Important:

A hypothesis is not simply a guess. It must be testable.

5️⃣ Variables in an Experiment

Independent Variable

The factor that the researcher deliberately changes.

Dependent Variable

The factor that is measured or observed as a result of changing the independent variable.

Controlled Variables

Factors that are kept constant so that they do not affect the result.

Variable Meaning
Independent What the scientist changes
Dependent What the scientist measures
Controlled What is kept constant

7️⃣ Light Microscope

A light microscope is an instrument that uses visible light and lenses to magnify objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye.

🔬 Main Parts and Functions

Part Function
Eyepiece / Ocular lens Magnifies the image viewed by the observer.
Objective lenses Provide different levels of magnification.
Revolving nosepiece Holds and rotates objective lenses.
Stage Supports the slide.
Stage clips Hold the slide in position.
Diaphragm Controls the amount of light reaching the specimen.
Light source / Mirror Provides or directs light toward the specimen.
Coarse adjustment knob Moves the lens or stage for rough focusing.
Fine adjustment knob Produces precise focusing.
Arm Supports the upper parts and is used when carrying.
Base Supports the microscope.

8️⃣ How to Use a Light Microscope

  1. Place the microscope on a stable, flat surface.
  2. Clean the lenses using proper lens paper.
  3. Place the specimen on a clean glass slide.
  4. Cover the specimen with a cover slip carefully.
  5. Place the slide on the stage.
  6. Secure the slide using stage clips.
  7. Start with the lowest-power objective lens.
  8. Adjust the light.
  9. Use the coarse adjustment to obtain rough focus.
  10. Use the fine adjustment to obtain a sharp image.
  11. Move to a higher-power objective when necessary.
  12. Use the fine adjustment for high-power focusing.
⚠️ Important:

When using high-power objectives, avoid using the coarse adjustment knob because it may cause the lens to hit and damage the slide.

9️⃣ Magnification

Magnification tells us how many times larger an image appears compared with the actual specimen.

Formula:

Total Magnification = Eyepiece Magnification × Objective Magnification

Example

If the eyepiece is 10× and the objective is 40×:

10 × 40 = 400×

Therefore, the total magnification is 400×.

🔟 General Laboratory Safety Rules

  1. Always follow the teacher's instructions.
  2. Wear appropriate protective equipment when required.
  3. Do not eat or drink in the laboratory.
  4. Do not taste laboratory chemicals.
  5. Do not smell chemicals directly.
  6. Handle glassware carefully.
  7. Keep the working area clean.
  8. Report accidents immediately.
  9. Never perform an experiment without permission.
  10. Keep chemicals properly labeled.
  11. Wash your hands after laboratory work.
  12. Dispose of waste properly.
  13. Keep flammable materials away from flames.
  14. Use electrical equipment carefully.
  15. Do not run or play in the laboratory.

  • Variable: A factor that can change in an experiment.
