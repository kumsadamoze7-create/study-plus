1.1 Definition of Biology

What is Biology?

The word Biology comes from two Greek words: bios, meaning life, and logos, meaning study or knowledge. Therefore, biology is the scientific study of life and living organisms.

Biologists study living organisms such as humans, animals, plants, fungi and microorganisms. They investigate how organisms are structured, how they function, how they reproduce, how they interact with their environment, and how they change over time.

Major branches of Biology

  • Botany: Study of plants.
  • Zoology: Study of animals.
  • Microbiology: Study of microorganisms.
  • Ecology: Study of organisms and their environment.
  • Genetics: Study of heredity and variation.
  • Anatomy: Study of the structure of organisms.
  • Physiology: Study of how organisms and their parts function.
  • Cell Biology: Study of cells and their structures and functions.

Biology and other sciences

Biology is not isolated from other sciences. It works together with chemistry, physics, mathematics, earth science and computer science. For example, biologists use chemistry to understand chemical reactions in cells and mathematics to analyze biological data.

Example: When studying how the human heart works, biology may be combined with physics to understand pressure and movement of blood.
Remember: Biology = Scientific study of life and living organisms.

1.2 Why Do We Study Biology?

Biology is important because living organisms are directly connected to human life and the environment. Understanding biology helps us solve problems related to health, food, agriculture, environment and biotechnology.

Importance of studying Biology

1. Medicine and Health

Biology helps us understand the human body, diseases, microorganisms, nutrition and methods of disease prevention and treatment. Doctors, nurses, pharmacists and medical researchers depend heavily on biological knowledge.

2. Agriculture

Biology helps farmers understand crops, pests, diseases, soil organisms, animal production and plant growth. Biological knowledge can improve food production.

3. Environmental Protection

Biology helps us understand ecosystems, biodiversity, pollution, climate effects and conservation of natural resources.

4. Food Production

Biological knowledge is used in livestock production, crop production, food preservation and fermentation.

5. Biotechnology

Biology contributes to biotechnology, where living organisms or their components are used to produce useful products and services.

6. Understanding Ourselves

Biology helps humans understand their bodies, growth, reproduction, nutrition, heredity and relationships with the environment.

Exam Point: Biology has applications in medicine, agriculture, environmental conservation, food production, biotechnology and many other fields.

1.3 The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic way of investigating questions and solving problems using observations, evidence, experiments and logical conclusions.

Steps of the Scientific Method

  1. Observation

    Observation is the process of collecting information using our senses or scientific instruments.

  2. Question

    After observing something, a scientist asks a question that can be investigated.

  3. Hypothesis

    A hypothesis is a possible explanation or testable prediction about an observation or question.

  4. Experiment

    An experiment is carried out to test the hypothesis under controlled conditions.

  5. Data Collection

    Scientists collect and record information obtained from observations or experiments.

  6. Analysis

    The collected data are organized and examined to identify patterns or relationships.

  7. Conclusion

    The scientist decides whether the evidence supports or does not support the hypothesis.

  8. Communication

    Scientists communicate their findings through reports, presentations, tables, graphs, articles and other scientific methods.

Example: A student wants to know whether sunlight affects plant growth.

Observation: Plants near sunlight appear healthier.
Question: Does sunlight affect plant growth?
Hypothesis: If a plant receives more suitable sunlight, then it will grow better.
Experiment: Grow plants under different light conditions.
Data: Measure plant height regularly.
Conclusion: Use the collected evidence to determine whether the hypothesis is supported.

Variables

A variable is a factor that can change in an experiment.

  • Independent variable: The factor that the researcher changes.
  • Dependent variable: The factor that is measured or observed.
  • Controlled variables: Factors kept constant so they do not affect the result.
Remember: A good scientific investigation should be based on evidence and should be repeatable.

1.4 Tools of a Biologist

Biologists use different tools and equipment to observe organisms, collect samples, measure quantities, conduct experiments and record information.

1.4.1 Laboratory Tools

  • Test tube: Used to hold, mix or heat small amounts of substances.
  • Beaker: Used for holding, mixing and sometimes heating liquids.
  • Measuring cylinder: Used to measure the volume of liquids.
  • Balance: Used to measure mass.
  • Thermometer: Used to measure temperature.
  • Dropper: Used to transfer small amounts of liquid.
  • Petri dish: Used to culture or observe microorganisms and biological samples.
  • Funnel: Used to help transfer liquids or for filtration.
  • Glass slide: Used to hold specimens for microscopic observation.
  • Forceps: Used to hold or pick up small specimens.
  • Dissecting tools: Used for biological dissection and examination of specimens.

1.4.2 Field Tools

Biologists also work outside laboratories. Field tools help them observe, collect and measure organisms and environmental conditions.

  • Hand lens – used to magnify small objects.
  • Binoculars – used to observe distant organisms, especially birds.
  • Measuring tape – used to measure length or distance.
  • Thermometer – used to measure temperature.
  • Camera – used to record organisms and observations.
  • Specimen containers – used to safely collect samples.
  • Notebook – used to record observations and field data.
Important: Different biological tools are selected according to the type of observation, measurement or experiment being performed.

1.5 The Light Microscope

A light microscope is an instrument used to observe objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the unaided human eye. It uses visible light and lenses to magnify specimens.

Why is a microscope important?

Cells and many microorganisms are too small to be observed clearly with the naked eye. A microscope allows biologists to observe their structures and characteristics.

1.5.1 Parts and Functions of a Light Microscope

  • Eyepiece / ocular lens: The lens through which the observer looks.
  • Objective lenses: Lenses that provide different levels of magnification.
  • Revolving nosepiece: Holds the objective lenses and allows the user to change them.
  • Stage: Supports the microscope slide.
  • Stage clips: Hold the slide in position.
  • Diaphragm: Controls the amount of light reaching the specimen.
  • Light source: Provides light for viewing the specimen.
  • Coarse adjustment knob: Used for large changes in focus.
  • Fine adjustment knob: Used for small adjustments to obtain a sharp image.
  • Arm: Supports the upper parts of the microscope and is used when carrying it.
  • Base: Supports the microscope.

Magnification

The total magnification of a compound light microscope is found by multiplying the magnification of the eyepiece lens by the magnification of the objective lens.

Example:
Eyepiece = 10×
Objective = 40×

Total magnification = 10 × 40 = 400×

1.5.2 Handling and Using a Light Microscope

  1. Carry the microscope carefully using both hands.
  2. Place it on a stable and clean surface.
  3. Place the prepared slide on the stage.
  4. Secure the slide with the stage clips.
  5. Start with the low-power objective.
  6. Use the coarse adjustment to bring the specimen into focus.
  7. Use the fine adjustment to make the image clear.
  8. Adjust the light for proper viewing.
  9. Change to higher magnification when necessary.
  10. Clean the microscope properly after use.
Warning: Never use your fingers to clean microscope lenses. Use appropriate lens-cleaning material.

1.6 General Laboratory Safety Rules

A biology laboratory contains chemicals, glassware, instruments, specimens and other materials that can cause injury if they are not handled properly. Laboratory safety rules help prevent accidents.

Important Laboratory Safety Rules

  1. Wear appropriate protective equipment such as a laboratory coat and protective eyewear when required.
  2. Do not eat or drink in the laboratory.
  3. Do not run or play in the laboratory.
  4. Follow the teacher's or laboratory supervisor's instructions.
  5. Handle chemicals carefully.
  6. Never taste laboratory chemicals.
  7. Do not directly smell chemicals. Use appropriate laboratory procedures.
  8. Handle glassware carefully.
  9. Report accidents, broken glass and spills immediately.
  10. Keep the working area clean and organized.
  11. Wash your hands after laboratory activities.
  12. Dispose of waste using the correct disposal method.
  13. Never use damaged laboratory equipment.
  14. Know where emergency equipment is located.
Remember: Safety is everyone's responsibility in the laboratory. Never perform an experiment without understanding the instructions and possible hazards.
