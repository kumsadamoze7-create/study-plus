📜 Grade 9 History

UNIT 6

🌍 AFRICA AND THE OUTSIDE WORLD 1500–1880s

📚 Detailed Topic-by-Topic Notes

📖 Unit Introduction

From the 15th century onward, Africa entered into increasingly intensive relationships with Europe and other parts of the world. These contacts changed African political, economic and social life in important ways.

European maritime expansion brought European traders, missionaries, explorers and settlers into closer contact with African societies.

Unit 6 focuses on five major areas:
  1. Contact with the Outside World
  2. Slavery
  3. The “Legitimate” Trade
  4. The White Settlement in South Africa
  5. European Explorers and Missionaries

🎯 Major Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the nature of African-European relations.
  • Explain the origin and development of the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade.
  • Analyze the political, social and economic effects of the slave trade.
  • Explain the transition to the so-called “legitimate” trade.
  • Explain European settlement in South Africa.
  • Explain the motives and activities of European explorers and missionaries.

🌍 6.1 Contact with the Outside World

🌊 Portuguese Maritime Expansion

The Portuguese were among the first European powers to establish sustained maritime contact with the African coast.

Portuguese sailors gradually explored the Atlantic coast of Africa and eventually reached the Indian Ocean.

Why did Europeans search for new routes?

  • To obtain valuable trade goods.
  • To find direct routes to Asia.
  • To bypass existing trade intermediaries.
  • To expand political influence.
  • To spread Christianity.
  • To obtain gold and other resources.

⚓ Portuguese activities

The Portuguese established trading posts and forts along parts of the African coast. They became involved in existing African commercial networks.

European coastal contacts did not mean that Europeans immediately controlled the whole continent. African states and rulers continued to control most inland territories.

Important:

Early European influence was strongest along coastal areas where Europeans could use ships, forts and trading posts.

🇨🇩 Contact with Kongo

Portuguese contact with the Kingdom of Kongo became important from the late 15th century.

Portuguese missionaries also entered Kongo and attempted to spread Christianity.

Some Kongo rulers initially welcomed Portuguese contact because of potential political, commercial and technological advantages.

⛓️ 6.2 Slavery

What is slavery?

Slavery is a system in which people are treated as the property or controlled labor of other people and are deprived of important freedoms.

Slavery before the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade

Slavery existed in different societies in Africa, Europe and Asia long before the beginning of the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade.

Enslaved people could be used in agriculture, domestic work, military service and other activities.

🌍 African involvement

Some African rulers and merchants participated in slave trading networks. Captives could be obtained through warfare, raids and political conflicts.

Important distinction:

The existence of earlier forms of slavery does not make the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade harmless. The Atlantic system developed into a huge international system involving the forced transportation of millions of Africans.

🚢 Origin of the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade

The expansion of European plantation economies in the Americas created a huge demand for labor.

European powers increasingly transported enslaved Africans across the Atlantic to work in plantations and mines.

🌱 Plantation Economy

Plantations in the Americas produced crops such as:

  • Sugar
  • Tobacco
  • Cotton
  • Coffee

These plantations required large amounts of labor.

🔺 The Triangular Trade

The Atlantic trade is often described using the term Triangular Trade.

Route General movement
Europe → Africa Manufactured goods and other commodities
Africa → Americas Enslaved Africans
Americas → Europe Plantation products such as sugar and tobacco

🚢 Middle Passage

The forced voyage of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic from Africa to the Americas is known as the Middle Passage.

The conditions were extremely overcrowded, violent and unhealthy. Many people died during the voyage.

📈 Expansion of the slave trade

European powers including Portugal, Spain, Britain, France and the Netherlands became involved in Atlantic slavery at different times.

European merchants, African intermediaries and American plantation owners participated in different parts of the system.

💥 Effects on Africa

Political effects

  • Increased warfare in some regions.
  • Some states became stronger through slave trading.
  • Other states were weakened or destabilized.
  • Political rivalries intensified in some areas.

Economic effects

  • Some rulers and merchants gained wealth.
  • Local economies became increasingly connected to external markets.
  • Human beings became a major commodity.
  • Other productive activities were sometimes neglected.

Social effects

  • Large numbers of people were forcibly removed.
  • Families and communities were disrupted.
  • Population losses affected many regions.
  • Fear and insecurity increased in affected areas.

Cultural effects

  • African cultures spread throughout the Americas.
  • African languages, music, food and religious traditions influenced the Americas.

💰 6.3 The “Legitimate” Trade

What does “legitimate trade” mean?

The term “legitimate trade” refers mainly to commercial trade in commodities other than enslaved people, especially after European opposition to the slave trade increased.

🛑 Abolition of the Slave Trade

During the late 18th and 19th centuries, abolitionist movements increasingly opposed the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade.

Britain became a major force in suppressing the Atlantic slave trade.

🌴 Major legitimate trade products

  • Palm oil
  • Groundnuts
  • Ivory
  • Gold
  • Rubber

🌴 Palm oil

Palm oil became especially important in West Africa. It was used in industries and became an important export.

📦 Effects of legitimate trade

  • Expansion of commercial agriculture.
  • Growth of coastal trading centers.
  • Increase in European economic influence.
  • Expansion of African-European commercial relations.
  • Increased European interest in African resources.
Important:

Although called “legitimate,” this trade did not mean that European-African economic relations became equal. European demand continued to shape African economies and helped prepare the way for later colonial expansion.

🇿🇦 6.4 White Settlement in South Africa

⚓ Dutch settlement at the Cape

European settlement in South Africa began with the establishment of a Dutch settlement at the Cape.

1652: The Dutch East India Company established a refreshment station at the Cape under Jan van Riebeeck.

Why was the Cape important?

  • It was located on the sea route between Europe and Asia.
  • Ships needed fresh food and water.
  • The settlement could support Dutch maritime trade.

👨‍🌾 Boers

Dutch-speaking settlers who moved inland and established farms became known as Boers.

They developed a farming and pastoral economy.

⚔️ Conflict with African peoples

Expansion of European settlement caused conflict with African communities over land, livestock and resources.

The Dutch settlers gradually expanded their territory inland.

🇬🇧 British control

Britain later took control of the Cape Colony.

British rule introduced new political and economic pressures and intensified conflicts involving settlers and African societies.

🏃 Great Trek

Some Dutch-speaking settlers moved away from British-controlled areas in the Cape. This migration became known as the Great Trek.

The migrants established Boer republics in the interior.

🎯 Motives of white settlers

  • Search for land.
  • Pastoral and agricultural opportunities.
  • Economic interests.
  • Political independence.
  • Expansion of settlement.
Exam point:

The expansion of white settlement resulted in repeated conflicts over land, labor, livestock and political power between settlers and African societies.

🧭 6.5 European Explorers and Missionaries

🔎 European Explorers

From approximately the 1780s to the 1870s, increasing numbers of European explorers travelled into the interior of Africa.

European knowledge of Africa was still limited despite centuries of coastal contact.

Why did explorers travel into Africa?

  • Scientific curiosity.
  • Desire for adventure.
  • Search for fame.
  • Geographical discovery.
  • Mapping unknown regions.
  • Search for commercial opportunities.
  • Support for future European expansion.
Important:

The textbook emphasizes that explorers were not always completely independent scientists. Many journeys were supported by people and organizations interested in future trade and expansion.

👤 David Livingstone

David Livingstone was a famous Scottish missionary and explorer who travelled extensively in Africa.

He explored parts of central and southern Africa and strongly opposed the slave trade.

👤 Henry Morton Stanley

Henry Morton Stanley was an explorer who travelled through Central Africa and became famous for his exploration of the Congo region.

His expeditions contributed to European geographical knowledge and later colonial activity in Central Africa.

✝️ European Missionaries

Christian missionaries travelled to Africa with the goal of spreading Christianity.

Some missionaries also established schools, medical services and written materials.

Portuguese missionaries

Roman Catholic missionaries followed Portuguese coastal expansion in tropical Africa.

During the late 15th and early 16th centuries, missionaries attempted to convert African rulers and communities.

Political dimension:

Some European powers hoped that converted African rulers would become political allies. Therefore missionary activity could have both religious and political dimensions.

📚 Missionary contributions

  • Spread of Christianity.
  • Establishment of schools in some areas.
  • Translation and writing of African languages.
  • Medical activities in some regions.
  • Production of geographical and cultural information.

⚠️ Limitations and contradictions

Missionary activities were not always separated from European political and economic interests. Some missionaries opposed slavery and supported African communities, while European colonial expansion was developing at the same time.

🧠 Unit 6 Quick Revision

  • European contact: European maritime powers, especially Portugal, established coastal contacts and trading posts.
  • Slavery: Slavery existed before Atlantic slavery, but the Trans-Atlantic system expanded enormously with plantation economies in the Americas.
  • Middle Passage: Forced Atlantic voyage of enslaved Africans to the Americas.
  • Triangular Trade: Connected Europe, Africa and the Americas through a system of trade.
  • Legitimate Trade: Commercial trade in products other than enslaved people, especially after abolition.
  • White Settlement: Dutch settlement began at the Cape in 1652 and later British control and Boer expansion changed southern Africa.
  • Explorers: European explorers travelled into Africa for geographical, scientific, commercial and political reasons.
  • Missionaries: Spread Christianity and sometimes established schools and medical services, but missionary work could also be linked to European political interests.
