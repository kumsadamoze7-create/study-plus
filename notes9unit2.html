2.1 Revision on Natural Numbers and Integers

Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are the positive counting numbers.

N = {1,2,3,4,5,...}

Integers

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

Z = {...,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,...}
Example:
7 is a natural number and an integer.
−5 is an integer but NOT a natural number.
0 is an integer.
⚡ Exam Trick:
Negative numbers → integers, not natural numbers.

2.1.1 Euclid Division Lemma

For positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that:

a = bq + r

0 ≤ r < b

Where:

  • a = dividend
  • b = divisor
  • q = quotient
  • r = remainder
Example:

Divide 47 by 5.

47 = 5(9) + 2

Therefore:
q = 9
r = 2
🔥 Entrance Exam Example:

If 157 = 12q + r and 0 ≤ r < 12, find q and r.

157 = 12(13) + 1

Therefore q = 13 and r = 1.
⚡ Short Trick:
Remainder is ALWAYS smaller than divisor.

r < b
⚠️ Do not allow r ≥ b.

2.1.2 Prime and Composite Numbers

Prime Number

A prime number has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19...

Composite Number

A composite number has more than two positive factors.

4 = 1 × 4 = 2 × 2
Therefore 4 is composite.

Important Fact

1 is neither prime nor composite.
⚡ Fast Prime Test:
To check whether n is prime, test divisibility by prime numbers up to √n.
🔥 Example:
Is 97 prime?

√97 ≈ 9.85.
Check 2, 3, 5 and 7.
97 is not divisible by any of them.

Therefore 97 is prime.

2.1.3 Divisibility Tests

Number Divisibility Rule
2 Last digit is even.
3 Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
4 Last two digits are divisible by 4.
5 Last digit is 0 or 5.
6 Divisible by both 2 and 3.
8 Last three digits are divisible by 8.
9 Sum of digits is divisible by 9.
10 Last digit is 0.
11 Difference between alternating digit sums is 0 or a multiple of 11.
Example:
Is 4,536 divisible by 9?

4+5+3+6 = 18
18 is divisible by 9.

Therefore 4536 is divisible by 9.
⚡ For 3 and 9 → ADD THE DIGITS.

For 2 → LAST DIGIT.
For 5 → 0 or 5.
For 10 → 0.

2.1.4 Greatest Common Factor and Least Common Multiple

GCF / HCF

The greatest common factor is the largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.

Find GCF of 18 and 24.

Factors of 18: 1,2,3,6,9,18
Factors of 24: 1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24

GCF = 6

LCM

The least common multiple is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of all given numbers.

Find LCM of 6 and 8.

Multiples of 6: 6,12,18,24...
Multiples of 8: 8,16,24...

LCM = 24

Prime Factorization Method

12 = 2² × 3
18 = 2 × 3²

GCF = 2¹ × 3¹ = 6

LCM = 2² × 3² = 36
For two positive integers:

GCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers
🔥 Example:
If GCF(12,18)=6 and LCM=36:

6 × 36 = 216
12 × 18 = 216

Correct.
⚡ GCF → take the SMALLEST powers.
LCM → take the LARGEST powers.

2.2 Rational Numbers

A rational number is a number that can be written in the form:

a/b, where a,b ∈ Z and b ≠ 0
Examples:

1/2, −3/4, 5, 0, 2.75

All are rational.

Every integer is rational because:

5 = 5/1
⚡ If you can write a number as fraction of integers, it is rational.

2.2.1 Representation of Rational Numbers by Decimal

Terminating Decimal

A decimal that ends after a finite number of digits.

1/4 = 0.25
3/8 = 0.375

Converting Terminating Decimal to Fraction

0.75

= 75/100
= 3/4
⚡ Count decimal places → put 1 followed by that many zeros.

Representing Rational Numbers on Number Line

To locate 3/4:

Divide the interval from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts.
The third point is 3/4.

2.2.2 Converting Repeating Decimals into Fractions

Repeating decimals can always be expressed as rational numbers.

Let:

x = 0.7777...

10x = 7.7777...

Subtract:
10x − x = 7
9x = 7
x = 7/9

Example with Two Repeating Digits

x = 0.121212...

100x = 12.121212...

100x − x = 12
99x = 12
x = 12/99
x = 4/33
⚡ One repeating digit → denominator 9.
Two repeating digits → denominator 99.
Three repeating digits → denominator 999.

2.3 Irrational Numbers

An irrational number cannot be written as a ratio of two integers. Its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Examples:

√2, √3, √5, π
Irrational = Non-terminating + Non-repeating
⚠️ Not every square root is irrational.

√9 = 3, so √9 is rational.

2.3.1 Neither Repeating Nor Terminating Numbers

An irrational decimal continues forever without repeating a fixed pattern.

π = 3.1415926535...

√2 = 1.4142135623...

These decimals neither terminate nor repeat.

Locating Irrational Numbers on Number Line

To locate √2:

Since:
1² = 1
2² = 4

√2 lies between 1 and 2.

Using a right triangle with legs 1 and 1:
hypotenuse = √(1²+1²)=√2.

That length can be transferred onto the number line.
⚡ First find two perfect squares surrounding the number.

For √20:
16 < 20 < 25
Therefore 4 < √20 < 5.

2.3.2 Operations on Irrational Numbers

Addition

√2 + √2 = 2√2

Subtraction

5√3 − 2√3 = 3√3

Multiplication

√2 × √8

= √16
= 4

Division

√18 / √2

= √9
= 3
⚠️ Only like radicals can be added or subtracted.

√2 + √3 cannot be simplified to √5.
⚡ Simplify radicals FIRST before combining them.

2.4 Real Numbers

Real numbers consist of both rational and irrational numbers.

Real Numbers = Rational Numbers ∪ Irrational Numbers
Rational: 1/2, 3, −5, 0.25

Irrational: √2, π

All are real numbers.
⚡ Every rational and irrational number is real.

Comparing Real Numbers

Numbers on the number line increase from left to right.

Compare √2 and 1.5.

√2 ≈ 1.414
1.414 < 1.5

Therefore:
√2 < 1.5
⚡ When comparing radicals, convert them to decimals or compare their squares when both numbers are positive.

Intervals

An interval represents a set of real numbers between two endpoints.

Open Interval

(a,b) = {x : a < x < b}

Closed Interval

[a,b] = {x : a ≤ x ≤ b}

Half-Open Intervals

[a,b) = {x : a ≤ x < b}

(a,b] = {x : a < x ≤ b}
⚡ Square bracket [ ] → endpoint INCLUDED.
Round bracket ( ) → endpoint EXCLUDED.

2.4.2 Absolute Value

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero on the number line.

|x| ≥ 0
|5| = 5
|-5| = 5
|0| = 0

Important Rules

|x| = a → x = ±a

|x| < a → −a < x < a

|x| > a → x < −a or x > a
🔥 Example:

|x| < 3

Therefore:
−3 < x < 3
⚡ Absolute value = DISTANCE.
Distance cannot be negative.

2.4.3 Exponents and Radicals

An exponent tells how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Laws of Exponents

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ

(a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ

a⁰ = 1, a ≠ 0

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
2³ × 2⁴
= 2⁷
= 128
⚡ Same base × → ADD powers.
Same base ÷ → SUBTRACT powers.
Power of power → MULTIPLY powers.

Radicals

A radical is an expression containing a root symbol.

√a = a¹ᐟ²

∛a = a¹ᐟ³
√50
= √(25×2)
= 5√2

Multiplication of Radicals

√a × √b = √ab
√3 × √12
= √36
= 6

Division

√a / √b = √(a/b)

Law of Accuracy / Approximation

When an exact value is not required, numbers may be approximated to a required degree of accuracy.

√2 ≈ 1.41421356...

To 2 decimal places:
√2 ≈ 1.41
⚡ Look at the digit immediately after the required place.

5 or greater → round UP.
Less than 5 → keep the digit.

Rounding

Nearest Whole Number

7.6 → 8
7.3 → 7

Decimal Places

12.476 to 2 decimal places:

12.48

Significant Figures

Significant figures are digits that carry meaningful information about the precision of a measurement.

45.67 has 4 significant figures.

0.00456 has 3 significant figures.
⚡ Leading zeros are NOT significant.
Zeros between non-zero digits ARE significant.
Trailing zeros after a decimal ARE significant.

Exponent / Standard Notation

Standard form expresses a number as:

a × 10ⁿ
where 1 ≤ |a| < 10
450000

= 4.5 × 10⁵
0.00072

= 7.2 × 10⁻⁴
⚡ Large number → positive exponent.
Small decimal → negative exponent.
🔥 Entrance Example:

(3×10⁴)(2×10³)

= 6×10⁷

Operations Involving Exponents and Radicals

Example 1

2³ × 2⁵
= 2⁸
= 256

Example 2

√12 + √27

= 2√3 + 3√3
= 5√3

Example 3

(√5)² = 5
⚠️ Do NOT distribute square root incorrectly:

√(a+b) ≠ √a + √b

Rationalization

Rationalization means removing a radical from the denominator of a fraction.

Simple Case

1/√2

Multiply numerator and denominator by √2:

= √2/2

Denominator with Two Terms

Use the conjugate.

(a+b)(a−b)=a²−b²
1/(√3+1)

Multiply by (√3−1):

= (√3−1)/(3−1)

= (√3−1)/2
⚡ Rationalization Trick:
One radical → multiply by the same radical.

Two terms → multiply by the conjugate.

1. Euclid: a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b

2. Prime → exactly 2 factors.

3. 1 → neither prime nor composite.

4. GCF → smallest prime powers.

5. LCM → largest prime powers.

6. Rational → fraction of integers.

7. Terminating decimal → rational.

8. Repeating decimal → rational.

9. Non-terminating + non-repeating → irrational.

10. Real = rational + irrational.

11. [ ] means included.

12. ( ) means excluded.

13. |x| means distance from zero.

14. Same base multiplication → add exponents.

15. Same base division → subtract exponents.

16. Power of a power → multiply exponents.

17. Large standard-form number → positive exponent.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistakes

❌ Thinking 1 is prime.

❌ Forgetting the condition r < divisor in Euclid's lemma.

❌ Adding unlike radicals: √2 + √3 ≠ √5

❌ Thinking every square root is irrational.

❌ Treating |−5| as −5.

❌ Confusing open and closed intervals.

❌ Adding exponents when dividing.

❌ Subtracting exponents when multiplying.

❌ Rounding without checking the next digit.

❌ Leaving a radical in the denominator when rationalization is required.
