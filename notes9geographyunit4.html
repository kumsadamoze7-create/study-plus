Unit 4 — Population and Demographic Characteristics of Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Introduction

Population is one of the most important components of human geography. It refers to the people living in a particular area at a particular time.

The study of population helps us understand the number of people, their characteristics, where they live, how fast the population changes and how population affects economic and social development.

Unit 4 focuses on:
  • Population concepts
  • Population growth
  • Population composition
  • Population distribution
  • Population density
  • Rural and urban settlements
  • Health and disease
  • Population and sustainable development

4.1 Meaning of Population

Population is the total number of people living in a particular geographical area at a particular time.

Population may be studied at different scales such as a village, town, region, country or the whole world.

Importance of Population Study

  • Helps governments plan schools and hospitals.
  • Helps plan housing and infrastructure.
  • Helps estimate labour supply.
  • Helps understand food and water requirements.
  • Helps plan employment opportunities.
  • Helps understand population-related environmental pressure.

4.2 Demography

Demography is the scientific study of population size, composition, distribution and changes over time.

Main Demographic Processes

  • Birth
  • Death
  • Migration

These processes determine how the size and composition of a population change.

4.3 Population Census

Population census is the systematic process of collecting, compiling and publishing demographic, economic and social information about all people in a country or defined area at a specified time.

Importance of Census

  • Provides population size information.
  • Shows population distribution.
  • Provides information about age and sex.
  • Supports development planning.
  • Helps allocate public resources.
  • Supports electoral and administrative planning.
  • Provides information for research.
Remember:

A census attempts to cover the whole population, whereas a sample survey studies only a selected part of the population.

4.4 Population Growth

Population growth refers to the increase or decrease in the number of people in a population over a given period of time.

Population size changes mainly because of births, deaths and migration.

Natural Increase

Natural increase is the difference between the number of births and the number of deaths during a given period.

Natural Increase = Births − Deaths

If births are greater than deaths, the population has positive natural increase.

If deaths are greater than births, the population has negative natural increase.

4.5 Birth Rate and Fertility

Crude Birth Rate

The crude birth rate is the number of live births per 1,000 people in a population in a given year.

CBR = (Number of live births ÷ Mid-year population) × 1000

Fertility

Fertility refers to actual reproductive performance, particularly the number of children born to women or a population.

Factors Affecting Fertility

  • Age at marriage
  • Education
  • Access to family planning
  • Economic conditions
  • Cultural practices
  • Women's employment
  • Urbanization
  • Access to health services

4.6 Mortality and Death Rate

Mortality refers to deaths occurring within a population.

The crude death rate is the number of deaths per 1,000 people in a population during a given year.

CDR = (Number of deaths ÷ Mid-year population) × 1000

Factors Affecting Mortality

  • Access to health services
  • Nutrition
  • Clean water
  • Sanitation
  • Diseases
  • Age structure
  • Living conditions
  • Conflict and disasters

4.7 Migration

Migration is the movement of people from one place to another with the intention of establishing a new residence, temporarily or permanently.

Types of Migration

1. Internal Migration

Movement of people within the boundaries of the same country.

Example: Movement from a rural area to a city.

2. International Migration

Movement of people from one country to another.

3. Rural-Urban Migration

Movement of people from rural areas to urban areas.

4. Urban-Rural Migration

Movement from urban areas to rural areas.

Push Factors

Push factors are conditions that encourage people to leave their place of origin.

  • Unemployment
  • Drought
  • Conflict
  • Land shortage
  • Poverty
  • Lack of services

Pull Factors

Pull factors are conditions that attract people to a destination.

  • Employment opportunities
  • Better education
  • Better health services
  • Higher income opportunities
  • Better infrastructure
  • Safety and security
🧠 Memory Trick

Push = pushes you away
Pull = pulls you toward a place

4.8 Population Composition

Population composition refers to the characteristics of a population according to variables such as age, sex, occupation, education, language and religion.

Major Components

  • Age
  • Sex
  • Occupation
  • Education
  • Language
  • Religion
  • Ethnicity

4.9 Age Composition

Age composition describes the distribution of people according to age groups.

Common Age Groups

Age Group General Meaning
Children / Young population People who are generally dependent and not yet in the main working-age group.
Working-age population People who are generally in the main labour-force age range.
Elderly population Older people who may require greater social and health support.

Importance of Age Structure

Age structure affects the demand for schools, jobs, health services, housing and social support.

Young population:

A large young population creates a high demand for education, health services and future employment.

4.10 Sex Composition

Sex composition refers to the proportion of males and females in a population.

Sex Ratio

Sex ratio compares the number of males with the number of females in a population.

Sex Ratio = (Number of males ÷ Number of females) × 100

Sex composition influences labour supply, family structure and social services.

4.11 Population Pyramid

A population pyramid is a graph that shows the age and sex structure of a population.

Males are commonly shown on one side and females on the other side. Age groups are arranged vertically.

Main Types

1. Expansive Pyramid

Has a broad base, indicating a relatively large proportion of young people and generally high fertility.

2. Stationary Pyramid

Has a more balanced structure, with relatively similar proportions across several age groups.

3. Constrictive Pyramid

Has a relatively narrow base, indicating lower fertility and a larger proportion of older people.

🧠 Easy way to remember:

Wide base → Many young people
Narrow base → Fewer young people

4.12 Population Distribution

Population distribution refers to the spatial pattern of where people live in a particular area.

Population in Ethiopia is unevenly distributed. Some areas have high concentrations of people while others have relatively few inhabitants.

Factors Affecting Population Distribution

  • Relief and altitude
  • Climate
  • Availability of water
  • Soil fertility
  • Economic opportunities
  • Transport and infrastructure
  • Historical factors
  • Political and social conditions
  • Health conditions

4.13 Population Density

Population density is the average number of people living per unit area of land.
Population Density = Total Population ÷ Total Land Area

Population density is commonly expressed as people per square kilometre.

Example:

If an area has 100,000 people and covers 2,000 km²:

Density = 100,000 ÷ 2,000

Density = 50 people per km²

Do not confuse:

Distribution = Where people are located.
Density = How many people live per unit area.

4.14 Population Distribution in Ethiopia

The population of Ethiopia is concentrated unevenly across the country.

Areas of Relatively High Population Concentration

Many highland areas have historically supported large populations because of relatively favourable temperatures, agricultural conditions, water availability and settlement history.

Areas of Relatively Low Population Concentration

Some lowland and arid areas have lower population densities because of limited rainfall, high temperatures, water scarcity and difficult environmental conditions.

Important:

Population distribution is influenced by both physical and human factors. It cannot be explained by climate alone.

4.15 Settlement

Settlement refers to a place where people live and establish homes and communities.

Types of Settlement

  • Rural settlement
  • Urban settlement

Rural Settlement

Rural settlements are generally located in countryside areas where agriculture and other primary activities are important.

Urban Settlement

Urban settlements are towns and cities characterized by higher population concentration and a greater importance of industrial, commercial and service activities.

4.16 Rural Settlement Patterns

1. Dispersed Settlement

Houses are spread relatively far apart from one another.

2. Nucleated / Clustered Settlement

Houses and people are concentrated closely together around a particular location.

3. Linear Settlement

Houses are arranged along a road, river, valley or other linear feature.

🧠 Remember:

Dispersed → Spread out
Nucleated → Clustered
Linear → Along a line

4.17 Urbanization

Urbanization is the process through which an increasing proportion of the population lives in urban areas.

Causes of Urbanization

  • Rural-urban migration
  • Natural population growth
  • Expansion of towns
  • Employment opportunities
  • Education
  • Health services
  • Industrialization

Positive Effects

  • More employment opportunities
  • Better access to services
  • Economic growth
  • Innovation and technology
  • Improved infrastructure

Challenges

  • Housing shortages
  • Traffic congestion
  • Unemployment
  • Waste management problems
  • Air pollution
  • Pressure on water supply
  • Expansion of informal settlements

4.18 Population, Health and Disease

Population characteristics and environmental conditions are closely related to health.

Factors Affecting Health

  • Availability of health facilities
  • Clean water
  • Sanitation
  • Nutrition
  • Education
  • Housing conditions
  • Income
  • Environmental quality

Common Health Challenges

Communities can face communicable diseases, malnutrition, water-related diseases and other health problems depending on local conditions.

Ways to Improve Health

  • Access to clean drinking water
  • Improved sanitation
  • Health education
  • Vaccination
  • Better nutrition
  • Accessible health services
  • Environmental sanitation

4.19 Health Conditions in Highlands and Lowlands

The physical environment can influence the distribution of diseases and health conditions.

Highland Areas

Highland environments have different temperatures, altitudes, vegetation and disease conditions compared with lowlands. Health patterns may therefore differ.

Lowland Areas

Lowland environments can have higher temperatures and different water and ecological conditions that influence the distribution of some diseases.

Important:

Disease patterns are influenced by many factors, including climate, altitude, environment, sanitation, health services and human behaviour. Geography alone does not determine disease.

4.20 Population Growth and Development

Population growth can create both opportunities and challenges for development.

Possible Benefits

  • Larger labour force
  • Larger domestic market
  • More ideas and innovation
  • Potential economic growth

Possible Challenges

  • Pressure on land
  • Pressure on water
  • Pressure on schools
  • Pressure on health facilities
  • Unemployment
  • Housing shortages
  • Environmental degradation
  • Food security challenges

4.21 Population and the Natural Environment

Population growth and human activities can increase pressure on natural resources.

Major Environmental Pressures

  • Deforestation
  • Soil erosion
  • Overgrazing
  • Water pollution
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Land degradation
  • Waste generation

Possible Solutions

  • Environmental conservation
  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Efficient resource use
  • Urban planning
  • Family planning and reproductive health services
  • Environmental education
  • Improved infrastructure

4.22 Population and Sustainable Development

Sustainable development means improving people's well-being while protecting the resources and environment needed by future generations.

Population-related Development Priorities

  • Quality education
  • Accessible health care
  • Employment creation
  • Food security
  • Gender equality
  • Environmental conservation
  • Improved infrastructure
  • Responsible urban planning
🧠 Key idea:

Population + Resources + Economy + Environment must be balanced for sustainable development.

4.23 Cultural and Social Diversity

Ethiopia is characterized by substantial linguistic, ethnic, cultural and religious diversity.

This diversity is an important part of the country's social and cultural heritage.

Importance of Diversity

  • Rich cultural heritage
  • Different languages and traditions
  • Traditional knowledge
  • Cultural tourism
  • Social identity
Important:

Respect for diversity and peaceful coexistence are important for social stability and sustainable development.
