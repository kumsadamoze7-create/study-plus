Unit 2 — Climate of Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Overview

Climate is one of the most important geographical factors influencing life and human activities in Ethiopia. This unit explains weather and climate, the elements and controls of climate, rainfall and temperature patterns, Ethiopia's climatic regions, seasonal changes and the relationship between climate, vegetation, agriculture and human activities.

Important idea:

Weather describes the atmospheric condition of a place at a particular time, while climate describes the long-term average pattern of weather in a place.

2.1 Weather and Climate

What is Weather?

Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time.

Weather can change from hour to hour or from day to day. For example, a morning may be cool and cloudy while the afternoon becomes hot and sunny.

Examples of Weather Conditions

  • Temperature
  • Rainfall
  • Cloud cover
  • Wind
  • Humidity
  • Atmospheric pressure
  • Sunshine

What is Climate?

Climate is the long-term average pattern of atmospheric conditions of a place, normally considered over many years.

Climate helps us understand the usual temperature, rainfall, wind and other atmospheric characteristics of a region.

Difference Between Weather and Climate

Weather Climate
Short-term atmospheric condition Long-term atmospheric pattern
Can change quickly Changes relatively slowly
Example: Today's rainfall Example: Average annual rainfall pattern
🧠 Memory Trick

Weather = What is happening now?
Climate = What usually happens?

2.2 Elements of Climate

Climate is described using several atmospheric elements. The most important ones include temperature, rainfall, humidity, atmospheric pressure, wind and sunshine.

1. Temperature 🌡️

Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of the atmosphere or a place.

Temperature is commonly measured using a thermometer and expressed in degrees Celsius (°C).

2. Rainfall 🌧️

Rainfall is water that falls from clouds to the Earth's surface in the form of liquid water.

Rainfall is an important source of water for agriculture, rivers, lakes and ecosystems.

3. Humidity 💧

Humidity refers to the amount of water vapour present in the atmosphere.

High humidity means that the air contains a large amount of water vapour.

4. Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the force exerted by the weight of air on a unit area of the Earth's surface.

5. Wind 💨

Wind is the horizontal movement of air from an area of higher atmospheric pressure toward an area of lower atmospheric pressure.

6. Sunshine ☀️

Sunshine provides the energy that drives most atmospheric processes. Differences in the amount of solar energy received contribute to differences in temperature and climate.

2.3 Factors Affecting Climate

The climate of a place is controlled by several physical factors. The most important factors include latitude, altitude, relief, distance from large water bodies, winds and vegetation.

1. Latitude

Latitude is the angular distance north or south of the Equator.

Places near the Equator generally receive more direct solar radiation than places farther from the Equator. Therefore, latitude influences temperature.

2. Altitude ⛰️

Altitude is the height of a place above sea level. As altitude increases, temperature generally decreases.

General relationship:

Higher altitude → Lower temperature

Lower altitude → Higher temperature

3. Relief / Topography

Mountains and highlands influence rainfall and temperature. Mountain slopes can force moist air to rise, cool and produce rainfall.

4. Distance from Water Bodies

Large water bodies heat and cool more slowly than land. Therefore, areas close to large water bodies often have smaller temperature variations than inland areas.

5. Winds and Air Masses

Winds transport heat and moisture from one place to another. Moist winds can bring rainfall, while dry winds may contribute to dry conditions.

6. Vegetation

Vegetation influences humidity, evaporation, soil moisture and local temperature conditions.

☀️ 2.4 Solar Radiation and Temperature

The Sun is the main source of energy for the Earth's atmosphere and surface.

Solar radiation heats the Earth's surface. The heated surface then transfers energy to the atmosphere.

Why Temperature Differs From Place to Place

  • Latitude
  • Altitude
  • Cloud cover
  • Season
  • Time of day
  • Surface characteristics
  • Vegetation

Temperature and Altitude in Ethiopia

Ethiopia has large differences in elevation. Therefore, temperature varies considerably from the hot lowlands to the cooler highlands.

Example:

A highland town can have a much cooler climate than a lowland area even though both are located in the same general region.

🌧️ 2.5 Rainfall in Ethiopia

Rainfall is one of the most important climatic elements in Ethiopia because agriculture and water resources depend heavily on the availability and seasonal distribution of rainfall.

Major Types of Rainfall

1. Convectional Rainfall

Convectional rainfall occurs when the Earth's surface is strongly heated. Warm moist air rises, expands and cools. Water vapour condenses and may produce heavy rainfall, often accompanied by thunderstorms.

2. Orographic / Relief Rainfall

Orographic rainfall occurs when moist air is forced to rise over mountains or highlands.

As the air rises, it cools and water vapour condenses, forming clouds and rainfall.

3. Frontal / Cyclonic Rainfall

Frontal rainfall occurs when different air masses meet and warmer air is forced to rise over colder air.

The rising air cools and condensation produces rainfall.

🧠 Rainfall Memory Trick

Convectional → Heating
Orographic → Mountain
Frontal → Air masses meet

2.6 Distribution of Rainfall in Ethiopia

Rainfall is not evenly distributed throughout Ethiopia. There are significant spatial and seasonal differences.

Factors Affecting Rainfall Distribution

  • Altitude
  • Relief
  • Latitude
  • Seasonal wind systems
  • Moisture sources
  • Distance from moisture sources

Seasonal Rainfall

Different parts of Ethiopia receive rainfall during different seasons and in different amounts.

The highlands of western, central and southwestern Ethiopia generally receive more rainfall than many eastern and southeastern lowland areas.

Important:

Rainfall amount alone is not enough. The timing, duration and reliability of rainfall are also important for agriculture and water availability.

2.7 Humidity, Clouds and Condensation

Humidity

Humidity is the amount of water vapour in the atmosphere.

Evaporation

Evaporation is the process by which liquid water changes into water vapour due to heating.

Condensation

Condensation occurs when water vapour cools and changes into tiny liquid water droplets.

Cloud Formation

Clouds form when moist air rises, cools and reaches a condition in which water vapour condenses into tiny droplets or ice crystals.

Process:

Water → Evaporation → Water vapour → Rising/Cooling → Condensation → Clouds → Rainfall

2.8 Atmospheric Pressure and Wind

Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of air.

Pressure varies with altitude and temperature.

Wind

Wind is the horizontal movement of air from high-pressure areas toward low-pressure areas.

High Pressure → Low Pressure = Wind

Importance of Wind

  • Moves heat from one region to another.
  • Transports moisture.
  • Influences rainfall.
  • Affects evaporation.
  • Influences weather conditions.

2.9 Seasons and Seasonal Variation in Ethiopia

Ethiopia experiences seasonal variations in rainfall, temperature, wind and atmospheric conditions.

Main Rainfall Seasons

In many parts of Ethiopia, the major rainy period is associated with the movement of moist atmospheric systems during the summer months.

Other areas, particularly some southern and southeastern regions, may have different rainfall seasons.

Dry Season

During dry periods, rainfall decreases significantly in many regions. Clear skies and strong daytime heating can occur, although conditions vary from region to region.

Remember:

Ethiopia does not have exactly the same rainfall pattern everywhere. Regional differences are very important.

2.10 Climatic Regions of Ethiopia

Because of Ethiopia's large differences in altitude, topography, latitude and rainfall, the country has different climatic conditions.

Traditional Altitude-Based Climate Classification

Traditional Ethiopian climatic classification uses altitude and associated temperature conditions. Important categories include:

1. Bereha

Very hot lowland areas with generally high temperatures and, in many locations, low rainfall.

2. Kolla

Low-altitude areas that are generally warm to hot.

3. Weyna Dega

Intermediate highland areas with moderate temperatures. They support many important agricultural activities.

4. Dega

Higher elevation areas with cooler conditions.

5. Wurch

Very high mountain environments characterized by very cold conditions and specialized vegetation.

🧠 Altitude Climate Trick

Bereha → Very hot
Kolla → Hot
Weyna Dega → Moderate
Dega → Cool
Wurch → Very cold

🌳 2.11 Climate and Natural Vegetation

Climate strongly influences the distribution, density and type of natural vegetation.

Temperature and rainfall are especially important. Areas with adequate rainfall and suitable temperatures can support dense vegetation, while dry areas tend to have sparse vegetation.

Examples

  • Wet highland areas can support relatively dense vegetation.
  • Dry lowlands often have grasses, shrubs and drought-resistant plants.
  • Very cold high-altitude environments support specialized vegetation.

🌾 2.12 Climate and Agriculture

Agriculture is strongly influenced by climate. Temperature, rainfall amount, timing and length of the growing season affect crop production.

Effects of Rainfall

  • Determines water availability for crops.
  • Influences planting time.
  • Influences crop selection.
  • Affects crop yields.
  • Influences pasture availability.

Effects of Temperature

  • Controls the growth rate of crops.
  • Influences crop suitability.
  • Affects evaporation and soil moisture.
  • Influences livestock conditions.
Climate Risk:

Drought, floods, irregular rainfall and extreme temperatures can reduce agricultural productivity and threaten food security.

👥 2.13 Climate and Human Activities

Climate influences many human activities and patterns of life.

  • Agriculture
  • Settlement
  • Livestock production
  • Transportation
  • Tourism
  • Water supply
  • Energy production
  • Health

Climate and Settlement

People tend to prefer environments where temperature, water availability, disease conditions and other environmental factors are relatively favourable.

Climate and Tourism

Climate affects the attractiveness and accessibility of tourist destinations. Mountain climates, lakes, wildlife areas and unique landscapes can support tourism.

🌍 2.14 Climate Variability and Climate Change

Climate Variability

Climate variability refers to variations in climatic conditions over time, such as changes in rainfall amounts or temperature from one year to another.

Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in the average state and characteristics of the climate. Modern climate change is strongly associated with human activities that increase greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere.

Possible Impacts

  • More frequent drought in some areas.
  • Changes in rainfall patterns.
  • Flood risks.
  • Changes in agricultural productivity.
  • Pressure on water resources.
  • Effects on ecosystems and biodiversity.

Adaptation

Adaptation means adjusting human systems and activities to reduce the harmful effects of climate-related changes.

Examples of Adaptation

  • Water harvesting
  • Improved irrigation
  • Drought-resistant crops
  • Soil and water conservation
  • Early warning systems
  • Climate-smart agriculture
