📘 Unit 3: Cells

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.

Cells are extremely small, but they carry out the essential processes needed for life. Different cells may have different structures and functions, but all living cells share some basic features.

Key idea:

The cell is the smallest unit of life capable of carrying out life processes.

1️⃣ 3.1 What is a Cell?

A cell is the smallest basic structural and functional unit of a living organism.

Unicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of only one cell are called unicellular organisms.

The single cell performs all necessary life activities.

Examples

  • Amoeba
  • Paramecium
  • Many bacteria
  • Yeast

Multicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of many cells are called multicellular organisms.

In multicellular organisms, cells become specialized for particular functions and work together.

Examples

  • Humans
  • Dogs
  • Maize plants
  • Trees
Difference:
Unicellular Multicellular
Made of one cell Made of many cells
One cell performs life processes Different cells may have specialized functions
Example: Amoeba Example: Human

2️⃣ 3.2 Cell Theory

Cell theory explains the fundamental importance of cells in living organisms.

Scientists Who Contributed to Cell Theory

🔬 Robert Hooke

In 1665, Robert Hooke observed thin slices of cork using a microscope.

He saw many small box-like structures and called them cells because they reminded him of the small rooms occupied by monks.

Remember:

Robert Hooke introduced the term "cell" after observing cork.

🔬 Matthias Schleiden

Matthias Schleiden was a German botanist.

In 1838, he concluded that all plants are made up of cells.

🔬 Theodor Schwann

Theodor Schwann was a German zoologist.

In 1839, he concluded that all animals are made up of cells.

🔬 Rudolf Virchow

Rudolf Virchow was a German pathologist.

In 1855/1856, he proposed the idea that new cells arise from existing cells through cell division.

⭐ Three Main Statements of Cell Theory

  1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
Modern cell theory also recognizes that:
  • Cells contain genetic material.
  • Energy flow occurs within cells.
  • Cells have chemical similarities.
  • Cells have a membrane separating their contents from the environment.

3️⃣ 3.3 Cell Structure and Function

Cells contain structures called organelles. Different organelles perform different functions.

Common Cell Structures

🧠 1. Nucleus

The nucleus contains most of the cell's genetic material and controls many activities of the cell.

  • Contains DNA.
  • Controls cell activities.
  • Important in cell division.

🧱 2. Cell Membrane

The cell membrane surrounds the cell and controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

  • Separates the cell from its surroundings.
  • Controls transport.
  • Is selectively permeable.

💧 3. Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm is the jelly-like material inside the cell where many chemical reactions occur.

⚡ 4. Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the major sites of aerobic cellular respiration.

They release usable energy from food in the form of ATP.

Exam point:

Mitochondria = major site of aerobic respiration.

🧬 5. Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis.

🕸️ 6. Endoplasmic Reticulum

The endoplasmic reticulum is a membrane system involved in the production and transport of materials.

Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum

Has ribosomes attached to its surface and is involved in protein production and transport.

Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum

Lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid production and other cellular processes.

📦 7. Golgi Apparatus

The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, packages and distributes proteins and other materials.

♻️ 8. Lysosomes

Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that help break down waste materials and worn-out cell components.

🌱 9. Chloroplast

Chloroplasts are found in photosynthetic plant cells and contain chlorophyll.

They are the main sites of photosynthesis.

Chloroplast = Photosynthesis

💧 10. Vacuole

Vacuoles store substances such as water, dissolved materials and cell wastes.

Plant cells usually have a large central vacuole.

🧱 11. Cell Wall

The cell wall provides support, protection and maintains the shape of plant cells.

Plant cell walls are mainly composed of cellulose.

4️⃣ 3.4 Types of Cells

Cells can be broadly divided into two major types:

  • Prokaryotic cells
  • Eukaryotic cells

🔵 Prokaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus. Their DNA is located in a region called the nucleoid.

Characteristics

  • Usually small.
  • Usually unicellular.
  • No true nucleus.
  • No membrane-bound organelles.
  • DNA is located in the nucleoid.
  • Usually have a cell membrane.
  • Many have a cell wall.
  • Ribosomes are present.

Example

Bacteria.

🟢 Eukaryotic Cells

Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Characteristics

  • Usually larger than prokaryotic cells.
  • Have a true nucleus.
  • Contain membrane-bound organelles.
  • May be unicellular or multicellular.
  • Examples include plant, animal, fungal and protist cells.
Feature Prokaryotic Eukaryotic
True nucleus Absent Present
Membrane-bound organelles Absent Present
Size Usually smaller Usually larger
DNA Nucleoid region Inside nucleus
Examples Bacteria Plants, animals, fungi, protists

6️⃣ 3.6 Observing Cells Under a Microscope

A microscope is an instrument used to observe objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye.

Why do we use a microscope?

  • To magnify cells.
  • To observe cell structures.
  • To compare plant and animal cells.
  • To study microorganisms.

🔬 Temporary Slide Preparation

  1. Place a drop of water or suitable liquid on a slide.
  2. Place the specimen on the slide.
  3. Add a suitable stain if needed.
  4. Lower the cover slip carefully.
  5. Place the slide on the microscope stage.
  6. Start with the low-power objective.
  7. Focus the image.
  8. Increase magnification if necessary.

Staining

Stains increase contrast and make cell structures easier to observe.

⚠️ Microscope Safety
  • Carry the microscope with two hands.
  • Keep lenses clean.
  • Use lens paper for cleaning lenses.
  • Start with the low-power objective.
  • Do not force the adjustment knobs.

7️⃣ 3.7 The Cell and Its Environment

Cells are constantly exchanging substances with their surroundings.

Substances such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, water and dissolved nutrients move into or out of cells.

Transport across the cell membrane can occur through passive transport or active transport.

8️⃣ 3.7.1 Passive Transport

Passive transport is the movement of substances across a cell membrane without the direct use of cellular energy (ATP).

Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

High concentration → Low concentration

Examples

  • Oxygen entering cells.
  • Carbon dioxide leaving cells.
  • Movement of some dissolved substances.

Osmosis

Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration.

Water: Higher water concentration → Lower water concentration

In simple terms, water moves across a selectively permeable membrane toward the side with the lower water concentration.

Importance of Osmosis

  • Water absorption by plant roots.
  • Movement of water between cells.
  • Maintenance of cell water balance.

💧 Effects of Different Solutions on Cells

Hypotonic Solution

A solution with lower solute concentration outside the cell than inside the cell. Water tends to enter the cell.

Hypertonic Solution

A solution with higher solute concentration outside the cell than inside the cell. Water tends to leave the cell.

Isotonic Solution

A solution with approximately equal solute concentration inside and outside the cell. There is no net movement of water.

Solution Water Movement Possible Effect
Hypotonic Into cell Cell may swell
Hypertonic Out of cell Cell may shrink
Isotonic No net movement Cell remains relatively stable

9️⃣ 3.7.2 Active Transport

Active transport is the movement of substances across a cell membrane using cellular energy, usually ATP.

Low concentration → High concentration

This movement can occur against the concentration gradient, so energy is required.

Examples

  • Mineral ion uptake by plant root cells.
  • Movement of certain ions across cell membranes.
  • Transport of substances where the cell needs to move them against their concentration gradient.
Feature Passive Transport Active Transport
Energy required No direct ATP use Yes
Direction Down concentration gradient Can move against gradient
Examples Diffusion, osmosis Mineral ion uptake

🔟 3.8 Levels of Biological Organization

In multicellular organisms, cells are organized into different levels of increasing complexity.

1. Cell

The basic structural and functional unit of life.

Example: nerve cell, red blood cell.

2. Tissue

A group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function.

Example: muscle tissue, nerve tissue.

3. Organ

A structure made of different tissues working together to perform a specific function.

Example: heart, stomach, leaf, root.

4. Organ System

A group of organs working together to carry out major functions of an organism.

Example: digestive system, nervous system.

5. Organism

A complete living individual.

Example: human, dog, maize plant.

Remember the order:

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism

🌱 Biological Organization in Plants

Plants also have specialized cells, tissues and organs.

  • Root cells → root tissue → root → root system
  • Xylem cells → xylem tissue → root/stem/leaf structures
  • Leaf cells → leaf tissues → leaf → shoot system

🧠 Key Terms

  • Cell: Basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Cell theory: A set of principles explaining the fundamental role of cells.
  • Organelle: Specialized structure inside a cell that performs a particular function.
  • Prokaryotic: Having no membrane-bound true nucleus.
  • Eukaryotic: Having a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Diffusion: Net movement of particles from high concentration to low concentration.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Active transport: Energy-dependent movement of substances across a membrane.
  • Tissue: Group of cells working together.
  • Organ: Structure made of different tissues working together.
  • Organ system: Group of organs working together.
