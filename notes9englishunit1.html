📚 Introduction to Tenses

A tense shows the time when an action or situation happens. English has three main time periods:

  • Present — something happening now, regularly, or generally.
  • Past — something that happened before now.
  • Future — something that will happen later.

Each time period has four common forms: Simple, Continuous, Perfect and Perfect Continuous. Therefore, we study twelve major English tenses.

1.1 Simple Present Tense

📌 Meaning

The Simple Present is used mainly for habits, routines, general truths, permanent situations and regular activities.

📐 Form

Affirmative: Subject + V1 (+ s/es with he, she, it)

Negative: Subject + do/does + not + V1

Question: Do/Does + Subject + V1?

Examples

I study English every day.
She studies English every day.
They play football.
He plays football.

❌ Negative

I do not study English.
They don't play football.
He doesn't play football.
She doesn't study English.

❓ Questions

Do you study English?
Does he play football?
Does she work here?

📌 Main Uses

  • Daily routines — I go to school every day.
  • Habits — She drinks coffee every morning.
  • General truths — Water boils at 100°C.
  • Scientific facts — The earth moves around the sun.
  • Permanent situations — He lives in Ethiopia.
  • Timetables — The bus leaves at 7:00.
Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, every week, every month.
Important: After doesn't, use V1, not V1+s.

❌ He doesn't plays football.
✅ He doesn't play football.
Exam Trick:
If you see every day, always, usually, often, never, check Simple Present first.

1.2 Present Continuous Tense

📌 Meaning

Present Continuous describes an action happening now, around the present time, or a temporary situation.

📐 Form

Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are + V-ing

Negative: Subject + am/is/are + not + V-ing

Question: Am/Is/Are + Subject + V-ing?

Examples

I am studying now.
She is cooking.
They are playing football.

Negative

I am not sleeping.
He isn't studying.
They aren't playing.

Questions

Are you studying?
Is she cooking?
Are they playing?

📌 Uses

  • Action happening now.
  • Temporary situations.
  • Changing situations.
  • Future arrangements.
Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week.
I am meeting my teacher tomorrow.
❌ He doesn't studying.
✅ He isn't studying.
Exam Trick: When you see now / at the moment / right now, look for am/is/are + V-ing.

1.3 Present Perfect Tense

📌 Meaning

Present Perfect connects a past action with the present. The exact past time is usually not important.

📐 Form

Affirmative: Subject + have/has + V3

Negative: Subject + have/has + not + V3

Question: Have/Has + Subject + V3?

Examples

I have finished my homework.
She has eaten lunch.
They have gone home.

📌 Uses

  • Past action with a present result.
  • Life experience.
  • Recently completed action.
  • Actions within an unfinished time period.
  • Actions continuing from the past to the present.
I have lost my key.

She has visited Addis Ababa.

I have studied three lessons this week.
Signal words: already, just, yet, ever, never, recently, lately, since, for.

Since vs For

Since = starting point
since Monday, since 2020, since morning

For = duration
for two hours, for three years, for a long time
Do not normally use Present Perfect with a finished, specific past time.

❌ I have seen him yesterday.
✅ I saw him yesterday.
Exam Trick: If you see yesterday, last year, ago, in 2020, Simple Past is normally required.

1.4 Present Perfect Continuous

📌 Meaning

It describes an action that started in the past and has continued until now, especially when we want to emphasize the duration or activity.

📐 Form

Subject + have/has + been + V-ing

Examples

I have been studying for three hours.
She has been working since morning.
Signal words: for, since, all day, all morning, lately, recently.

Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

Present Perfect:
I have read the book.

Focus = completed action/result.

Present Perfect Continuous:
I have been reading the book for two hours.

Focus = duration/process.

2.1 Simple Past Tense

📐 Form

Affirmative: Subject + V2

Negative: Subject + did not + V1

Question: Did + Subject + V1?
I played football yesterday.
She went home last night.
They studied English.
After did/didn't, always use V1.

❌ She didn't went.
✅ She didn't go.
Signal words: yesterday, last week, last year, ago, in 2020, last night.
Exam Trick: Finished past time → Simple Past.

2.2 Past Continuous Tense

📐 Form

Subject + was/were + V-ing
I was studying at 8 p.m.
They were playing football.

Interrupted Action

One action was already happening when another shorter action happened.

I was sleeping when the phone rang.

was sleeping = action in progress
rang = interruption

While

While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.
Exam Trick:
while → often introduces a continuous action.
when → often introduces the shorter interrupting action.

2.3 Past Perfect Tense

📐 Form

Subject + had + V3
I had finished my homework.
She had gone home.
They had eaten dinner.

📌 Use

Use Past Perfect to show that one past action happened before another past action.

When I arrived, she had left.

First → she left.
Second → I arrived.

After he had finished his work, he went home.
Exam Trick: Two past actions + the earlier action → consider Past Perfect.

2.4 Past Perfect Continuous

📐 Form

Subject + had been + V-ing
I had been studying for three hours when he arrived.

The action started before another past event and continued for a period of time.

Common clues: for, since, before, when.

Difference

Past Perfect: I had studied for three hours.
Focus = completed action.

Past Perfect Continuous: I had been studying for three hours.
Focus = duration/process.

3.1 Simple Future Tense

📐 Form

Subject + will + V1
I will study tomorrow.
She will come later.
They will play football.

📌 Uses

  • Predictions.
  • Promises.
  • Offers.
  • Quick decisions.
I think he will win.
I will help you.
The phone is ringing. I will answer it.
Common clues: tomorrow, next week, next year, I think, probably, maybe, I hope.

3.2 Future Continuous Tense

📐 Form

Subject + will be + V-ing
I will be studying at 8 p.m. tomorrow.
She will be working tomorrow morning.

It describes an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

Signal words: this time tomorrow, at 8 tomorrow, at this time next week.

3.3 Future Perfect Tense

📐 Form

Subject + will have + V3
I will have finished my homework by 8 p.m.

She will have arrived by tomorrow.

It describes an action that will be completed before a specific future time.

Signal words: by, by then, by tomorrow, by next year, before.
Exam Trick: By + future time → check Future Perfect.

3.4 Future Perfect Continuous

📐 Form

Subject + will have been + V-ing
By 8 p.m., I will have been studying for three hours.

It emphasizes how long an action will have continued up to a particular point in the future.

Common pattern:
By + future time + for + duration
Exam Trick: If you see by + future time + for + period of time, check Future Perfect Continuous.

🎯 Important Exam Tricks

  • Every day / always / usually → Simple Present
  • Now / at the moment → Present Continuous
  • Since / for / already / just / yet → often Present Perfect
  • Yesterday / last / ago → Simple Past
  • While + action in progress → Past Continuous
  • Earlier past action before another past action → Past Perfect
  • Tomorrow / next week / I think → Simple Future
  • This time tomorrow → Future Continuous
  • By tomorrow / by next year → Future Perfect
  • By + future time + for + duration → Future Perfect Continuous
