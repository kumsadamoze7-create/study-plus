Unit 5 — Major Economic and Cultural Activities in Ethiopia

📖 Detailed Study Plus Notes

📚 Unit Introduction

Economic activities are activities through which people produce, distribute, exchange and consume goods and services.

Ethiopia has a diverse economy based on agriculture, industry, trade, transport, services and other activities. Culture also plays an important role in people's livelihoods, tourism and national identity.

Unit 5 focuses on:
  • Economic activities
  • Agriculture
  • Livestock production
  • Fishing
  • Trade
  • Industry
  • Transport
  • Road safety
  • Cultural activities
  • Tourism
  • Cultural landscapes

5.1 Meaning of Economic Activities

Economic activities are activities involved in the production, distribution, exchange and consumption of goods and services to satisfy human needs and wants.

Major Types of Economic Activities

Sector Main Activities
Primary Agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry and mining
Secondary Manufacturing, processing and construction
Tertiary Trade, transport, banking, tourism, education and health services
🧠 Remember:

Primary → Take from nature
Secondary → Process / manufacture
Tertiary → Provide services

5.2 Agriculture in Ethiopia

Agriculture is the cultivation of crops and the raising of animals for food, raw materials and other economic purposes.

Agriculture is one of the most important economic activities in Ethiopia. It provides food, employment, raw materials and income for many people.

Importance of Agriculture

  • Provides food.
  • Creates employment.
  • Provides raw materials for industries.
  • Generates income.
  • Supports exports.
  • Provides markets for industrial products.
  • Supports rural livelihoods.

Major Agricultural Activities

  • Crop production
  • Livestock production
  • Mixed farming
  • Commercial agriculture
  • Subsistence agriculture

5.3 Subsistence Agriculture

Subsistence agriculture is farming mainly intended to produce food for the farmer and the farmer's household.

Characteristics

  • Small-scale production
  • Limited use of modern technology
  • Production mainly for household consumption
  • Relatively low market orientation
  • Often depends on rainfall
Example:

A household grows cereals and vegetables mainly to feed its family and sells only a small surplus.

5.4 Commercial Agriculture

Commercial agriculture is farming primarily undertaken to produce agricultural goods for sale in local, national or international markets.

Characteristics

  • Market-oriented production
  • Larger-scale production in many cases
  • Greater use of inputs and technology
  • Specialization may occur
  • Production for income and profit

Examples of Commercial Crops

  • Coffee
  • Sesame
  • Tea
  • Flowers
  • Fruits and vegetables
  • Sugarcane

5.5 Crop Production

Crop production depends on physical and human factors such as climate, soil, relief, water, labour, technology and market access.

Factors Affecting Crop Production

  • Rainfall
  • Temperature
  • Soil fertility
  • Relief
  • Availability of water
  • Labour
  • Technology
  • Access to markets
  • Availability of agricultural inputs

Major Food Crops

  • Teff
  • Maize
  • Wheat
  • Sorghum
  • Barley
  • Millet
  • Pulse crops
Important:

Crop distribution varies according to altitude, temperature, rainfall, soil and farming practices.

5.6 Livestock Production

Livestock production involves raising animals for food, income, transport, manure, hides, skins and other purposes.

Major Livestock

  • Cattle
  • Sheep
  • Goats
  • Camels
  • Donkeys
  • Horses
  • Poultry

Importance

  • Provides meat.
  • Provides milk.
  • Provides eggs.
  • Provides hides and skins.
  • Provides draft power in some farming systems.
  • Provides income.
  • Provides manure.

Challenges

  • Disease
  • Shortage of feed
  • Water shortage
  • Drought
  • Limited veterinary services
  • Low productivity in some systems

5.7 Fishing

Fishing is the activity of catching fish and other aquatic organisms for food and economic purposes.

Importance of Fishing

  • Provides food.
  • Creates employment.
  • Generates income.
  • Supports local markets.

Challenges

  • Overfishing
  • Water pollution
  • Habitat destruction
  • Limited fishing technology
  • Climate and environmental changes

5.8 Forestry and Forest Products

Forestry involves the management and use of forest resources.

Forest Products

  • Timber
  • Fuelwood
  • Charcoal
  • Honey
  • Medicinal plants
  • Other non-timber forest products

Importance of Forestry

  • Provides raw materials.
  • Creates employment.
  • Protects soil.
  • Protects watersheds.
  • Supports biodiversity.
  • Supports tourism.

5.9 Mining and Mineral Activities

Mining is the extraction of useful minerals from the Earth.

Economic Importance

  • Provides raw materials.
  • Creates employment.
  • Generates government revenue.
  • Can generate export earnings.
  • Supports industrial development.

Environmental Problems

  • Land degradation
  • Water pollution
  • Loss of vegetation
  • Habitat destruction
  • Waste generation

5.10 Industrial Activities

Industry refers to economic activities involving the processing of raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods.

Types of Industry

  • Food and beverage industries
  • Textile industries
  • Leather industries
  • Cement industries
  • Metal industries
  • Chemical industries
  • Pharmaceutical industries

Importance of Industry

  • Creates employment.
  • Adds value to raw materials.
  • Provides manufactured goods.
  • Supports economic diversification.
  • Generates government revenue.
  • Supports exports.

Industrial Challenges

  • Limited infrastructure
  • Energy shortages
  • Limited access to finance
  • Technology limitations
  • Raw material supply problems
  • Environmental pollution

5.11 Trade

Trade is the buying and selling or exchange of goods and services between people, businesses or countries.

Types of Trade

Domestic Trade

Trade that occurs within the boundaries of a country.

International Trade

Trade between different countries.

Exports

Goods and services sold to other countries.

Imports

Goods and services purchased from other countries.

🧠 Remember:

Export = Out
Import = In

5.12 Transport

Transport is the movement of people and goods from one place to another.

Major Modes

  • Road transport
  • Rail transport
  • Air transport
  • Water transport

Importance of Transport

  • Moves agricultural products to markets.
  • Connects producers and consumers.
  • Supports trade.
  • Facilitates tourism.
  • Connects rural and urban areas.
  • Supports national economic integration.

5.13 Road Safety

Road safety refers to measures and behaviours designed to prevent road crashes, injuries and deaths.

Causes of Road Accidents

  • Speeding
  • Distracted driving
  • Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Fatigue
  • Unsafe overtaking
  • Vehicle defects
  • Poor road conditions
  • Failure to obey traffic rules

Road Safety Measures

  • Obey traffic signs.
  • Wear seat belts.
  • Use helmets on motorcycles.
  • Do not drive while impaired.
  • Do not use a phone while driving.
  • Respect speed limits.
  • Maintain vehicles properly.
  • Use pedestrian crossings where available.
Remember:

Road safety is the responsibility of drivers, passengers, pedestrians and other road users.

5.14 Service Activities

Service activities provide services rather than physical goods.

Examples

  • Education
  • Health services
  • Banking
  • Insurance
  • Communication
  • Transport
  • Tourism
  • Wholesale and retail trade
  • Information technology

Importance

  • Creates employment.
  • Supports other economic sectors.
  • Improves people's quality of life.
  • Generates income.
  • Supports economic growth.

5.15 Culture in Ethiopia

Culture is the shared way of life of a group of people, including beliefs, values, traditions, language, customs, arts and social practices.

Elements of Culture

  • Language
  • Religion
  • Traditional clothing
  • Food
  • Music
  • Dance
  • Art
  • Architecture
  • Festivals
  • Traditional knowledge

Ethiopia has rich cultural diversity resulting from the long history and interaction of different peoples.

5.16 Cultural Landscapes

Cultural landscape is a landscape that has been shaped by the interaction of people and the natural environment over time.

Examples

  • Traditional agricultural landscapes
  • Terraced farming areas
  • Historic towns
  • Traditional villages
  • Religious sites
  • Historic buildings

Cultural landscapes show how human communities adapt to and modify their environments.

5.17 Tourism

Tourism involves travelling to and staying in places outside one's usual environment for purposes such as recreation, business or other activities.

Tourism Resources of Ethiopia

  • Historical sites
  • National parks
  • Wildlife
  • Mountains
  • Lakes
  • Waterfalls
  • Cultural festivals
  • Traditional communities
  • Religious heritage

Importance of Tourism

  • Creates employment.
  • Generates income.
  • Supports local businesses.
  • Can generate foreign exchange.
  • Encourages cultural preservation.
  • Can support conservation.
  • Promotes cultural exchange.

5.18 Cultural Tourism

Cultural tourism focuses on experiencing the culture, heritage, traditions, history and way of life of different communities.

Cultural Tourism Attractions

  • Historical monuments
  • Religious heritage
  • Traditional music
  • Traditional dances
  • Festivals
  • Traditional food
  • Handicrafts
  • Traditional architecture
Why is it important?

Cultural tourism can create income while encouraging communities to preserve their heritage.

5.19 Challenges of Tourism

  • Limited infrastructure in some destinations
  • Environmental degradation
  • Loss or commercialization of cultural heritage
  • Inadequate promotion
  • Accessibility problems
  • Insufficient tourism facilities
  • Security concerns in some areas

Ways to Improve Tourism

  • Improve roads and transport.
  • Develop accommodation facilities.
  • Protect cultural and natural heritage.
  • Promote destinations responsibly.
  • Train tourism workers.
  • Involve local communities.
  • Improve environmental management.

5.20 Sustainable Economic Activities

Economic activities should be carried out in ways that support economic development while protecting natural resources and cultural heritage.

Examples

  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Responsible tourism
  • Efficient resource use
  • Environmental protection
  • Waste reduction
  • Renewable energy use
  • Protection of cultural heritage
🧠 Key Idea

Economy + Environment + Culture = Sustainable Development
