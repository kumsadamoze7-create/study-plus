Unit 7 — Temperature and Thermometry

📖 Complete Study Notes

📘 Unit 7 Introduction

Temperature is a physical quantity that tells us how hot or cold an object is.

Temperature plays an important role in everyday life, weather, cooking, medicine, industry and scientific experiments.

A device used to measure temperature is called a thermometer.

1. What is Temperature?

Temperature is a measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

It is related to the average kinetic energy of the particles making up a substance.

When the average kinetic energy of particles increases, temperature generally increases.

2. Heat and Temperature

Heat and temperature are related, but they are not the same thing.

  • Temperature: indicates how hot or cold an object is.
  • Heat: is energy transferred between bodies because of a temperature difference.
Do not confuse heat with temperature. Temperature is a measure of thermal state, while heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference.

3. Thermal Equilibrium

When two objects at different temperatures are placed in thermal contact, heat flows from the hotter object to the colder object.

The heat transfer continues until the objects reach the same temperature.

At this point they are said to be in thermal equilibrium.

4. Temperature Scales

Different temperature scales are used to measure temperature. The common scales are:

  • Celsius scale
  • Fahrenheit scale
  • Kelvin scale

5. Celsius Scale

The Celsius scale is commonly used in everyday temperature measurements.

The freezing point of water is:

0°C

The boiling point of water at standard atmospheric pressure is:

100°C

6. Fahrenheit Scale

The Fahrenheit scale is another temperature scale.

At standard atmospheric pressure:

  • Freezing point of water = 32°F
  • Boiling point of water = 212°F

7. Kelvin Scale

The Kelvin scale is an important temperature scale used in science.

The SI unit of thermodynamic temperature is the kelvin (K).

The Kelvin scale starts from absolute zero.

0 K = absolute zero

Absolute zero is approximately:

−273.15°C

8. Celsius to Fahrenheit

°F = (9/5 × °C) + 32
Example:

Convert 20°C to Fahrenheit.

°F = (9/5 × 20) + 32
°F = 36 + 32
°F = 68°F

9. Fahrenheit to Celsius

°C = (5/9)(°F − 32)
Example:

Convert 68°F to Celsius.

°C = (5/9)(68 − 32)
°C = (5/9)(36)
°C = 20°C

10. Celsius to Kelvin

K = °C + 273.15
Example:

Convert 25°C to Kelvin.

K = 25 + 273.15
K = 298.15 K

11. Kelvin to Celsius

°C = K − 273.15
Example:

Convert 300 K to Celsius.

°C = 300 − 273.15
°C = 26.85°C

12. Comparison of Temperature Scales

Scale Freezing Point of Water Boiling Point of Water
Celsius 0°C 100°C
Fahrenheit 32°F 212°F
Kelvin 273.15 K 373.15 K

13. Thermometer

A thermometer is an instrument used to measure temperature.

A thermometer works by using a physical property that changes with temperature.

14. Thermometric Property

A thermometric property is a physical property that changes predictably with temperature and can be used to measure temperature.

Examples include:

  • Expansion of a liquid
  • Electrical resistance
  • Pressure of a gas
  • Thermoelectric effect

15. Liquid-in-Glass Thermometer

A liquid-in-glass thermometer uses the expansion of a liquid when its temperature changes.

Common liquids include:

  • Mercury
  • Colored alcohol

As temperature increases, the liquid expands and rises in the narrow tube.

16. Mercury Thermometer

Mercury expands fairly uniformly with temperature and is easy to see in a glass tube.

Mercury is toxic. Modern thermometers often use safer alternatives for many applications.

17. Alcohol Thermometer

Colored alcohol can be used in thermometers because it expands when heated.

Alcohol thermometers are useful for measuring low temperatures because alcohol has a very low freezing point.

18. Digital Thermometer

A digital thermometer uses an electronic sensor to measure temperature and displays the result digitally.

Digital thermometers are widely used because they are convenient and easy to read.

19. Clinical Thermometer

A clinical thermometer is designed to measure human body temperature.

It is commonly used in medical settings and homes.

20. Laboratory Thermometer

A laboratory thermometer is used to measure the temperature of substances during scientific experiments.

It generally has a wider temperature range than a clinical thermometer.

21. Calibration of a Thermometer

Calibration means assigning known temperature values to the scale of a thermometer.

For a Celsius thermometer, the freezing and boiling points of water at standard atmospheric pressure provide important fixed points.

  • Lower fixed point = 0°C
  • Upper fixed point = 100°C

22. Thermal Expansion

Thermal expansion is the increase in size of a substance when its temperature increases.

When a substance is heated, its particles generally vibrate more strongly and their average separation increases.

23. Thermal Contraction

When a substance cools, it generally decreases in size. This is called thermal contraction.

Heating → generally expansion

Cooling → generally contraction

24. Expansion of Solids

Solids expand when heated and contract when cooled.

Expansion can occur in length, area and volume.

25. Linear Expansion

Linear expansion refers to the change in length of a solid due to a change in temperature.

ΔL = αL₀ΔT

Where:

  • ΔL = change in length
  • α = coefficient of linear expansion
  • L₀ = original length
  • ΔT = change in temperature

26. Thermal Expansion Example

Consider a metal rod that becomes slightly longer when heated. The change may be small, but it is important in engineering.

If a metal bridge becomes longer during hot weather, engineers must provide space for expansion.

27. Applications of Thermal Expansion

  • Expansion gaps in bridges
  • Gaps between railway tracks
  • Overhead electrical wires
  • Thermometers
  • Riveting and fitting metal parts
  • Bimetallic strips

28. Expansion Gaps in Bridges

Bridges are exposed to temperature changes. Their materials expand when heated and contract when cooled.

Expansion joints are therefore provided to prevent damage.

29. Railway Tracks

Metal railway tracks expand when temperature increases. Small gaps or expansion arrangements are provided to allow this expansion.

Without enough room for expansion, rails can bend or buckle when they become very hot.

30. Electrical Wires

Overhead electrical wires expand in hot weather and contract in cold weather.

This is why wires may hang lower during hot conditions.

31. Bimetallic Strip

A bimetallic strip consists of two different metals joined together.

Because the two metals expand by different amounts when heated, the strip bends.

Bimetallic strips are used in devices such as thermostats.

32. Thermostat

A thermostat is a device that automatically controls temperature.

A bimetallic strip can be used in a thermostat to open or close an electrical circuit when temperature changes.

33. Importance of Temperature Measurement

  • Medical diagnosis
  • Weather forecasting
  • Food preparation
  • Industrial processes
  • Scientific experiments
  • Refrigeration
  • Heating systems

  • 0 K represents absolute zero.
