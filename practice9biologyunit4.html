🧬 Grade 9 Biology

Unit 4 — Reproduction

📝 60 Multiple Choice Questions

1. What is reproduction?
Reproduction is the biological process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind.
2. Which is a major type of reproduction?
The two major types of reproduction are asexual and sexual reproduction.
3. How many parents are usually required in asexual reproduction?
Asexual reproduction generally involves only one parent.
4. Which process does NOT occur in asexual reproduction?
Asexual reproduction does not involve the fusion of male and female gametes.
5. Which organism commonly reproduces by binary fission?
Amoeba is a unicellular organism that commonly reproduces by binary fission.
6. Binary fission produces how many daughter cells from one parent cell?
In binary fission, one parent cell divides into two daughter cells.
7. Which organism commonly reproduces by budding?
Hydra reproduces asexually by forming a bud that grows on the parent.
8. Budding is best described as:
In budding, a new individual develops from an outgrowth or bud on the parent.
9. Which organism is a common example of budding?
Yeast commonly reproduces asexually by budding.
10. What is fragmentation?
In fragmentation, pieces of the parent can develop into new organisms.
11. Which is an example of an organism that can reproduce by fragmentation?
Some algae can reproduce asexually through fragmentation.
12. Which organisms commonly reproduce through spores?
Many fungi reproduce by producing spores.
13. Vegetative propagation is mainly associated with:
Vegetative propagation is asexual reproduction using plant parts such as stems, roots or leaves.
14. Potato commonly reproduces vegetatively through:
A potato tuber is a modified underground stem that can produce a new plant.
15. Onion commonly reproduces vegetatively through a:
The onion bulb can produce a new plant by vegetative propagation.
16. Strawberry commonly reproduces vegetatively by:
Strawberry plants commonly produce runners that can form new plants.
17. What is parthenogenesis?
Parthenogenesis is development of an organism from an unfertilized egg.
18. Which animal is an example associated with parthenogenesis?
The whiptail lizard Aspidoscelis uniparens is a well-known example of parthenogenesis.
19. In honeybees, an unfertilized egg can develop into a:
In honeybees, unfertilized eggs develop into haploid males called drones.
20. Which is an advantage of asexual reproduction?
Asexual reproduction generally requires only one parent and no mate.
21. A major disadvantage of asexual reproduction is:
Because offspring are generally genetically similar, there is usually less variation in asexual reproduction.
22. Sexual reproduction usually involves:
Sexual reproduction involves gametes and usually their fusion during fertilization.
23. The male gamete in humans is the:
Sperm is the male reproductive cell in humans.
24. The female gamete in humans is the:
The egg is the female gamete in humans.
25. Which organ produces sperm?
The testes produce sperm and the hormone testosterone.
26. Which organ produces eggs?
The ovaries produce eggs and female sex hormones.
27. Which structure is the usual site of fertilization in humans?
Fertilization normally occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct).
28. Where does the embryo normally develop?
The embryo implants in and develops within the lining of the uterus.
29. What is fertilization?
Fertilization is the fusion of sperm and egg.
30. What is the cell formed immediately after fertilization called?
The zygote is formed when the sperm and egg nuclei fuse.
31. Human sperm normally contains how many chromosomes?
Human sperm cells are haploid and normally contain 23 chromosomes.
32. Human egg cells normally contain:
Egg cells are haploid and normally contain 23 chromosomes.
33. What is the chromosome number of a normal human zygote?
23 chromosomes from the sperm plus 23 from the egg gives a diploid zygote with 46 chromosomes.
34. What is ovulation?
Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from an ovary.
35. Which organ is mainly responsible for producing testosterone?
The testes produce sperm and the male sex hormone testosterone.
36. Which gland contributes fluid to semen?
The prostate gland contributes fluid to semen.
37. What is the function of the epididymis?
Sperm mature and are stored in the epididymis.
38. Which structure transports sperm from the epididymis?
The vas deferens transports sperm from the epididymis toward the urethra.
39. Which structure connects the uterus to the vagina?
The cervix is the lower part of the uterus and opens into the vagina.
40. Which structure is the muscular canal connecting the cervix to the outside?
The vagina is a muscular canal extending from the cervix to the outside.
41. What is the main function of the uterus?
The uterus is where the implanted embryo and developing fetus grow.
42. What is the endometrium?
The endometrium is the inner lining of the uterus.
43. What happens if fertilization does not occur?
If pregnancy does not occur, the thickened uterine lining is shed during menstruation.
44. What is the placenta?
The placenta provides an important exchange surface for oxygen, nutrients and wastes between mother and fetus.
45. What connects the fetus to the placenta?
The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta.
46. Which process allows the zygote to develop into an embryo?
After fertilization, the zygote undergoes repeated cell divisions and development to form an embryo.
47. Implantation occurs when:
Implantation is attachment of the developing embryo to the uterine lining.
48. Which of the following is a method of contraception?
Condoms are a barrier contraceptive method.
49. Which contraceptive method can also reduce the risk of many STIs?
Correct and consistent condom use can reduce the risk of many sexually transmitted infections as well as pregnancy.
50. What does STI stand for?
STI means Sexually Transmitted Infection.
51. Which of the following is an STI?
Gonorrhea is a sexually transmitted infection.
52. HIV mainly attacks:
HIV attacks important cells of the immune system.
53. HIV is NOT normally transmitted by:
HIV is not transmitted through ordinary casual contact such as shaking hands.
54. Which type of reproduction generally produces greater genetic variation?
Sexual reproduction combines genetic material from gametes and generally creates greater genetic variation.
55. Which process produces gametes?
Meiosis is the specialized cell division that produces haploid gametes.
56. Why are human gametes haploid?
Each gamete has 23 chromosomes. Fusion of two gametes restores the normal diploid number of 46.
57. Which statement about asexual offspring is generally correct?
Asexual reproduction generally produces offspring genetically very similar to the parent, although mutations can introduce differences.
58. Which sequence is correct?
Sperm and egg fuse during fertilization to form a zygote.
59. Which statement correctly compares sexual and asexual reproduction?
Sexual reproduction involves gametes and fertilization, while asexual reproduction does not involve gamete fusion.
60. What is the main biological importance of reproduction?
Reproduction allows organisms to produce new individuals and helps ensure the continuation of species from one generation to another.
